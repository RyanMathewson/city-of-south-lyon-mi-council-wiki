# Rules of Conduct for Public Comment at Council Meetings $ ^{*} $

Members of the public may speak at a Council meeting upon recognition by the Mayor. Public comment may only occur during periods designated on the agenda for public comment or a public hearing. A person may speak for up to two (2) minutes during each of the two public comment periods on agenda items or non-agenda items. Waivers of the time requirement may only be granted in the discretion of the Mayor, and waivers to speak at a time other than a designated comment period may only be granted by the Council. Any person wishing to make a presentation longer than two minutes or requiring audio-visual equipment is asked to contact the City Clerk requesting to appear on a future agenda.

A person may only address Council from the podium. Only one person may occupy the podium at a time. All remarks are to be directed to the Mayor and Council. Speakers are not to engage in direct dialog with other meeting attendees.

Any person who violates the Rules of Conduct, disturbs the peace at the meeting, and/or interferes with the meeting may be warned, ordered to be seated, removed, and/or ticketed.

## MERS-Presentation

Sue Feinberg of MERS gave a presentation regarding the employee's retirement programs. She discussed the annual evaluation which projects the employer's contribution. She further stated the current funded status is 70%. She explained the primary reason is we had a good market and the return was over 14%. Councilmember Kurtzweil asked if the gains made in a year is 14%, why can't the portion we receive be used to reduce the unfunded liability. Ms. Feinberg stated the gains are still being used in the city's fund, but by using a more conservative rate of return assumption it doesn't impact it as much. Discussion was held regarding the assumptions the actuaries use such as inflation which is 2.5 or 3%. Further discussion was held regarding unfunded liability. The city is expected to be fully funded within 16 years if only paying the minimum amount. They always encourage people to fund more if possible. Ms. Feinberg stated the city is currently contributing $786,588.00 and that will be going down, and next year it will be $50,000.00 less. For projecting for budget purposes, we are looking at the assumption of 7.00% rate of return. Councilmember Kurtzweil asked if she had heard any discussion about the government assisting cities for unfunded liability, because they are doing that now with other communities that are over 60% unfunded liability. Ms. Feinberg stated they have not heard any discussions. Ms. Feinberg explained a graph showing the history of the valuations from 2011-2021. She stated the city made over 2.2 million dollars that went into the plan. She stated the city's contribution was $674,370 while the employee contributions were $49,336. Councilmember Kurtzweil stated it is important to realize the city employees' contributions are much smaller than the city's contribution. She hopes the employees of the city understand the sacrifices being made by the residents of this city to fund this retirement program and she is sure they are. Ms. Feinberg stated the employee contributions are very low compared to other communities.
