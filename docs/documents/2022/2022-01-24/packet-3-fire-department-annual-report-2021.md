#### SOUTH LYON FIRE DEPARTMENT 2021 ANNUAL REPORT

### **MESSAGE FROM THE INTERIM FIRE CHIEF**

It is my pleasure to present our Department Annual Report for the 2021 calendar year.
With the ongoing support from city administration and the citizens that live and visit the
City of South Lyon, we have been able to continue to provide excellent fire and EMS
services in line with our mission statement.

Throughout 2021 the men and women of the South Lyon Fire Department responded to
a record high 1058 incidents. This is a 20% increase from 2020. During the 2021
calendar year, we continued to face challenges associated with the COVID-19
pandemic. These challenges included incident mitigation while keeping staff protected
against COVID-19 exposure and maintaining a healthy, capable, and ready to respond
roster. Despite facing enormous challenges, and cases of COVID-19 among our own
staff, we continued to provide uninterrupted fire and EMS service with an entirely paidon-call staffing model. Without the dedication of the employees in this department along
with the sacrifices felt by their families, we would not be able to continue to serve the
citizens of our community. I am proud to work alongside each of them as they continue
to selflessly give their time and energy to serve as a South Lyon Firefighter. Each
member operates with a “service above self” mentality, and this mentality is a staple of
this department’s success.

Despite the continued struggles with the ongoing pandemic, we were able to make
enhancements to our organization, operation, and administration along with making
progress on capital purchases and proactively planning for the future needs of the
department.

The previous Fire Chief, Robert Vogel, resigned effective October 1, 2021. I have been
filling his role until a replacement is hired. It is an honor to serve in this capacity and I
look forward to working alongside the next Fire Chief to lead this department towards
future progress.

Respectfully Submitted

### **TRAINING**

In 2021 firefighters completed 3135.75 hours of training. These hours
included formalized certification and licensing courses like the fire academy
and Emergency Medical Technician as well as informal weekly in-house
trainings that focus on continuing education and specialized skill
development. We focus on honing employee firefighting skills and aim to
improve the lesser encountered activities such as vehicle extrication as well
as continuing education credits as required to maintain their State of
Michigan EMS licenses. Additionally, each new employee completes a
rigorous On-The-Job Training (OJT) program which acclimates the
employee with city and departmental policies but delves into firefighting and
emergency medical services as provided by this department. We have
found that this OJT program sets our new hires up for improved success in
the formal training each must complete. SLFD also utilizes an online
training resource “Fire Rescue 1”. This platform allows staff to meet specific
training needs through a computer based and online platform which helps
to remove some of the burden from an already demanding profession as
well as standardizing the training that each member receives.

### **GRANTS/DONATIONS**

**Firefighter Turnout Gear Grant**
SLFD was awarded a grant for the purchase of 10 sets of firefighter turnout
gear in the amount of approximately $43,000 from a muti-jurisdictional
grant with WAMAA (Washtenaw Area Mutual Aid Association) of which
SLFD is a member.

**AFG Extrication Grant**
SLFD took delivery of new vehicle extrication equipment on April 20 [th] in the
amount of $23,042.73. This grant was secured through our membership
with WAMAA.

**Lowes**
Captain Armstrong secured a $1000 donation of building materials to be
used inside the training building located at the Waste Water Treatment
Plant.

**Training Facility / Burn Building**
Work continued on the training facility received through grant monies
awarded in 2020. The building had windows and doors added along with
the two separate containers being welded together to create a single
structure.

**SCBA**
SLFD was awarded a grant for the purchase of SCBA in a multijurisdictional grant application with area departments. Commerce Fire
Department was the lead organization. SLFD was awarded $182,000 with
a 10% match for the purchase of the MSA G1 SCBA (Self-Contained
Breathing Apparatus).

**Firehouse Subs Grant**
SLFD was awarded a grant for the purchase of 8 new sets of firefighter
turnout gear. The total value awarded was $23,703.68.

**Food Donations**
SLFD received numerous donations throughout the year from citizens and
business in the South Lyon area. Firefighters are forever grateful for the
many donations of food and treats.

### **FIREFIGHTER OF THE YEAR**

Firefighter Tim McGillen was selected as the 2021 Firefighter of the Year.
Tim has served with the South Lyon Fire Department since January of
1998. Previously working as a paid-on-call firefighter in Utica, Tim is a
wealth of knowledge and history when it comes to SLFD and has worked
under 3 different chiefs in his time here. Tim holds the position of Aerial
Platform Operator (APO) and can often be found telling jokes in the
apparatus bay. Tim was selected for this recognition because of his
reliability, run percentage, fantastic attitude, and love for the job. Tim
frequently covers public education and community engagement events,
promotes SLFD and fire safety to the public. Tim has recently volunteered
to work with the new SCBA in-service and instruction.

### **SLFD IN THE COMMUNITY**

### **FIRE PREVENTION**

The South Lyon Fire Department Fire Prevention Bureau is staffed by 1 part time Fire
Inspector with the Fire Chief holding the position of Fire Marshal. Inspector Moynihan
conducts annual fire prevention and safety inspections on all commercial buildings
within the city. Additionally, as requested by a city resident, Inspector Moynihan will
conduct a fire safety check of private residences. In 2021 SLFD had no structural fires
in commercial occupancies. This is attributed to Inspector Moynihan’s proactive
approach to partnering with businesses located within the city to maintain and improve
fire safety. Our goal for each inspection is for voluntary compliance and to create a
partnership as opposed to relying on punitive enforcement for the safety of the public.
The fire prevention bureau issued no citations to businesses in 2021. This is a
testament to the relationship that has been cultivated with our businesses.
