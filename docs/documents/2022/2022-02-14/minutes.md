### 5. Unsolicited written materials

City Manager Zelenak explained that it was brought to his attention by a Councilmember that they felt it would be important to have an ordinance on the books relating to the receiving of unsolicited written materials, specifically to regulate the delivery of those materials. Councilmember Kivell stated if it is a chronic circumstance, it would be nice for us to have a legal way to address this issue. He can't help but think the publishers of these things aren't getting jammed up in court because of this kind of thing. Maybe they would be more mindful of who is delivering and whether they are doing it in a way a city would like it. It would be more responsible to place them in mail boxes or some other thing rather than tossing it out in front of a house. City Attorney Hamameh stated she was asked by the City Manager to look into if her firm has ever addressed this issue. She further stated her law firm drafted some ordinances for some communities and she has provided those copies. She stated there was litigation against Orion Township in the past regarding this. City Manager Zelenak stated he has spoken with the Orion Township Supervisor and it actually went to Federal Court. It had to do with the violation of first amendment rights, not only freedom of speech, but freedom of the press. He stated they ended up settling. Their first avenue was looking at the delivery and just considering it litter, but the courts looked at what litter is. By definition, a newspaper being delivered is not litter. He further stated a paper that is delivered isn't being discarded, it is delivered with the intent for someone to read it. In Orion Township, they went through several years in the court, and each township board member, along with the township was facing a law suit against them for 5 million dollar each because of the first amendment rights of the free press. This has also occurred in Alabama, New Jersey, New York, and many other states. Some of the decisions are based upon supreme court cases. In Wyoming, in 1994 the supreme court voted to strike down the restrictions on distribution of papers being delivered. He stated we looked into this more, the final results to settle, the residents were provided with a contact number for the individuals that were actually handling the delivery of those items and the residents were able to contact them to tell them they don't want it delivered. Other jurisdictions are handling it the same way. He stated another concern is who enforces the ordinance, and against who. The person delivering the paper, or the company hiring the people to deliver, or the paper. There is a lot to consider. Councilmember Kurtzweil questioned some of the language in the sample ordinances. She then stated when it comes to where things can be delivered, people aren't always comfortable walking up to someone's door, which may be why they are delivered on the easement or driveway. Homeowners have dogs, there could be things where people could trip and fall. She further questioned how someone would attach something securely to a door, or between the exterior front doors if unlocked, which she would not advise anyone to do that. She stated the sample ordinances seem very restrictive. She then stated when she looked into this, anything with a first amendment message, you will have a hard time defending the ordinance. Hello everyone, I am Larry Ledbetter 11343 Clovis Pointe South Lyon. I am the President of the S.L. Historical Commission.

In 1975 The Depot was spared from demolition by Mayor John Noel and Policeman Don Fletcher and others. It was moved and restored in 1976 through the efforts of Mayor Andy Rajkovich, clerk Gail Smolarz, The City Council and I am sure many other civic leaders.

In 1981 the first Depot Days weekend celebration included a parade and a big community event. This year marks the 41 $ ^{st} $ Depot Day. We are planning now to have day of family fun.

Model trains, butter making, bubbles, pony rides, touch a truck fire engine, police cars, front loader tractors, hula hoops, sack races, music, bee keeper, balloons, and of course tours of the village. All this is planned for September 10, 2022 from 10:00 AM to 4:00 PM.

All year we try to provide interesting programs to the community such as: Native American History,

Railroads, Route 66, Tube Mill Co. World War II, and others. Our Mission is to preserve and make

available the history and artifacts in the museum. My biggest thrill is the sharing with young people

about the days when there was no television, no refrigerator in your home, listening to the Lone Ranger

on the radio, playing kick the can in the alley, telephone booths, no cell phones, no internet, no video

games: what did we do????

We have a dream about adding a barn and a house to the Village. We have been talking about it for years. Linda Ross (Historical Society President) made a list from some of our Commission minutes when we recorded our thought about a barn and house. A barn would allow us to display the relics of when South Lyon was a farming community. At one time S.L. was called “The Horse Capital of Michigan”.

Paul Zelenak encouraged us to put a plan together, and we started the barn committee two years ago. We have visited several barns and houses, interviewed restorers, asked for quotes and marked off our site preference. Bob Polasky has put together a very good presentation of the project. He will soon be asking to make that presentation to City Council. The barn is just South of Eight Mile West of Pontiac Trail, The Roper family home.

Our hope is to start the project this year and proceed with the restoration to have a dedication in 2023. A significant date indeed, the 150th year of the Village of South Lyon, The Sesquicentennial. Pete Niedzielski had a zoom call with Erica Wilson, Paul Zelenak and Mark Russell during which he asked for a modification to the planned use of the East portion of the Village for parking. The driveway runs right through our house. We hope that moving the driveway will allow all to succeed. We have been very fortunate that our closest neighbor The First United Methodist Church has allowed us to use their baseball field to park for special events much the same way that U of M Big House uses Pioneer High School and The Golf course for games.

There is a TV show on PBS called Under the Radar Michigan. Tom Daldin is a fast talking jovial host who visits all things Michigan. I have invited him to The Historic Village for Depot Day, to tour the Tube Mill, the S.L. Hotel, the Christmas House, the Corner Social, Luca's Coney, Lake Street Tavern, Grand Trunk, Pumpkinfest, Memorial Day, Diane's Doll House etc. so many interesting places, I hope he accepts.

Thank You for your time.

Larry Ledbetter, Feb 14, 2022
