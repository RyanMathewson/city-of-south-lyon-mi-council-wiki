## NEW BUSINESS

1. Replacement of loadbreak and meter bay at wastewater treatment plant

Water and Wastewater Superintendent Beason explained they are requesting the purchase and installation of a new primary (13,200) loadbreak (primary switch) and meter bay. The current one malfunctioned during a recent preventative maintenance inspection and has been flagged by DTE. Utilities Instrumentation Service (UIS) has inspected the loadbreak and determined it cannot be repaired due to its age and condition. This equipment is critical and is required to isolate the plant from DTE in emergencies. He stated he has received 2 bids and is recommending the purchase and installation of a new primary loadbreak from DoubleJack Electric Company in the amount of $36,500.00 Councilmember Hansen asked what is its lifetime. Mr. Beason stated this is the original one so approximate 40 years, and the new one will only have 1 year warranty but they are very reliable. Councilmember Kurtzweil asked about the double load. Mr. Beason stated it is a disconnect to de-energize the water plant, instead of having to contact DTE. He then stated this is for the portion DTE takes care of. They have to have a way so they can read our usage. He further stated we will be working with DTE on this. We'll have 3 crews, an overhead crew to disconnect overhead wire, underground crew, and the meter crew. He stated he is expecting the end of March to early April. Councilmember Bogert asked the nature of the mal-function. Mr. Beason stated it would not close properly, this switch needs to close instantly, and it wouldn't so they walked away from it. He further stated we were doing preventative maintenance when it happened. Councilmember Bogert asked if it was hard to mitigate it not closing. Mr. Beason stated it is mechanical, they aren't allowed to touch it because its 13,000 volts. It is supposed to disengage immediately. He further stated the switchgear is on, but we can't disconnect, we would have to call DTE. Councilmember Kivell asked if he is confident DTE will be able to meet our schedules. Mr. Beason stated the electrician we are using has worked with DTE on something similar, they come highly recommended from HRC and they know the schedule so he is confident these are the people we want to work with. Councilmember Bogert asked how the system will be energized during the changeout. Mr. Beason stated it will be on a generator, and there may be another cost because he would like to purchase another generator to be a standby generator. He further stated he will have one on site, and it will be a long day, but they think they can get it done in one day.

3. Purchase of new 750kw standby commercial generator

Water and Wastewater Superintendent Beason stated they are requesting the purchase of a new 750kw diesel generator located at the wastewater treatment plant. It will replace the current 1980 620kw generator. The current generator is critical to the operations of the wastewater treatment plant during power outages and has been experiencing some mechanical issues in recent years. HRC along with department staff have determined that replacing the current generator is essential to maintain a reliable standby power source. This is a scheduled purchase for this year's budget as a capital improvement. We received 3 bids and are recommending the purchase of the generator from Cummins in the amount of $245,050. Councilmember Bogert stated in the proposal he didn't see anything about removing the existing generator. Mr. Beason stated they will keep the existing generator and the new one will be in a trailer outside. He stated we will use both of them by alternating them. We could get another 6 months or a year, but he knows there are mechanical issues and there are things we cannot buy anymore for it. We are having more and more issues with it. Councilmember Bogert asked if the purchase is turnkey. Mr. Beason stated it is not, there will be more purchases for design, running the lines and things like that. Councilmember Kivell asked if he had any idea what the scrap value would be on the old generator. Mr. Beason stated it might be worth something, but taking out of the building would be an issue. We would have to either remove a wall, or remove the roof to remove it. Councilmember Kivell stated he is worried about how much money we will spend to try to keep it running. Mr. Beason stated we will not maintain it anymore. It would move to the newer one. Councilmember Kivell stated he doesn't know why we would give up the space for something that isn't working anymore. Mr. Beason stated it is a tight space, we couldn't put the new generator in that space, it isn't feasible, so it will be outside.

5. Fiscal year 2022 State and Local Cybersecurity grant program (SLCGP) local consent agreement

Finance and Benefit Administrator Tiernan stated the SLCGP is administered by the Department of Homeland Security and funded by the Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act. The State of Michigan was awarded $4.7 million for the federal fiscal year 2022. The SLCGP is a reimbursable pass-through grant program with an overall goal to improve the cybersecurity of state, local and territorial governments by providing assistance for managing and reducing systemic cyber risk through the following programs: Endpoint Detection and Response Software licenses, Cybersecurity assessments, and incident response planning and training activities. ## Office of the Mayor

## PROCLAMATION

WHEREAS, 2023 marks a milestone for the South Lyon First United Methodist Church celebrating their 190 $ ^{th} $ year in the City of South Lyon; and

WHEREAS, the South Lyon First United Methodist Church has been a welcoming and affirming congregation since its humble beginning in 1833 when 20 people first met to hear a sermon from a Methodist Episcopal minister; and

WHEREAS, the congregation has grown over the years from its modest beginnings to almost 650 members; and

WHEREAS, the South Lyon First United Methodist Church has been and continues to be an important, caring, and supportive part of the community contributing to the spiritual and physical health of its membership.

NOW THEREFORE, I, Stephen B. Kennedy, Mayor of the City of South Lyon on behalf of the City Council and the entire community offer congratulations to the South Lyon First United Methodist Church and their membership on providing 190 years of service and leadership since its founding.
