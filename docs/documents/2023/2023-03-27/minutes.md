### 1. 2023-2024 City Budget

City Manager Zelenak stated Council was presented with the draft 2023-2024 budget this evening and this budget was prepared and based upon City Council recommendations, staff input and review of needs of departments and the community. The preliminary numbers are reflective of expected revenues based upon taxable values provided by Oakland County Equalization and proposed millages. He then reminded Council the budget work session will be Wednesday April 5th at 6pm. Councilmember Kurtzweil asked if the bonds have gone to sale yet. Finance and Benefit Administrator Tiernan explained it was completed in February and the funding is sitting in the bank account we set up specifically for that. She stated we have utilized some expenditures for the bond sale, the finance consultant, our bond lawyers, and some fees we had to pay to process the paperwork. She further stated Huntington Securities purchased the bonds, and she just received the schedule showing the payment and interest, which will be provided at the budget meeting on the 5th. Councilmember Kurtzweil asked the total revenue and what the interest rate was. Ms. Tiernan stated we received 8.8 million and she doesn't recall the interest rate, but will give Council that information at the budget meeting. Councilmember Kurtzweil stated she was looking at the City Charter in regards to the banking issue that occurred a week ago. She was wondering if the language would allow us to use a more financially backed bank, and not just the local banks. She stated we may need to look at the Charter to be able to move money if there is trouble. She stated if you follow the trends in banking, and you wonder if some of the local banks will be in business in 5-8 years. Some are going to virtual banking only. It isn't an emergency right now, but we have to watch the trend. Ms. Tiernan stated she and Clerk Deaton have been working on looking at all of our banking options even before the banking crisis hit the news. We are looking at more diversification with our accounts. Council did approve a new investment policy a few years ago, and we are looking at Oakland County's Investment Program. She further stated we are trying to stay ahead of the game.

### 2. Opioid Settlement Resolution

After several years of negotiation, nationwide settlements relating to the distribution and sale of opioids, have been reached against two national pharmacies, CVS and Walmart, and against two pharmaceutical companies, Teva and AbbVie/Allergan. The two settlements are in addition to the settlements previously approved in 2021 with McKesson, Cardinal Health and AmerisourceBergen and Jansen Pharmaceuticals, INC and its parent company Johnson and Johnson. The settlements require companies to pay up to $16.5 billion dollars over 13 years. The city is eligible to participate with the settlements. Councilmember Dilg asked if there is any reason to not join. City Manager Zelenak stated we have discussed this and all the municipalities have not found a reason to not join. We just have to wait to see how it must be spent.

## MANAGER'S REPORT

City Manager Zelenak stated staff has been working on a more efficient way to handle collecting brush discarded at the curb by residents. He stated this will allow the crew to pick up debris in specific areas at specific times during the month, excluding of course, severe weather-related events. He then reminded Council of the budget meeting on the 5th of April at 6pm. He stated the bids for the Hagadorn Road/Sanitary Sewer project will be opened on March 29th, and the bids for the railway project will be opened on March 30th. The Oakland County administrators in the SW part of the county continue to meet to discuss creating a consortium for the purpose of providing and maintaining safe transportation for our residents and members of the consortium. We have a preliminary agreement for joining the consortium and it will be discussed at a future council meeting. He then stated we continue to review opportunities for the city's 150th anniversary. We have received 150th logo stickers that will be placed on various buildings and city vehicles. The logo will also be on all city letterhead and possibly the city banner poles. He then stated we continue to submit grants for funds through Representative Slotkin's office for Fire Department equipment and for Lafayette Park. We have been informed that our submittals have passed the first approval. We are now submitting follow up material as part of the review process, and will await the final approval of the committee to see if we receive the funds. City Manager Zelenak stated the city has received notice that there is $600,000 worth of street lighting improvements that will be done in the year 2025. The locations will be at Pontiac Trail, 10 Mile, Reynold Sweet and McHattie. He further stated that will be coordinated with the road plan for the following year. He then stated Councilmember Kennedy helped with us getting the updates through SEMCOG. Councilmember Kurtzweil asked about the rebuilding of the bridge at Oak Creek. City Manager Zelenak stated that went out for review for our engineers, as well as the permits from the State of Michigan. It was discussed at a prior meeting we would pay for that with ARPA funds this year and it will be in the upcoming budget, as well as the wooden bridge. Councilmember Kurtzweil asked why are we waiting for the ARPA funds while we have money in the budget that could be used now. City Manager Zelenak stated we already have the ARPA funds, but we are waiting for the design and the permits from the State. Councilmember Kurtzweil stated Redford Township is putting in a 19 million dollar health and wellness center and it was interesting how they got the funding together and a lot of it came from ARPA funds.

He then recognized Deputy Chief, Mike Weir. Mike was with the South Lyon Fire Department for almost twenty years. He began his career as a firefighter and rose through the ranks to serve as the Fire Inspector, Sergeant, Lieutenant, Captain, Deputy Chief and also served as the Interim Fire Chief. Throughout his career Mike received numerous lifesaving awards and letters of recognition including ten special recognition awards and unit citations. He was instrumental in writing and securing numerous grants for the department during his years of service and he managed several projects for the department as well. He stated he also instituted the smoke alarm and carbon monoxide alarm program in which the SLFD provides and installs the units free of charge for City residents. He managed taking over the City hydrants for inspection, testing and maintenance; and he served on the design committees for Engine 7-2 and Engine 7-1.
