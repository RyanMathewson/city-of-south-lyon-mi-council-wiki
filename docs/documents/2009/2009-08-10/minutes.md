### 1. 2009 Street Improvement Project Bid Option

City Manager Murphy stated that at the last meeting Council approved the bid for the 2009 Street Improvements. However, there was an option for 6,220 yards of TruPave paving mat in the amount of $20,712.60, and there was question as to why we should approve that option.

Mr. Jesse VanDeCreek of HRC stated that he has provided some documentation regarding a new maintenance technology that they feel should be considered when rehabilitating the streets. When reevaluating the maintenance program it was observed emanation of the cracks in the joints in the new paving material. The majority has not cracked, however on a wet day you can observe that there is a large "checkerboard pattern" to it which is the material moving along with the concrete slab underneath. These are maintenance projects and all they are doing is milling the top two inches, joint repair of the old concrete slabs and re-capping the road. These roads are placed on black base. Most of the roads we are dealing with are 20-30 years old and they did go in with an 8 inch sub-base aggregate that would allow the water to drain away. When we mill the roads, even when we do the joint repairs, you do get a degree of settlement. They brought this to the attention of administration and what they did was design this with a TruPave engineering mat to help mitigate the promulgation of those cracks and help redistribute the load due to frost heave if and when that happens. Due to the cost of the project, they did decide to bid this as an alternate. The final bid price did come in below the original estimate and it does include the addition of the True Pave paving mat. This is about a 7% increase in project cost to use this material. These are maintenance projects that are supposed to last 5-10 years. By adding the 7% increase we can add approximately 50% to the life project and save a significant amount of money. This is a design tool that they feel should be implemented where they see the concrete base is put on a black base and there is no way to get rid of the drainage without excavating the whole road. It is another way to make sure that the maintenance life is extended as much as possible. It is new for South Lyon, but it is not new for southeast Michigan or in the region where you have frost condition. He explained what the material looks like. He stated that it comes in rolls and it is laid out in a grid pattern to help distribute the reflective cracking sideways rather than just vertically. They would recommend that it become a standard when we do mill and overlay on concrete and possibly even on asphalt depending on the condition of the base.

Councilman Morelli stated that he understands based on his driveway that depending on how much it rains in the fall depends on how much it heaves in the winter when it freezes. He asked that if this product is guaranteed to work and do we have any information that says that it will extend the life of the road 50% longer. Mr. VanDeCreek stated that what they have seen is laboratory testing that is similar to vehicular and weather conditions when you add this type of material. TruPave is just one name, but is not a sole source product. Test results have shown that it does produce a longer lasting product. It appears to extend the life of maintenance projects significantly. It is difficult to say that is going to last an additional 2-3 years because it is a maintenance project and what we are trying to do is to make sure that what we put down is going to last as long as possible.

Discussion was held on the maintenance projects. Mr. VanDeCreek stated that this is our third year in what we consider a maintenance program with mill and overlay whether it be on existing concrete slab or existing asphalt. We are talking about doing several roads for just a few hundred thousand dollars. These roads have served their purpose and now we are investing a fraction of the cost that it would be to build them up as new. In five to ten years more than likely we will not have that mill and overlay option. Councilman Morelli stated that he would have like to have had something included in his packet that he could have read about the product, who has done it and how it is working. Mr. VanDeCreek stated that he could provide some additional information. He stated that this type of product does have a market that is fairly vast.

Council Member Maida asked if this is a new product. Mr. VanDeCreek stated that it is new for South Lyon. Council Member Maida too asked for additional information on the product. Councilman Wedell also agreed that he would like additional information on the product and an idea about the return on the investment.

### 4. Michigan Suburbs Alliance

Superintendent Renwick stated that at the last meeting Council was given some information on the Michigan Suburbs Alliance program. We have Conan Smith present to answer any questions Council may have.

Mr. Conan Smith, Executive Director of the Michigan Suburbs Alliance stated that the Alliance is the fudiciary agent for the coalition of government oriented non-profits which is working on a project called the Southeast Michigan Regional Energy Office. The Suburbs Alliance is a coalition of 31 southeast Michigan cities and is partnered with the Michigan Municipal League, SEMCOG and Warren Training Center, which is a non-profit based in the City of Detroit focused on energy efficiency activities. They put together a coalition to build a regional Energy Office to help municipalities do energy efficiency and renewable energy work in anticipation of some of the changes coming from the Federal level and pressure across the region to focus on energy. They are now the only metropolitan area in the country that is looking at doing a regional Energy Office. They are hoping to bring in a lot of dollars from the Energy Efficiency and Conservation Block Grant Program. They have other partners in the project as well including DTE Energy, US Green Building Council, etc. Their focus is to create an intergovernmental cooperative agreement with municipalities throughout the region with the idea of bringing in Federal dollars into our region to do municipal based energy projects including updating City Halls, etc. and ultimately saving dollars. The Block Grant program is providing hundreds of millions of dollars to the State of Michigan and $400 million federal dollars that will be available competively. For communities such as South Lyon, they are not considered an entitlement community so they have to compete for the dollars available in this State. South Lyon would be eligible for $50,000 in direct allocation and another $250,000 for LED light projects. Both the Federal and the State are prioritized cooperative applications so you will have a better chance if you do a regional project, and the Southeast Energy Office gives an opportunity to do that. In the partnership agreement, there is a financing mechanism where they re-capture the portion of the grant that they would get for the City. They will do the fundraising, write the grant, evaluate the projects, and find partners so that you can put forth the most competitive proposal to the State and Federal. Any grants received, they ask that the City re-pay that grant back into the Energy Office, which will be structured as a 501-3C non-profit. Those funds would then be put into an endowment program so that they can continue to support municipal energy work without charging membership fees.

Councilman Morelli asked what the cost is to the City. Mr. Smith stated that there would be nothing up front. There is a payback mechanism whereby they would re-capture the grant if they win one for the City through the City's energy savings. As the City is saving dollars, they would re-pay the value of that grant into the Energy Office endowment fund. This would be broken out based on a simple payback analysis. For example if the $50,000 grant ends up being a $10,000 per year energy savings, the payback period would be five years.

Councilman Morelli stated that approximately a year ago, there was an organization that the City Manager recommended that we not participate with and asked if it was this group. City Manager Murphy stated that it was not this group, but rather a group dealing with right-of-ways.

Councilman Morelli questioned the insurance aspect. He stated that the agreement indicates that each community is to provide insurance. In his experience, they had purchased some equipment and let different stores use it and they had their own insurance. In this case if a community damages the equipment, it is covered by insurance but does the insurance policy cover rental equipment until the equipment is fixed to cover the events that other communities have scheduled. He further voiced his concern about if the equipment does not work, who is responsible, the community that was in possession of it or the community that just picked it up. City Manager Murphy stated that is a great question, but it is not something they thought about. He stated that they would have a checklist for when the equipment is picked up. He further stated that he will discuss the issue of replacement equipment and insurance with the other communities.

Councilman Wedell asked if it would be possible for one community to insure it as a rider on their insurance. Attorney Lee stated that typically a policy would not cover equipment if it is taken outside the City limits or not under the control of the insuring party. Discussion was held on the cost of an insurance rider being very minimal.
