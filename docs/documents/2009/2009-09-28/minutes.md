### 1. Beer Tent

Mr. Cory Bala of the South Lyon Hotel stated that they are applying for a beer tent in connection with the Pumpkinfest. It will be the Chamber of Commerce beer tent and he has offered his assistance with the event and will be selling the beer to the Chamber through one of his vendors. He presented a diagram of the beer tent area. The Chamber has applied for the license from the Liquor Control Commission and have gotten the liability insurance as well. He has also taken the liberty to take out an additional $500,000 insurance policy.

Councilman Morelli asked how they plan to stop people from carrying out the beer from the bar area. Mr. Bala stated that there will be only one opening at the southern end and there will be 4 foot snow fencing around the entire area. There will be several people from his establishment as well as several volunteers on that entrance. There will be food served outside the beer tent. There will be 6 feet between the two snow

Council Member Kopkowski stated that in the past some of the youth communities, churches and neighboring organizations have not been in favor of such an event and asked if contact had been made with any of these. Mr. Gary Childs stated that he spoke with the President of Active Faith and he is 100% behind them, but they are the only organization he has talked with. However, he has not received any negative feedback.

Councilman Kivell asked if there would be any alcohol awareness training for servers. Mr. Childs stated that he and his wife are both trained by the State. Mr. Bala stated that his staff has also taken the same course and are fully trained.

Councilman Kivell stated that he had heard that we are only one of two communities in the State of Michigan that do not have a beer tent in one of their festivals. It would seem that these other communities must see something of value. Mr. Bala stated that it is their hope that it will be a good thing for everyone. It is their main goal to make it a good event and not have problems and there is always room for growth and improvement and he would hope that they could lay the ground work for future events.

### 2. Temporary suspension of enforcement of noise ordinance

City Manager Murphy stated that the request is to suspend enforcement of the noise ordinance until 11:00 p.m. for the band to play until that time.

Attorney Lee stated that the request is to extend, for this limited engagement, the ability to allow the band to play until 11:00 p.m. where our current ordinance would require them to stop at 10:00 p.m. The resolution simply suggests that it would be for the one-hour period only.

Councilman Wedell asked if the resolution has the area well enough defined. Attorney Lee stated that it is referring to the festival, which is limited to the DDA. Therefore, it could not be interpreted to go outside the DDA area.

## CM 9-4-09 – SUSPENSION OF ENFORCEMENT OF NOISE ORDINANCE

## Motion by Kivell, supported by Wedell

WHEREAS, the South Lyon Downtown Development Authority has applied for a special permit from the City of South Lyon to allow a band to play at the Pumpkin Festival; and

WHEREAS, South Lyon City Code 42-62(9) specifically addresses the playing of musical instruments and states as follows:

Each of the following acts is declared unlawful and prohibited, but this enumeration shall not be deemed to be exclusive, namely:

(9) Radio, television and musical instruments. The playing of any radio, television set, phonograph, or any musical instrument in such a manner or with such volume, particularly during the hours between 10:00 p.m. and 7:00 a.m. or at any time or place so as to annoy or disturb the quiet, comfort, or repose of persons in any office or in any dwelling, hotel, or other type of residence, or of any persons in the vicinity; and

WHEREAS, South Lyon Downtown Development Authority has requested that the band be permitted to play until 11:00 pm; and

WHEREAS, the Pumpkin festival is a Celebration of the City of South Lyon for its citizens, and the event attracts other visitors to the area, enhances its public image, promotes economic development and fosters a sense of civic pride among its citizens; and

WHEREAS, the City Council of the City of South Lyon considers this request to be in the best interests of the City of South Lyon and in the interests of the citizens' safety, health and welfare for the above-stated reasons;

NOW THEREFORE, the City Council for the City of South Lyon does hereby grant permission to Downtown Development Authority to allow the band Global Village to play until 11:00 pm on Saturday, October 3, 2009 in the areas specifically requested by the Downtown Development Authority.

### 1. 2008/2009 Audit

<a id="p4"></a>
*[Page 4 of the original](https://www.southlyonmi.org/document_center/City%20Council/Agenda%20packet%20and%20Minutes/2009/minutes/092809minutes.pdf?t=202108310733010#page=4)* (read by OCR; check the original)

Mr. Brian Camiller, Audit Manager and Alicia Davis who over saw the daily audit operations were present represent from Plante Moran. Mr. Camiller stated that Les Pulver, Audit Partner sends his regards, but he was unable to attend the meeting. Mr. Mike Swartz also Audit Partner is present if there are any questions. He further stated that there are three documents, the Financial Report with Supplemental Information which is the financial statement, they have a graph package covering the highlights of the audit and the Opinion Letter, which is the only part of the financial statement that is Plante Moran's. The rest of it is the City of South Lyon's Financial Statement. The Opinion Letter is the "report card". They believe that the financial statement is complete and accurate and follow generally accepted accounting principals. They have again given an Unqualified Opinion or an "A" on the report card. This is not an automatic; not everyone gets an Unqualified Opinion. The City has to work very hard to prepare an accurate Financial Statement and follow the rules. They also provide a document referred to as The Report to the Council.

Ms. Alicia Davis of Plante Moran provided and discussed a set of graphs showing Governmental Activities Revenue Trend from 2006 through 2009, Governmental Activities Expense Trend from 2006 through 2009 as well as General Fund Revenue and Expenditure Trend from 2005 through 2009. She discussed the Comparison of General Fund – Fund Balance including designated and undesignated funds. She discussed the Water and Sewer Fund Activity including Working Capital, Operating Income before depreciation and Restricted Assets. She further discussed the Property Tax Composition showing the breakdown for the various jurisdictions that the City collects for including both homestead and non-homestead millage rates. Finally, she covered the Statistical Information including revenue and other receipts for all funds.

Mr. Camiller stated that regarding the Water and Sewer Fund Activity, the balance sheet versus the liabilities shows that the balance that the City has is still very strong and has been. The reason they suggested an increase in rates based on the study that they did was because it was starting to slide. We want to maintain that balance sheet.

Councilman Kivell discussed the Major and Local Street funds and asked if that is why we projected a $300,000 use of Fund Balance and only ended up using $30,000. Mr. Camiller stated that is part of it.

Mr. Camiller discussed the Report to City Council. He stated that what this is a collection of several documents. The first part is letters required as part of their audit standards. It has specific language that they are required to use. The first letter is a report on internal controls. Although they have issued an opinion on the internal controls, they are required to point out when they see a problem. The comments that they made are very mild though some of the terminology seems harsh either "significant deficiency" or "material weakness". The significant deficiency pointed out on page 2 referring to Auditors assistance was required in order for the financial statements to be in conformity with generally accepted accounting principals in no way is a judgment of the quality of your bookkeeping. The City does an excellent job in keeping complete and accurate records. The adjustments they suggested during the audit are simply year-end adjustments. This is not day to day accounting, but a paper matter to make sure that the financial statements are complete. The other item pointed out here is the GASB 45, which is a new accounting standard relating to other postemployment benefits liability. They assisted with that calculation rather than the City hiring an actuarial.

Mr. Camiller discussed the final results of the audit. He stated that at the beginning of the audit they had a conversation with the Mayor and explained their process. It is also an opportunity for the Mayor to express any concerns or anything they should look into initially. They sent a letter out to the entire Council explaining that conversation and now we are closing the circle. The question is were they able to do the audit that they originally planned and the answer is yes. Had they had disagreement with management, or problems conducting the audit or if the city workers were not forthcoming with information, this is the letter they would explain that. As you can see from reading this letter, there are none of those issues. The audit is very clean and they appreciate the efforts that the City goes through in order to make it that way. The next document is called "Other Comments and Recommendations". These are City specific. These comments are not a requirement, but things they think are important and the City should know. He discussed the $30,000 decrease in fund balance as opposed to the planned decrease of approximately $291,000. As far as segregation of duties and management oversight, the City does not have a ten person finance department.

<a id="p5"></a>
*[Page 5 of the original](https://www.southlyonmi.org/document_center/City%20Council/Agenda%20packet%20and%20Minutes/2009/minutes/092809minutes.pdf?t=202108310733010#page=5)* (read by OCR; check the original)

If you did, you would divide up the duties. They are simply pointing out that controls over bank reconciliation is very important. They are recommending that you look into your departmental expense allocation. With tight budget constraints, if your general fund is somehow subsidizing other funds through administrative fees, it would make sense to charge those funds an appropriate amount so that the general fund does not have the full burden. They also noted some additional information controls based on a review that they did. If the City was larger and had more complex IT, they would be required to do a more indepth review. Due to the size of the City, they do an overview, however much of your IT is contracted out. Finally, they have the informational section, which are not specific South Lyon issues, but more broad finance issues with the two major issues being revenue sharing and property taxes. Council is aware of the issues facing both of those revenue sources. The economy is affecting the taxable value for the upcoming year. The taxable value is down 9.3% or a $450,000 affect on general fund. He stated that as it relates to Shared Revenue, he did check several websites today and he does not see any updates as to what is going on with the State budget. It is quite possible that State Shared Revenue will be eliminated in its entirety, which is another $130,000 hit to general fund. The constitutional portion of the revenue share is completely dependent on sales tax collected at the state level. When people are not buying consumer goods, not as much is collected resulting in not as much State Shared Revenue. They have already cut that back to 4.9% and it is possible if their current projection of sales tax collection does not come in where they think, there could be further cuts as well. As soon as they hear anything, they will make sure the City is informed. He stated that some other issues have also pointed out such as changes in the Investment Act, recent revisions to the State Transportation Funding Program, etc.

Councilman Kivell stated that one of the recommendations was to invest in Capital Assets software and asked Mr. Camiller if he knew how much that would cost. Mr. Camiller stated that there is a module for the City's General Ledger package and the cost would not be prohibitive.

Councilman Morelli stated that he would like to make it clear that the "A" grade that the City is given is based on the way we keep our books, not necessarily the financial situation we may be in. He asked Mr. Camiller what grade he would give about the financial shape. Mr. Camiller stated that they are often asked what amount the fund balance should be, and the answer is that you cannot ever have enough given with what is going to happen with property taxes and State Shared Revenues, etc. But, to have 60% of a year's expenditure in fund balance is well above average. Most neighboring communities are very jealous of the amount of fund balance the City has. That has been accumulated over the last 30 years and is a result of good decision making by both management and legislative levels of the City. However, it can be gone if you are not on top of it.

Mayor Doyle thanked Mr. Camiller and his staff. He stated that his interaction with them have been very professional. He thanked Lori Mosier, Bookkeeper who does a lot of work preparing for and through the audit process. He stated that we are very fortunate, but that does not mean that we can simply sit back. We still need to be very vigilant moving forward. He further thanked Councilman Wedell for his expertise as well.
