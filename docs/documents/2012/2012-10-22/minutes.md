### 4. Water/DPW Mobile Radio Purchase

Superintendent Martin stated that January 1, 2013, the City has to abide by a new FCC regulation which mandates a new type of radio with narrowbanding requirements. Our radios will not accept this. We do have radios in all of our trucks. We tried to find out if we could adjust our existing radios to narrowbanding, but it will not work. We received three bids. We budgeted for this. There are 19 radios in the DPW with radios in all of the vehicles, tractors, etc. and there are 12 in Water & Wastewater for a total of 31 radios. The low bid was from Cynergy Wireless in the amount of $10,626. This includes installation, programming and a two year warranty. This is approximately $600 less than what we budgeted. This is truly an unfunded mandate.

Councilman Kivell asked if we have any recourse with the FCC. Attorney Wilhelm stated that he will look into it.

Councilman Kivell asked if we are going to retain or sell the existing units. Superintendent Martin stated that he is not sure what we can do with them at this time. Chief Kennedy stated that the Fire Department went through the same thing last fiscal year. Chief Collins discussed the Oakland County system and the local activation of the tornado sirens. We own the radios inside the outdoor warning devices. Novi also had to narrowband the transmitter.

Councilman Kivell asked if there is a private entity that we could sell to. Chief Kennedy stated that we could put them out on the bid site. A private entity could use them.

## CM 10-7-12 – MOBILE RADIO PURCHASE

Motion by Kramer, supported by Kivell

To approve the purchase of 31 narrowband mobile radios from Cynergy Wireless in the amount of $10,626.

## MANAGER REPORT:

City Manager Murphy stated that we have not had the fire hydrants checked in private developments annually as they were supposed to. Two years ago, we said if you give us the easement, we will go out and do it just one time, and we did. After that they were supposed to provide us with a report every April indicating that they have been inspected and are working properly. They have not done that. We only had one development turn over their systems. Council Member Dixson stated that Colonial Acres has been trying. Attorney Wilhelm stated that the dedication is on his desk, but there are some issues. There are also two other developments trying to do the same thing. Further discussion was held on Colonial Acres.

Discussion was held on the reports generated when the City conducted the inspections.

Discussion was held on sending the developments a letter reminding them of the need to do the inspections. Council Member Kopkowski stated that she would like those letters sent certified. Superintendent Martin stated that he hand delivered the previous letter to approximately half the private developments in the City.

Discussion was held on providing a list of qualified inspectors. It was stated that we need to make sure that when the fire truck rolls up, there is a working hydrant. Discussion was held on the need for winterizing and annual maintenance.

Discussion was held on the City Code and the enforcement and penalty. Attorney Wilhelm stated that the ordinance identifies a violation as a Municipal Civil Infraction. He is not sure that the City could do the inspection and charge the development or place a lien on the property. He stated that there is a larger policy issue.

Council Member Kopkowski stated that we need to enforce for public safety. It was stated that it is an issue that should not be taken lightly.

Discussion was held on the Fire Department conducting inspection on those hydrants on the City's system.
