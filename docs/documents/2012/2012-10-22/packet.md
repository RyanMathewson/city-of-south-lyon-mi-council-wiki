### A. Introduction

The Michigan Municipal Risk Management Authority (hereinafter "MMRMA") is created by authority granted by the laws of the State of Michigan to provide risk financing and risk management services to eligible Michigan local governments. MMRMA is a separate legal and administrative entity as permitted by Michigan laws. City of South Lyon (hereinafter "Member") is eligible to be a Member of MMRMA. City of South Lyon agrees to be a Member of MMRMA and to avail itself of the benefits of membership.

City of South Lyon is aware of and agrees that it will be bound by all of the provisions of the Joint Powers Agreement, Coverage Documents, MMRMA rules, regulations, and administrative procedures.

This Coverage Proposal summarizes certain obligations of MMRMA and the Member. Except for specific coverage limits, attached addenda, and the Member's Self Insured Retention (SIR) and deductibles contained in this Coverage Proposal, the provisions of the Joint Powers Agreement, Coverage Documents, reinsurance agreements, MMRMA rules, regulations, and administrative procedures shall prevail in any dispute. The Member agrees that any dispute between the Member and MMRMA will be resolved in the manner stated in the Joint Powers Agreement and MMRMA rules.

# Understanding FCC Narrowbanding Requirements

## Key Points: FCC Narrowbanding Requirements

- The Federal Communications Commission (FCC) has mandated that all non-Federal public safety licensees using 25 kHz radio systems migrate to narrowband 12.5 kHz channels by January 1, 2013.

• Agencies that do not meet the deadline face the loss of communication capabilities.

- Agencies need to start planning now to migrate to narrowband systems by assessing their current radio equipment and applying for new or modified licenses.

## Overview

*[Image or figure on this page; see the original.]*

<div>Figure 1: Narrowband channels allow additional channels to exist in the same spectrum.</div>

Private land mobile radio (LMR) systems—including municipal government and State and local public safety systems—use blocks of radio spectrum called channels. Historically, LMR systems have used 25 kHz-wide channels. In December 2004, the Federal Communications Commission mandated that all private LMR users operating below 512 MHz move to 12.5 kHz narrowband voice channels and highly efficient data channel operations by January 1, 2013. This migration complements a National Telecommunications and Information Administration mandate for more rapid Federal agency migration to 12.5 kHz narrowband operation by January 1, 2008. The earlier Federal deadline affects State and local FCC licensees that interface or share frequencies with Federal radio systems.

Using narrowband channels will ensure that agencies take advantage of more efficient technology and, by reducing channel width, will allow additional channels to exist within the same spectrum space, as illustrated in figure 1.

<a id="p24"></a>
*[Page 24 of the original](https://www.southlyonmi.org/document_center/City%20Council/Agenda%20packet%20and%20Minutes/2012/packets/DOC101812CC10-22-12packet.pdf?t=202108310732590#page=24)* (read by OCR; check the original)

## Deadlines

To phase in the migration deadline of January 1, 2013, the FCC has established interim deadlines. The first important deadline is January 1, 2011, after which:

- The FCC will not grant applications for new voice operations or applications to expand the authorized contour of existing stations that use 25 kHz channels. Only narrowband authorizations will be granted.

- The FCC will prohibit manufacture or importation of new equipment that operates on 25 kHz channels. This will reduce the availability of new equipment for legacy radio systems and will affect how agencies maintain and upgrade older systems.

## Planning for the Move to Narrowband

School districts need to aggressively develop a strategy to meet narrowband deadlines to avoid cancellation of existing wideband FCC authorizations. Although the migration deadline may seem far off, the long lead time and interim deadlines make it necessary for agencies/facilities to plan well in advance.

Assess current equipment and start planning to prepare for the migration, school districts should start assessing their radio systems and planning for replacements or upgrades. They should inventory their current equipment to ascertain what can be converted to 12.5 kHz and what will need to be replaced before January 1, 2013. Most new equipment has the capability for both 25 kHz and 12.5 kHz operation because any VHF/UHF radio equipment accepted by the FCC after February 14, 1997, had to have 12.5 kHz capability. Some newly release radios have the capability for 6.25 kHz. Keep this in mind since the FCC “strongly urges” license holders to consider migrating directly to 6.25 kHz by the January 1st, 2013 rather than adopting the 12.5 kHz first and then having to narrow band once again to the 6.25 kHz.

Obtain new or modified licenses to move to narrowband operations, agencies must apply for new frequencies or modify existing licenses. An agency that is licensed for a 25 kHz-wide channel is not guaranteed two 12.5 kHz channels. Licensees will have to justify to the FCC why they need additional channels. Consideration of applications for new narrowband licenses will follow the same process as a new license application. As agencies migrate to narrowband operation, however, the pool of available frequencies will increase.

# South Lyon Historical Commission Meeting Thursday, October 4, 2012 Minutes

Members Present: Norm Somers, Phil Weipert, Larry Ledbetter, Jack Renwick, Roger Heiple, Bob Tremitiere

Members Absent: Beth Pfiles

Others Present: SLHS President Linda Ross

President Jack Renwick called the meeting to order at 7:30. The September minutes were approved (Norm motioned, Larry seconded)

Depot Day: Larry reported that the day was a big success, with a good turnout and great weather. He said that the addition of the antique tractors and cars was a crowd pleaser. All of the exhibitors expressed an interest in returning next year. Linda reported that financially, it was about the same as last year, but the final numbers have not been tabulated. The bagpiper suggested that we start off with an opening ceremony, including the Pledge of Allegiance. Larry added that we could sing Amazing Grace, to honor the departed. Bob suggested that we have copies of the words to pass out. Larry also suggested that we have a music theme for the day, and have a few different groups perform in the Gazebo. Roger mentioned that his brother has a swing band. A new addition this year was Thomas the Train, which was very popular with the kids. Norm suggested that we get a different Thomas next year, that this one was a bit scruffy.

2012-13 Projects:

Gazebo Steps repair and sidewalk brick repair: Bob reported that he has two estimates, one for $1180 from Davor Zander, and another for $2400 from Greg Bricker. Larry said that there are two Scouts interested in pursuing the gazebo repairs and painting as an Eagle project. He has offered it to one, and if that Scout doesn't take it, he will offer it to the other. This project would include the stair and brick repair, which would probably be turned over to a contractor, as well as other repairs such as railing repair and repainting. The City would also like us to add lattice to the underside to keep kids out. Norm motioned, and Phil seconded, that we hire Davor Zander to do the brick and stair work. The motion passed.

Tree at end of Tracks: Bob asked if we want to pursue replacing the tree as we had talked about doing earlier in the year. The tree is healthy, but has a large growth around the base of the stump which is unsightly and keeps sprouting. Linda will ask the Society what their wish is, and will ask Mary Ann Guerriero for advice on what kind of replacement tree would be appropriate. Norm added that we need to be sure that a replacement tree is one that will not get so big that it makes it difficult to take pictures from that end.

Repair signs: We have an estimate from Donner to repair the signs, but have not acted on it. Bob motioned that we authorize him to do the work. Roger seconded the motion and it passed. Larry said that he will supply pipe to replace the warped 4x4s holding up the signs at the two ends of the parking lot.

Paint Depot: We have this item in the budget for this year, but some of it was completed as part of the recent wainscote replacement. Bob suggested that before we repaint the rest of the building, that we replace a few deteriorated clapboards on the south bay, and also the roof overhang. It was suggested that David Brown (who replaced the wainscote) might be interested in the work.

2013-14 Projects: Bob reminded that we will be asked in a few months to come up with a budget for the 2013-14 fiscal year. We discussed several possible projects:

-Replace wainscote on south side of freight house with Azek (like we did on Depot)

-Replace siding on south side of caboose with Azek

-Upgrade gazebo electrical. Bob Martin suggested this. Bob T. will contact him to get his recommendations.

Phil confirmed that the breakers often pop during a concert. Norm suggested that we bring in a drop from the Chapel, which has more capacity than the School, which is where the electric currently comes from.

-Washburn School rotted window sash: We agreed to do it this year. Larry will contact Village Glass.

-Linda suggested that we add a bike rack. We agreed that it should be near the Depot, and should be placed on pavement to eliminate the DPW having to trim around it.
