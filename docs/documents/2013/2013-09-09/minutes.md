### 4. Knolls of South Lyon

Carmin Avantini of CIB Planning stated Mr. Elkow first applied to Planning for a rezoning from R2 to R3 to develop this as a single family development. The Planning Commission suggested the developer come back with a request for a planned development which will have a little more flexibility, and to save natural features and work with the open spaces. He stated the planned development density is the same as the R2 development. He further stated with this plan, they have saved a lot of areas around the wetlands and it has valuable open space for walking trails. Mr. Avantini stated the biggest item of debate was the second means of access. The only potential of a second entrance would be the closest to Mill Street which is Kestrel Ridge. There are some concerns from the Police and Fire Department because they would like it to be a thoroughfare. The residents do not want to have a second access because of traffic. He stated as a middle ground with the residents they could have an emergency access. He further stated some residents want nothing there at all, some want a thru street, and some people want an emergency access. He further stated if Council approves this planned development rezoning, it will then go back to the Planning Commission for final site plan review approval which includes the building envelope, the design of the buildings, sidewalks, landscaping, and the emergency access. He stated the residents are concerned about using the emergency access for emergencies only and to make sure it is maintained. Mayor Wallace stated he is concerned about not having an access to

Eagle Heights, and there are too many dead ends in the City. He further stated at Clarks Crossing the residents didn't want the second access, but now they love it. Mr. Avantini stated that it is always the number one choice to have a thru connection, but the emergency access was offered as a middle ground to protect the residents who had concerns about traffic. Councilman Kivell stated there isn't a natural melding of the two developments to have a thru street. He further stated to have an access that close to the main entrance is not effective as if there was an entrance half way thru or at the other end of the development. Discussion was held regarding another access point. Councilman Kivell stated we have had some problems with side and rear setbacks in other subdivisions and people have had to apply for a variance to add on a deck. He further stated this development will be facing the same thing. He stated he would like to see floor plans and elevations so we can understand the width and depth so we can see how they will fit on the building envelope. Mr. Avantini stated there are only 5 lots that will not meet the frontage because they are on a curve. Councilman Kivell stated there is a lot of information to digest in just a couple of days. He has many concerns regarding this. Mr. Avantini stated this is just the first of two readings, and Council is not being asked to approve this as a final plan. He further stated if Council has any questions he will be available to answer them before the second reading. Councilman Kivell stated our ordinance stated R2 in open space is 8750. He stated the developer is stating 7200 as the proposed development of the lots. He further stated the marginal decreases of an area can make a big difference if people would like to add a deck to their home. Councilman Kivell stated the City will end up being exposed because we have the final say, and we should act as responsibly as possible. He stated HRC couldn't make any comments on how this development will impact our waste water and water system. Mr. Avantini stated the developer will bring the layouts and designs at the final site plan and at that time HRC will be able to assess things, and if it changes the layout significantly it would have to come back to Council. Councilmember Kopkowski stated she has many of the same concerns as Councilman Kivell, and she would have liked to have seen a basic presentation to see what things will look like on that site. She further stated she would like to hear the Chief's concerns on the emergency exit as opposed to a thru fare. Chief Collins stated his main concern is the significant traffic at peak times, such as morning rushes, people trying to go to work, driving their kids to school and the school buses. If you only have one entrance it could cause trouble. He further stated it will hamper the patrol patterns because it will be too predictable if someone wanted to commit a crime in that development.

Councilmember Kopkowski stated she does feel it was a lot of material to read in a short amount of time, and Mr. Elkow at other developments has put in a farming clause, and she would like to know if there is a farming clause in this development. Mr. Avantini stated there is not. Councilman Kramer stated in his subdivision there are 167 homes, and for many years, there was only one access point, how is that different. Chief Collins stated that was built before he was the Chief and he had no input on that. He asked how the emergency entrance would be controlled. Mr. Avantini stated it could be gated and made to not look like a thoroughfare. He further stated it would just be the minimum width necessary to be accessed by fire trucks and Police and Fire would have access to the gate with possibly a lockbox.

Councilman Kramer asked if this is approved, and the developer sold the property to a third party, would that buyer be held to this preliminary plan. Mr. Avantini stated yes, and any significant changes would have to be brought back to Council. Councilman Kramer stated this is just to approve the first reading of the rezoning from R2 to PD. Mr. Avantini stated yes, and Council will review this again at the second reading. Councilman Ryzyi asked if there was support from the residents for this development. Mr. Avantini stated, most residents were not against the development itself, they were just against the second access, into their subdivision. City Attorney stated no one wanted the access next to their home. Councilman Kivell stated item #2D in the agenda states usable open space should be provided.

at least equal to the total of the minimum usable open space which would be required for each of the component uses of the development. The City may, if deemed appropriate, require for planned developments more or less open space than that required by this chapter. He asked what is the minimum open space used in the development. Councilman Kivell asked if this was approved and turned into a planned development, would it return back to R2 if not acted on within a year? Mr. Paul Elkow stated he would like to say at the Planning Commission, the residents understood that they need another road. The residents thought they lived on a cul de sac, but their streets actually were not. They were not against the development; they were against the change that a road through their sub would change the traffic level. He further stated he is looking to provide a quality development and would like to add value to the homes that are already there. Mr. Elkow stated they did take their time and didn't try to ram anything through. He wants to have reasonably priced homes, such as $230,000 to $275,000, and 1400 to 1650 square feet. He further stated he feels they will raise the value of the homes in the area, and the Planning Commission will see what houses are being planned. Mr. Elkow stated they are still working on what the right product will be. He further stated he does not want to upsize the homes that are there. Councilman Kivell stated he would like to know if Mr. Elkow would entertain the idea of putting together a plan for R2 open space. Mr. Elkow stated the planning commission worked really hard on this and he is comfortable with what he is offering. He further stated people like the smaller lots because they are more manageable and he thinks this project will be well received by the public. Chief Collins stated he does not perceive the developer as the enemy of public safety, he realizes the residents were unhappy with having a thoroughfare on their street. He further stated if 8 or 9 residents objecting, versus the first responsibility of the City is the safety of all residents, and we need that access for public safety. He further stated the second access point would provide two ways out for the residents as well as the busses. Mr. Elkow stated he would like to find a way to work with the residents, Police and Fire departments to find a solution to this second access issue. Mr. Avantini stated the open space option is something they could do, but it does take away part of the open space, he would recommend the developer to show footprints with the decks to show they would fit on the envelope, because he feels the open space is important. Councilman Kivell stated if they cut back on the number of houses, they wouldn't lose a lot of the open space. Councilman Kivell stated he would like more time to investigate the information before voting on this. Mr. Elkow stated he is asking Council to approve the rezoning then he will continue with the Planning Commission and he will also share the information with Council.

Darren Anderson of 713 Kestrel Ridge court stated it was not just 9 people that lived on the court that were upset at the Planning Commission meeting. Their main concern was if the second access point was a thoroughfare. The plan they looked at and yelled about for 2 hours showed a full road access. He further stated by the end of the meeting they were happy because it was changed to be an emergency access only, not a thoroughfare. He stated the families would like to keep this as an emergency access only, not a thoroughfare.
