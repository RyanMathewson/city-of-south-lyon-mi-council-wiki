1. Gary Patrouitch, Director of Traffic Safety, Road Commission for Oakland County

City Manager Murphy stated that this is in regards to changing the speed limit on Ten Mile from 25 to 35 mph as he reported to Council in his last Manager's Report. A representative from the Road Commission has come in to explain the issue to Council.

Mr. Patrouitch, Director of Traffic Safety from the Road Commission from Oakland County stated that this is a result of a change in State Law which affects how we set speed limits, specifically at the County level. One of the elements is what is called the prima facia speed limit allowing for an access point method where they count the number of access points along the roadway. Based on that number, we could set the speed limit. It eliminated the “residential speed zones” that we had as well as allowing us to set 25 mph in platted subdivisions. You can also set a speed limit using an engineering study. The largest impact of the change was how we set the speed limits on gravel roads, which caused them to take a look at all of the speed limits in the County and determine whether we had a valid traffic control order. There are a number of speed limits on our roadways that have no Traffic Control Order or were under a prima facia under the previous law that was no longer allowable. One of these locations was Ten Mile Road through the City of South Lyon. There is no Traffic Control Order for the section between Woodland Drive and Reynold Sweet Parkway, which is currently signed at 25 mph. They looked at the law, and there are two options. The first option is that we can try and establish prima facia speed limits under the current law. There is still a commercial district allowance under the law, which would affect the area between Washington and Wells and allow them to prima facia that limit at 25 mph. The remainder of that area no longer meets that 25 mph zone. Under the access point method, they could post that area at 35 mph. Without going back to the State Police, they would be allowed to change those areas to 35 mph. The alternative would be to bring the State Police in and do an engineering study. However, based on their experience so far, you may get 25 mph in the downtown area, but it is highly unlikely that the remaining areas would be 25, more likely 35 mph. It is more likely that the two ends of town would go higher. The State Police require them to survey the entire Traffic Control Order which would be Ten Mile from our west county line all the way into Farmington Hills. There is no way to simply look at the area that we do not have a Traffic Control Order, but rather everything re-reviewed. The way things are going now, speed limits are going up. The State Police are trying to set a more realistic speed to what people are driving. Mr. Patrouitch stated that when we set an 85 percentile speed, it has been proven that 85% of the people drive safe for a specific roadway. The idea of setting speed limits is not to try and slow people down, but rather keep the road safe. A lower speed does not necessarily mean a safer road. He further discussed setting speed limits.

Councilman Kramer asked if we had the State Police come in and do a study, could the speed limit be above the 35 mph. Mr. Patrouitch stated that it is possible. He stated that he has driven the stretch of road several times and paced a lot of people. The prima facia 35 mph might not go higher, but below 35 mph is highly unlikely.

Councilman Kivell stated that we are at contrary objectives. The Road Commission's objective is to move traffic as quickly at safe speeds as they possibly can. Our concern is that we have a residential area through the vast majority of downtown, which is the reason it is 25 mph. The concern is not the safety of drivers, but the safety of people that live adjacent to that street. Every subdivision in the City is 25 mph to protect the residents of those streets. He discussed the areas west of Woodland and to the east of Reynold Sweet Parkway. He stated that the safety concern of the commercial area is recognized, but not the safety concern of the residential area. He discussed gravel and rural areas and how it compares to Ten Mile. Mr. Patrouitch stated that they are simply interrupting laws and applying it. The point they like to make is that if people are already driving 34 mph even if you set the speed at 25 mph you may think you are safer. If they are going to drive 34 mph what is going to change if the sign changes. Councilman Kivell stated that we have our own studies and people are driving 25 mph. Mr. Patrouitch stated that they did some studies in the area and they were not. If they are driving 25 than we would be able to set the limit at 25.

### 2. Gary VandenVork, Lawrence Technological University

Superintendent Martin stated that late last winter we started talking about looking at the wastewater plant to see if we could be more energy efficient. In doing that, we went to Lawrence Institute, and the college and the City has worked very closely. Gary VandenVork who is a graduate student at the college has come forward to present to Council what they have done.

Mr. Gary VandenVork stated that he has been working on his MBA at Lawrence University and has a concentration in Energy Environment Management and Alternative Energy Engineering. He stated that an upgrade to the facility was started in 2005 as a result of a mandate from the DEQ because of phosphate limits, etc. He described the new system versus the old system including a new building with approximately 21 motors. He discussed the way that the electric power enters the facility, which is part of what the study was about. He discussed the difference in power consumption and gas consumption before and after the upgrade with electric increasing 90% and gas 103%. He stated that electricity typically increases 2-3% each year and can increase up to 5%. He looked at the efficiency of the motors including the power factor and the amp analysis of the motor. He discussed capacitors, which is a small way of holding energy in order to draw it. He stated that the power factor of the facility is running at approximately 94%, which is good. He discussed the capacitors that are currently there. He stated that what he is proposing is to make sure the motor is working efficient, and if not we could add additional capacitors. Two areas to resolve inefficient motors is to install a high-efficiency motor the other is to put in a capacitor. He discussed the process of looking at amperage and power factor. He discussed the apparatus and how it works. He presented information some of the motors that they tested. He stated that the cost of the capacitor is $23,000 and it guarantees a 6-10% savings. The guarantee is associated with the motor that has the capacitors on it. If you look at the savings, you are looking at approximately a 2 year payback.

Councilman Morelli asked the life expectancy of the unit. Mr. VandenVork stated that the life expectancy would be approximately 20 years and guaranteed for 5.

Councilman Selden asked if there is any grant monies available for this type of project. Mr. VandenVork stated that they are working with DTE and FEMA trying to get this approved for some grant or rebate money.

Councilman Selden asked if we should be looking at a more efficient motor. Mr. VandenVork stated that it is cheaper to put in capacitors.

Mr. VandenVork thanked Superintendent Martin for allowing Lawrence Tech to work with the City and he is sure he will be working with them again with some other students.

Councilman Morelli asked if we looked at any other types of alternative energy such as solar or wind. Mr. VandenVork stated that the class he is taking this semester is wind and solar generation. If they do a project, he will be coming out to do more work. Further discussion was held on wind energy. Mr. VandenVork stated that it does depend on the size of wind turban as well as wind factor, etc.
