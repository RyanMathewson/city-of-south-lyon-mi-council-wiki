### 2. Chevy Tahoe purchase- Police Dept

Chief Unruh stated they are requesting approval to replace the 2019 Ford Explorer which has 106,455 miles on it and has experienced numerous maintenance issues and is showing signs of significant wear. Ongoing repairs have become costly and disruptive to operations, making it no longer reliable for frontline or support use. This replacement will allow for reassignment of existing vehicles within the department to better align with operational needs. Specifically, the 2022 Ford F-150 and is currently serving as the command vehicle, will be reassigned as the utility vehicle, its original intended use. This truck is more practical for utility functions, particularly for the truck bed can be used for hauling equipment to the range and property to the storage unit at the water treatment plant. The newly purchased vehicle, a 2025 Chevy Tahoe will serve as a patrol vehicle. Current patrol vehicle 553 will now be utilized as a command vehicle. She further stated she received 3 bids and Berger Chevrolet was the lowest. Discussion was held regarding the three quotes.

138 S. Lafayette: As you may know, the City owns the property at 138 S. Lafayette and Liberty, which is currently used as a City parking lot. The property was formerly a gas station, and the former property owner has been working with EGLE on remediation. Recently, we received communication from a representative of the former property owner requesting the City agree to restrict use of the property by the execution and recording of a restrictive covenants which would, in summary: prohibit use of the property as a residence; restrict groundwater use; and require compliance with EGLE regulations for the construction of buildings on the property. However, after discussions with the City Attorney and EGLE we learned that there are alternatives to remediation including excavation of the southern portion of the property, or collection of more soil gas samples to see if we get better results or waiting for the concentrations to continue to decline naturally. Since there are alternatives that will not encumber the City's property, we declined to consider the restrictive covenant option. If Council would like to discuss this issue further, please let me know and we can put it on a future agenda.
