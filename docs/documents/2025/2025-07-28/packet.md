### 1. City Council Rules of Procedure

City Manager Zelenak stated in 2020 city council adopted rules and procedures to ensure efficient operation of our city meetings. I am recommending that city council adopt the revised rules of procedure which rearranges the order in which the public comment will be accepted. Specifically, it would allow public comment before approval of the consent agenda to allow the public to comment on anything that is on the consent agenda, prior to adoption. It would also allow public comment after all new business items but before the Manager's report and council comments.

### 2. Contribution for medical insurance at retirement policy

Finance and Benefit Administrator Tiernan explained that the City of South Lyon contribution for medical insurance at retirement policy for non-union/non-contract employees in the current personnel policy manual language is vague and does not align with current collective bargaining agreements. In an effort to lower OPEB liability costs and avoid potential employee grievances, the city proposed a revised policy. The revised policy will define eligibility in all current city non-union/non-contract employees hired before July 1, 2024 as eligible for a $500 monthly stipend upon separation of employment provided the employee is at least 55 years of age at the time of separation. The second phase will be effective for all

<a id="p4"></a>
*[Page 4 of the original](https://www.southlyonmi.org/July%2028%202025%20online%20council%20packet%20final.pdf?t=202507251447220#page=4)* (read by OCR; check the original)

non-union/non-contract employees hired on or after July 1, 2025 as eligible for $500 monthly stipened upon separation of employment provided the employee is at least 55 years of age and been employed with the city for a minimum of 15 years at the time of separation. The $500 monthly stipend shall also be provided to employees who are determined to be permanently disable; due to work-related injury, from performing their job at the city by the city's workers compensation insurance provider. She then stated all other language will remain the same. Councilmember Kurtzweil asked if this increases the monthly reimbursement. Finance and Benefit Administrator Tiernan stated this defines retirement, because right our policy doesn't define retirement. Right now, an employee could retire at 50 and be eligible. We are looking to create the definition so there is no question if we deny someone because we felt they don't qualify for retirement from the city. But the state of Michigan has shown in court cases with other municipalities that they are a little more flexible in their interpretation, especially in recent years, so by us defining it we'll have it in our policy and as our best practice. She further stated hopefully that will control any potential grievances or lawsuits if it happens. Councilmember Kurtzweil asked how this is lowering our OPEB. Finance and Benefit Administrator Tiernan stated when we are doing our evaluation with Nyhart, because we don't have the definition with our non-union membership they are choosing an age. They are looking at a 15–20-year average for the non-union employees, based on when they are hired in which means they are predicting a longer liability. Councilmember Mosier stated the contracts are 25 and 55. She then asked why are we considering 15 years, that is longevity as far as she is concerned. Finance and Benefit Administrator Tiernan stated this is what was suggested by our labor attorney's because it is more common now for the work force to be older, and people are changing careers more often. It is more common for people to have a second career at an older age and may not work 25 years, but may be over 55 years of age, but work 15–20 years. Councilmember Mosier stated she would like to have it at least see 20 years. Councilmember Dilg asked how many people does this cover. Finance and Benefit Administrator Tiernan stated 8. Councilmember Kurtzweil asked how many have been here for close to 15 years. Finance and Benefit Administrator stated around 5.

## Purpose of Performance Evaluation

The annual performance review provides an important opportunity for the City Council and the City Manager to reflect on the past year's accomplishments, strengthen communication, and align on priorities for the future. This process is designed to:

- Evaluate the City Manager's performance and progress toward established goals.

Foster a collaborative dialogue to ensure mutual understanding of expectations.

Identify strengths, areas for development, and opportunities for enhancing city operations.

- Set clear objectives for the coming year to advance the City's mission and vision.

The goal is to create a constructive and forward-looking evaluation that benefits the City of South Lyon, its residents, and its employees.

## Evaluation Process

### 1. Self-Evaluation

○ By August 31, the City Manager will complete a self-evaluation addressing each section of this form.

The self-evaluation will be submitted to the City Clerk, who will distribute it to all Council members.

### 2. Council Member Feedback

Within two weeks of receiving the City Manager's self-evaluation, Council members will review and provide written feedback for each section of the form.

o Feedback should include specific observations and constructive comments, where applicable.

o Council members will return their completed forms to the City Clerk.

### 3. Compilation & Distribution

o The City Clerk will compile the Council members' feedback into a consolidated document.

The compiled evaluation will be shared with the City Manager and City Council for review prior to the meeting.
