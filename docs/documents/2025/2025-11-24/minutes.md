DDA Director Rivera updated Council on downtown events. She stated the Ladies Night Out was a great event and she thanked the sponsors, Lockwood of Lyon, Learning Experience, Mathnasium, Orange Theory, as well as the volunteers, and the generosity of the community for the Active Faith donations. She then stated there have been 379 responses to the branding survey. She then stated they will be doing 4 random drawings for the gift cards. ## FIRE CHIEF REPORT

Fire Chief Conrad stated they have had 1,283 year to date. Up over last year with a month to go in the year. Frank Walsh of Walsh Municipal Services gave a brief presentation regarding the City Manager Executive Search. He appreciates his company being chosen. He then thanked Clerk Deaton for her assistance with this process. He then stated the recruitment climate is difficult right now. He further stated back in the day, Boyne City was very popular, and they are now searching for the second time. Belding is going out for their 3rd time. He believes its being driven because people don't want to relocate. It is very difficult to recruit. He then explained the process of searching for candidates. He stated we have to be open to people in other professions. He then stated the process begins with a community profile. He stated they have completed 800 surveys of what candidates are looking for is the stability of the elected body. He then stated #2 is the salary, and the 3rd is the local school system. He stated we have high hurdles and we know where the competition is. Some communities are paying higher salaries, but South Lyon has a great community, downtown, and location. 5. Bid acceptance for water service main transfer project

DPW Superintendent Archey stated the DPW is requesting the services of a qualified company to perform a water main transfer project. The work involves relocating all existing water services and main line connections from the aging 8-inch cast iron water main located between Dorothy Street and Princeton Drive to the adjacent 12-inch ductile iron water main. The 8-inch main, which has experienced eight water main breaks in recent years, has become increasingly unreliable due to its age and material deterioration. To improve the reliability and efficiency of the city's water distribution system, all active service connections and water mains currently tied to the 8-inch main will be transferred to the newer 12-inch main. Once all the connections are made, the contractor will cut and cap both ends of the 8-inch main to ensure it is fully disconnected and no longer part of the city's active water system.

Councilmember Bogert asked about the difference between cast iron and ductile iron. Mr. Darga from HRC stated cast iron is older and ductile iron is stronger and it is used in the city's standards. He further stated once cast iron gets to 50 years old, you'll start having more problems with it. Councilmember Popa asked if there will be any effect on the city residents while this upgrade is being made. Mr. Darga stated there shouldn't be, it is set up so there is no disturbance of service, although there could be a time when they are connecting there could be a few hours disruption. Councilmember Hanson asked about the reason behind the difference in the bid amounts. Mr. Darga and Mr. Roland from HRC explained that they only have a certain amount of time to get the work done, and sometimes it fits into certain contractors' schedules easier than others. They stated they have worked with D'Angelo on many jobs and they are a good contractor and will do a good job for the city. Councilmember Kurtzweil stated we don't have a City Manager in place, and she wants to make sure HRC has sufficient staff to keep the job moving forward while the city is without a City Manager. Roland stated they have enough staff, and it will be the same group of people that have been working with the city project over the last few years.

6. Agreement and requirements for installation of license plate reader cameras

The Police Department is requesting the agreement to be signed for the placement of two license plate readers on county-maintained roads in the city of South Lyon. Both license plate readers will be placed on opposite ends of Ten Mile, one of Dixboro and the other Martindale. The license plate readers were included in the Police Department budget for fiscal year 2025-2026. Councilmember Bogert stated he motioned for this to be removed from the consent agenda because he has concerns about the privacy of the information that is collected. He further stated Flock cameras collect license plates from every vehicle that passes the camera. It also collects the identity of the car, the make, the color, the model of the vehicle, and from what he has seen, there is insufficient safeguards to protect the privacy of the people in the vehicles. He further stated anyone in law enforcement could have access to this and there have already been cases where there have been abuses by individuals in law enforcement for stalking. There are municipalities that are mired in lawsuits right now regarding Freedom of Information Requests which municipalities have to process. He then stated there are also lawsuits with other municipalities regarding 4th amendment violations from using this technology. The FBI has to have a warrant to put a tracking device on your vehicle. With this, anyone can put in a license plate number and see where your vehicle and where it can potentially be. Councilmember Squires asked how the South Lyon Police Department plan on using the cameras. Chief Unruh stated the information from the camera would be used as an investigation tool, it can also be used for a missing person. She further stated if someone is missing, we can find where their car was last. Having the flock cameras allows Police Officers to put in a license plate and see where the car went. She further stated Councilmember Bogert is correct and we can access other agencies cameras, so if someone is missing from South Lyon, and we enter the license plate, we can tell where the car has been and where it is going. Councilmember Bogert then stated he is a big supporter of law enforcement, and he pushed for the drone for the city. She further stated when you look around the world and see what other countries are going through with starvation, and the murders of Catholics and Christians in Nigeria, she realizes how lucky we are to live in a country where she can go to her own church regardless of you are Muslim, Catholic, Jewish or Christian, you have a community you can come and pray and not worry about being beheaded. That's the reality of the world we live in. We live in a great country and we have a lot to be thankful for this Thursday. She then stated, enjoy it, be thankful and share what you can with someone else, even if it's a handshake, a smile or a pat on the back, but share something on Thanksgiving. She then stated for the Carl and Joanne Foundation, she wants to say she has received many phone calls about how the tree got there, and so she posted the great video that is now on Facebook showing how the tree got from point A to point B. She further stated the tree is gaining a lot of notoriety, popularity and she thanked Jeff and his family for what they do for the City of South Lyon. We need a benefactor like him. We need their heart and commitment to the city. She then thanked Jeff, his dad and everyone on the team and wished them a Happy Thanksgiving.

Mayor Kennedy welcomed Council Member Popa and Council Member Squires to the City Council and thanked them for volunteering to help their city. He then thanked the voters for their support in returning Council Member Hansen and himself to the city council for another term, and he looks forward to continue building upon the progress and achievements we have made over the past several years to further improve things in our community.
