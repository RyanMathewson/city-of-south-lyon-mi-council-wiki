# ROAD COMMISSION FOR OAKLAND COUNTY

# AGREEMENT & REQUIREMENTS FOR LICENSE PLATE READER CAMERAS

## LOCATED IN the CITY of SOUTH LYON

WHEREAS, the Road Commission for Oakland County ("RCOC") has jurisdiction over certain public roads located in the City of South Lyon ("Municipality"); and

WHEREAS, RCOC allows installation of license plate reader cameras and posts by local municipalities on a case-by-case basis, provided that such installations do not result in an adverse impact to public safety or future roadway construction projects, that there is no cost to RCOC for installation or maintenance, and that certain design and permit requirements are met; and

WHEREAS, the Municipality wishes to install license plate reader cameras and posts ("Cameras and Supports") in the Municipality along certain public roadway(s) under the jurisdiction and control of RCOC ("RCOC Right-of-Way");

NOW THEREFORE, in consideration of the mutual covenants contained herein, it is hereby agreed between the parties as follows:

### 1. $ \underline{\text{Municipality Obligations}} $

The Municipality agrees to comply with the following terms and conditions for the installation and maintenance of Cameras and Supports along RCOC Right-of-Way in the Municipality.

### a.  $ \underline{\text{Permits}} $

The Municipality or its subcontractor shall acquire a permit from RCOC prior to the initial installation of any Cameras and Supports. The permit application shall include detailed drawings of the Cameras and Supports with locations depicted on a plan map. A contact phone number for the Municipality department responsible for subcontractor maintenance must be supplied as part of the permit application to allow the RCOC to report damage to Cameras and Supports to the City.

### b.  $ \underline{\text{Construction}} $

All Cameras and Supports shall be installed in accordance with the current edition of the Michigan Manual of Uniform Traffic Control Devices ("MMUTCD").

All Cameras and Supports shall be located no closer than five (5) feet to the back of curb or back edge of the gravel shoulder.

Each license plate reader camera must include a sticker on the back with the contact number of the Municipality department or subcontractor that is responsible for maintenance.

## Background

From the 1960s through the early 1990s, the appearance and vitality of downtown South Lyon suffered as the impacts of commercial sprawl and downtown disinvestment were felt within the City and within the immediate vicinity. The South Lyon Downtown Development Authority was established by ordinance of the City Council on February 13, 1995. The boundaries of the DDA district were modified by City Ordinance in 1997. In 2000/2001, a Tax Increment Financing Plan was approved by the City, establishing the DDA's tax increment base year as December 31, 2000, as equalized.

This Tax Increment Financing Plan is designed to

This section follows the informational requirements for Tax Increment Financing Plans as outlined in Section 214 of Part 2 – Downtown Development Authorities section of the Recodified Tax Increment Financing Act, PA 57 of 2018.

## Explanation of Tax Increment Procedure

Tax increment financing is a method of funding public investments in an area slated for redevelopment by capturing all or a portion of the increased tax revenue that occurs within the district. The concept of tax increment financing is applied only to the district for which a Development Plan has been prepared by a Downtown Development Authority (the South Lyon Downtown Development Authority) and adopted by the community's legislative body (the South Lyon City Council).

The Recodified Tax Increment Financing Act treats all increases in valuation resulting from the development plan whether in fact these increases bear any relation to the Authority's activities or not. All taxing jurisdictions that levy ad valorem taxes (taxes levied as a percentage of the taxable value of real or personal property) within the DDA are subject to DDA capture unless they've been specifically exempted under PA 57 of 2018. Ad valorem taxes include City, County, Library, special millages, etc. The amount to be transmitted to the DDA is that portion of the tax levy of all of these applicable taxing bodies paid each year on real and personal property that exceeds the base year valuation.

*[Image or figure on this page; see the original.]*

“Captured value” means the amount in any one year by which the current taxable value of the district, including the value of property for which specific local taxes are paid in lieu of property taxes, exceeds the initial value. “Initial value” means the taxable value, of all the property within the boundaries of the district

Property for which a commercial facilities exemption certificate or an industrial facilities exemption certificate is in effect shall not be considered to be property which is exempt from taxation. Tax dollars accruing from any incremental increase in taxable value above the initial value (base year total) is captured and may be expended by the DDA for any activities that are included within the entity's adopted development plan.

## Regional Initiatives

When appropriate, the DDA staff should support regional efforts that have a direct correlation to providing benefits within DDA district including regional greenways initiatives, downtown district enhancements, public transit, and other regional opportunities as they become available.

Though outside of the district and not eligible for DDA funding, extension of the Huron Valley Trail west to the Mike Levine/Great Lake to Great Lake Trail #1 in Whitmore Lake would be beneficial to Downtown South Lyon. The Great Lake to Lake Trail #1 is becoming one of Michigan's premier cross-state trail routes, and creating this linkage would fill-in one of the remaining gaps in this trail, directing new nonmotorized traffic through Downtown South Lyon and to patronize businesses within the historic Downtown.

Utilization of staff time would be appropriate for supporting these initiatives; however, the expenditure of dollars outside of the district would not be permissible under state law. Development of regional initiatives would be beneficial to the DDA and the City as their successful implementation would bring visitors into the DDA district, providing benefits to the residents and business owners within both the District and the wider City.

EXPLANATION OF TOPIC: Water Main Transfer – Dorothy Street to Princeton Drive

The Department of Public Works (DPW) is requesting the services of a qualified company to perform a water main transfer project. The work involves relocating all existing water services and main line connections from the aging 8-inch cast iron water main located between Dorothy Street and Princeton Drive to the adjacent 12-inch ductile iron water main.
