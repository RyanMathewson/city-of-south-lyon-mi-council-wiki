### 4. 2026 Road Improvement Program- Oak Creek

5. Bid acceptance for water service main transfer project

DPW Superintendent Archey stated the DPW is requesting the services of a qualified company to perform a water main transfer project. The work involves relocating all existing water services and main line connections from the aging 8-inch cast iron water main located between Dorothy Street and Princeton Drive to the adjacent 12-inch ductile iron water main. The 8-inch main, which has experienced eight water main breaks in recent years, has become increasingly unreliable due to its age and material deterioration. To improve the reliability and efficiency of the city's water distribution system, all active service connections and water mains currently tied to the 8-inch main will be transferred to the newer 12-inch main. Once all the connections are made, the contractor will cut and cap both ends of the 8-inch main to ensure it is fully disconnected and no longer part of the city's active water system.

Councilmember Bogert asked about the difference between cast iron and ductile iron. Mr. Darga from HRC stated cast iron is older and ductile iron is stronger and it is used in the city's standards. He further stated once cast iron gets to 50 years old, you'll start having more problems with it. Councilmember Popa asked if there will be any effect on the city residents while this upgrade is being made. Mr. Darga stated there shouldn't be, it is set up so there is no disturbance of service, although there could be a time when they are connecting there could be a few hours disruption. Councilmember Hanson asked about the reason behind the difference in the bid amounts. Mr. Darga and Mr. Roland from HRC explained that they only have a certain amount of time to get the work done, and sometimes it fits into certain contractors' schedules easier than others. They stated they have worked with D'Angelo on many jobs and they are a good contractor and will do a good job for the city. Councilmember Kurtzweil stated we don't have a City Manager in place, and she wants to make sure HRC has sufficient staff to keep the job moving forward while the city is without a City Manager. Roland stated they have enough staff, and it will be the same group of people that have been working with the city project over the last few years.

6. Agreement and requirements for installation of license plate reader cameras

The Police Department is requesting the agreement to be signed for the placement of two license plate readers on county-maintained roads in the city of South Lyon. Both license plate readers will be placed on opposite ends of Ten Mile, one of Dixboro and the other Martindale. The license plate readers were included in the Police Department budget for fiscal year 2025-2026. Councilmember Bogert stated he motioned for this to be removed from the consent agenda because he has concerns about the privacy of the information that is collected. He further stated Flock cameras collect license plates from every vehicle that passes the camera. It also collects the identity of the car, the make, the color, the model of the vehicle, and from what he has seen, there is insufficient safeguards to protect the privacy of the people in the vehicles. He further stated anyone in law enforcement could have access to this and there have already been cases where there have been abuses by individuals in law enforcement for stalking. There are municipalities that are mired in lawsuits right now regarding Freedom of Information Requests which municipalities have to process. He then stated there are also lawsuits with other municipalities regarding 4 $ ^{th} $ amendment violations from using this technology. The FBI has to have a warrant to put a tracking device on your vehicle. With this, anyone can put in a license plate number and see where your vehicle and where it can potentially be. Councilmember Squires asked how the South Lyon Police Department plan on using the cameras. Chief Unruh stated the information from the camera would be used as an investigation tool, it can also be used for a missing person. She further stated if someone is missing, we can find where their car was last. Having the flock cameras allows Police Officers to put in a license plate and see where the car went. She further stated Councilmember Bogert is correct and we can access other agencies cameras, so if someone is missing from South Lyon, and we enter the license plate, we can tell where the car has been and where it is going. WHEREAS, the qualified electors of the City of South Lyon, County of Oakland, State of Michigan (the “City”), at the election duly called and held on August 2, 2022, did by more than the required majority of those voting approve the following proposition (the “2022 Proposal”):

## Street Improvements Bond Proposal

Shall the City of South Lyon, County of Oakland, Michigan, borrow the principal sum of not to exceed Eighteen Million Four Hundred Sixty-Five Thousand Dollars ($18,465,000), and issue its unlimited tax general obligation bonds, payable in not to exceed ten (10) years, to pay the cost of constructing street improvements throughout the City, consisting of paving, repaving, resurfacing, reconstructing and improving streets? If approved, the estimated millage to be levied in 2023 is 1.9946 mills ($1.99 per $1,000 of taxable value) and the estimated simple average annual millage rate required to retire the bonds is 4.0894 mills ($4.09 per $1,000 of taxable value).

WHEREAS, pursuant to the 2022 Proposal, the City previously issued its 2023 Unlimited Tax General Obligation Bonds, dated February 7, 2023, in the principal amount of $7,525,000;
