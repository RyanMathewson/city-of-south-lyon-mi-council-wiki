##  $ \underline{\text{PRESENTATION-}} $ Life saving award

Chief Unruh asked Officer Osbourne, Officer Stevens and Mr. Meinecke and Ms. Bizek to come forward. She stated on October 17th Officer Osbourne and Officer Stevens were dispatched to Quail Run for an accident. A vehicle struck a building and the driver was unconscious and the car was still in gear. Officer Stevens arrived after 3 minutes of being dispatched to the scene. He then saw the driver was unconscious and not breathing. Without hesitation, Officer Stevens broke the window to gain access to the driver. He rendered the vehicle safe, and removed the driver from the vehicle. She then stated two citizens bystanders were present and willing to help. Officer Stevens had the good Samaritans begin CPR on the man as Officer Stevens retrieved the first aid equipment from the vehicle. Officer Osbourne arrived on the scene and began assisting with CPR. Officer Stevens retrieved an automated external defibrillator from his patrol vehicle and applied it to the patient. The AED completed an assessment and advised that a shock was needed. The shock was given and the officers continued to perform CPR. The patient began breathing unassisted shortly after. Officer Stevens and Officer Osbourne continued rendering aid until they were relieved by the South Lyon Fire Department and Huron Valley Ambulance. The patient was stabilized and transported to the hospital for further treatment. She then stated Kathryn Bizek and Frederick Meinecke both heard the accident and left their homes. While Officer Stevens was retrieving the AED out of his vehicle, both Kathryn Bizek and Frederick Meinecke performed CPR on the unconscious man. Once able, the Officers took over CPR and cared for the patient. She then stated without these citizens, the man would have went without CPR while Officer Stevens was going to the car to get the AED. It's very critical during every second that CPR is being delivered or the AED is applied. It is amazing to have citizens to step forward and offer to help us. And today, she is awarding them civilian awards for life saving, because without them, it breaks the chain of live saving measures. Chief Unruh then presented them with the awards. She then presented Officer Stevens and Osbourne with Lifesaving Ribbons.

1. 2026 Resolution authorizing 2026 unlimited tax general obligation bonds

Mayor Kennedy read the explanation of topic. The resolution authorizes the issuance of second and final series of bonds for the street improvement project based on the $18,465,000 bond proposal approved by the city's voters in August 2022. The bonds will pledge the city's unlimited tax full faith and credit for support of the bonds and will be paid from the special debt millage levied to pay the debt service on the bonds. Pat McGow of Miller Canfield, who is the city's bond attorney and Steve Burke of MFCI, who is the city's financial advisor discussed the resolution. He stated we are looking to go for the sale of the bonds in 2026. We will follow the same path as we did in 2023. Mr. Burke stated their role is to be the city's fiduciary and the city has a double A+ rating and there is a high demand for the city's bonds. We will see a tremendous number of underwriters for the city's bonds. Councilmember Bogert asked if the rates will fall in the current mileage. Mr. Burke stated is we sold those today, we will be in the 4.1 – 4.2 mills range. It also has a lot to do with the city's taxable value which has grown. ## The Problem:

The City of South Lyon used a chemical called bifenthrin in our parks last year.

The U.S. EPA classifies bifenthrin as a possible human carcinogen. (9).

## What happens to bifenthrin in the environment?

Bifenthrin is not likely to reach groundwater because it binds tightly to soil. However, soil-bound bifenthrin has the potential to contaminate surface waters through runoff.

## Can bifenthrin affect birds, fish, or other wildlife?

Bifenthrin is low in toxicity to birds. There are potential risks for birds and mammals that eat aquatic organisms because bifenthrin can last a long time in the environment and it may accumulate in fish. Bifenthrin is highly toxic to fish and small aquatic organisms. It's also very highly toxic to bees. (9)

Bifenthrin is not legal for agricultural use in the European Union and Canada has restricted its use.
