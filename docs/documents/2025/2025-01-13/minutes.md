## NEW BUSINESS

### 1. Public Act 233- Clean and Renewable Energy Reduction Act

City Manager Zelenak stated we will be discussing Public Act 233, Clean and Renewable Energy Reduction Act. He stated we will discuss whether we have enough information to give clear direction to our planner and attorney to draft an ordinance pertaining to the City of South Lyon and PA233. Attorney Kolb then stated the act became effective the end of last year, and it gives local governments the opportunity to adopt ordinances to regulate wind, solar and battery storage energy facilities. She then stated it is not likely that South Lyon would be presented with a large scale solar or wind facility. She said mainly because South Lyon is very small and there isn't a lot of undeveloped land left that would support a facility that would fall under the regulation of this act. She then stated the more likely thing South Lyon could see being presented with is the battery storage system. She stated a developer could put a battery facility under the state act on a piece of property as small as one acre. However, developers tend to look at property close to transmission lines and flat already cleared land, and close to highways for easy access for maintenance. City Planner Smith stated the city already has standards for accessory solar providing the main building on site, and it references commercial solar facilities, but doesn't have any use standards. Attorney Kolb stated if Council is interested there is some opportunity to update those provisions and address the personal or very small-scale energy facilities and if there is concern about a battery storage facility. She further stated it could be attractive for the developer to go through the city as opposed to the State, and there are certain benefits for the city if you require the developer to get approval from the state and not deal with it locally, which is mainly the payment of a host fee. She then stated there was a challenge filed with MPSC from multiple communities regarding how much control it takes away from local communities. She further stated the MPSC is very industry friendly and there is no guarantee on what will come out from the complaint. Planner Smith stated there has been a lot of research done on the Act. She further stated they have drafted ordinances that align with the state act, which would be considered the CREO ordinance. She further stated they have drafted ordinances for larger scale systems that are bigger than roof top systems, but don't qualify for the act. Councilmember Hansen stated he is happy to see this brought before Council, and he, Councilmember Dilg and Mayor Kennedy attended the presentation by EGLE. He stated what he took away was by having a CREO would speed up the process for a business or developer so it doesn't get into the state's hands, and making a delay, or taking away the ownership from the city. A CREO would put the decision in the city's hands, not the states. He said he would be adopting a CREO so we have that on the books and it allows the city to have the ownership of projects in the future. He further stated we should keep in the mind the city's land locked open space, and what kind of systems we're going to be looking at. Councilmember Dilg stated she thought the workable non-CREO would be the best for the city. Attorney Kolb stated a CREO must stick with state law, any ordinance that isn't a CREO would be a workable one that the developer
