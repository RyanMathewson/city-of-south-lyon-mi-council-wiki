### 3. Residential cross connection control program

Water and Sewer Superintendent Beason stated in accordance with the requirements set forth by EGLE and part 14 of PA 399 known as the safe drinking water act, South Lyon implemented a cross-connection control program to protect the public water supply system. EGLE is more stringently enforcing the inspection of residential accounts in addition to the commercial and industrial accounts. He further stated the current program involves the inspection of 244 commercial and industrial accounts. The City of South Lyon Water Department request the hiring of HydroCorp for the purpose of performing residential cross connection inspections. He further stated the city has 3,699 residential accounts. Services provided by HydroCorp under this agreement will include inspections, surveys, program administration, answering telephone call inquiries, scheduling of inspections, program compliance review, public education materials, and preparation of cross-connection reports. Under this agreement HydroCorp will perform a minimum of 960 inspections throughout a two-year contract, which would be 480 homes per year. He further stated the contract total for two years is $94,519.20. The amount of $46,332.00 is budgeted for 2024/2025 fiscal year under line item 592-556-802 contractual services. Councilmember Kurtzweil asked why HydroCorp is their choice for the contract to waive the bidding process. Water and Sewer Superintendent Beason then explained a cross-connection is an arrangement of piping that could allow non-potable water or contaminants could enter the potable water supply and contaminate it. It could be through reversal of flow. Under normal conditions we are under pressure continually at 65-70 PSI. He stated there are two circumstances that could cause that to happen. It could be pressure in a customer's home which would exceed the pressure of our water system in the form of booster pumps, or boiler systems or some other process within that building. He then explained another way that could happen would be back siphonage which is a significant pressure loss in our water system which could be caused by a water main break, or catastrophic failure at the water plant. He then explained examples of cross-connection with the use of water and water systems.

He stated we have irrigation lawn systems which most homes have an irrigation system. He stated water powered sump pumps are a new thing and those need to be protected. He said most homes have a water softener and they have a discharge pipe and those are normally connected to a sewer line or a drain in the basement. The unprotected cross connections can cause a serious health risk and can contaminate our water system. He further stated the risks can be eliminated by a backflow preventer. It is required by the building code, but sometimes when people are moving into homes, they aren't complete, and a water softener or water powered sum pump is added later.
