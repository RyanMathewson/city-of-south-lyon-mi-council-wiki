## Rules of Conduct for Public Comment at Council Meetings $ ^{*} $

Members of the public may speak at a Council meeting upon recognition by the Mayor. Public comment may only occur during periods designated on the agenda for public comment or a public hearing. A person may speak for up to two (2) minutes during each of the two public comment periods on agenda items or non-agenda items. Waivers of the time requirement may only be granted in the discretion of the Mayor, and waivers to speak at a time other than a designated comment period may only be granted by the Council. Any person wishing to make a presentation longer than two minutes or requiring audio-visual equipment is asked to contact the City Clerk requesting to appear on a future agenda.

A person may only address Council from the podium. Only one person may occupy the podium at a time. All remarks are to be directed to the Mayor and Council. Speakers are not to engage in direct dialog with other meeting attendees.

Any person who violates the Rules of Conduct, disturbs the peace at the meeting, and/or interferes with the meeting may be warned, ordered to be seated, removed, and/or ticketed.

## PROCLAMATION

WHERAS, Douglas Baaki, a veteran Police Officer with over 30 years of service to the City of South Lyon; and

WHERAS, the South Lyon City Council along with the entire community, would like to honor the contributions that Chief Baaki has made during his law enforcement career; and

WHERAS, Chief Baaki earned a Bachelor of Arts Degree in Criminal Justice Administration from Columbia Southern University in 2016; and

WHERAS, Chief Baaki started his law enforcement career when he joined the South Lyon Police Department as a part-time officer in 1990, becoming a full-time Officer in 1993. During his first six years of service, Chief Baaki took on a variety of roles including road patrol, field training officer, detective, and data master administrator. Chief Baaki was promoted to Sergeant in September 1999, to Lieutenant in February, 2019, and became our Chief of Police in 2022; and

WHERAS, Chief Baaki has attended dozens of advance training classes including New Chief's and Executive School, Supervisor, Leadership, and Liability, Performance and Accountability, and other various instructor schools. Chief Baaki has been involved with the Lakes Area Citizen's Police Academy since 1999 where he instructed classes regarding police response to resistance and patrol operations for citizens attending the academy. Chief Baaki is a member of the Michigan Association of Chiefs of Police, Oakland County Association of Chiefs of Police, and International Association of Chiefs of Police.

NOW, THEREFORE, I, Steve Kennedy, Mayor of the City of South Lyon, and on behalf of the South Lyon City Council, hereby commend the outstanding community service performed by Douglas Baaki during his law enforcement career of over 30 years and wish him all the best in retirement.

# AGENDA NOTE Unfinished Business Item #1

MEETING DATE: June 23, 2025

PERSON PLACING ITEM ON AGENDA: Planning Consultant, CIB Planning

AGENDA TOPIC: First Amendment to the Conditional Rezoning Agreement for Westlake, located on the east side of Dixboro Road, south of Ten Mile/Lake Street, parcels #21-30-126-006, -007, -008, -009, -010 (formerly vacant parcel #21-30-126-003).

EXPLANATION OF TOPIC: In July 2024, City Council approved Elevate Land Holdings, LLC's request for a conditional rezoning and associated agreement for a portion of the property on the east side of Dixboro Road, south of Ten Mile/Lake Street, to allow 252 new multiple-family residential units, 3 single-family residential lots, and the remaining portion of the parcel to be developed with approximately 64,000 square feet of industrial development.

Following approval of the conditional rezoning, Elevate Land Holdings began the final site plan review process for the development, per the ordinance. Upon detailed design of the plans, the applicant has proposed some changes to the plans. Due to the nature of the proposed changes, an amendment to the conditional rezoning agreement is required.

The first change warranting the amendment is a modification to the wetland crossing, as a result of detailed design. As an alternative to the approved three-barrel culvert crossing, the applicant is now proposing a less invasive, full span bridge over the wetlands. Materials provided by the applicant show that the bridge crossing represents the least amount of temporary and permanent disturbance to the wetlands, compared to the original culvert design. This proposed bridge design has been submitted to and approved by EGLE. Exhibits to demonstrate the designs and impacts are included as part of your packet materials.

The second proposed change warranting the amendment is a modification to the proposed phasing, which will result in a reduced timeline for overall completion. While the original plan was approved as 3 phases, the applicant has since changed the proposed phasing to 2 phases. Phase 1 of the multiple-family development will now include construction of buildings 4-10 (144 units total), the clubhouse, and ancillary amenities. Phase 2 will include construction of buildings 11-18 (108 units total) and additional amenities. The relocation of the Lake Street entrance is now proposed with Phase 1 of the development. Phasing of the industrial portion is not proposed to change.

The change in phasing naturally changes the proposed timing of the development, as outlined in the proposed amended agreement, and allows for an earlier completion date of the total project. The original timing for the development allowed for a completion date of

<a id="p55"></a>
*[Page 55 of the original](https://www.southlyonmi.org/6-23-25%20Council%20Packet%20-%20online%20only%20with%20proclamation.pdf?t=202506200915320#page=55)* (read by OCR; check the original)

February 2033, at the latest. The updated timing allows for a completion date of August 2031, at the latest.

Additional modifications to the plans include slightly modified minimum floor areas for units and a slight modification to the number of parking spaces provided, as outlined in the proposed amended agreement.

## "Can I Recycle Food & Beverage Containers?"

## The answer is: Keep them loose, clean, and dry!

Paper, plastic, glass, and metal food containers - such as peanut butter jars, tuna cans, and even clean pizza boxes - are recyclable at curbside and at the RRRASOC recycling drop-off sites. Remember to keep them loose (e.g. don't shove your water bottles inside your cereal boxes), clean (just a light rinse will do) and dry.

Another thing to be aware of when recycling food and beverage containers is the composition of the material. If it contains both paper and plastic, is it separable? When it comes to items such as potato chip tubes, they are often made with a mix of materials that cannot be mechanically separated, thus making them nonrecyclable. Check the Recycling Directory if you have questions about which food containers can be recycled!

*[Image or figure on this page; see the original.]*

Lastly, don't forget to return your bottles and cans to your local supermarket to get your deposit back - these items get recycled afterward! It's a win-win!
