# Rules of Conduct for Public Comment at Council Meetings $ ^{*} $

Members of the public may speak at a Council meeting upon recognition by the Mayor. Public comment may only occur during periods designated on the agenda for public comment or a public hearing. A person may speak for up to two (2) minutes during each of the two public comment periods on agenda items or non-agenda items. Waivers of the time requirement may only be granted in the discretion of the Mayor, and waivers to speak at a time other than a designated comment period may only be granted by the Council. Any person wishing to make a presentation longer than two minutes or requiring audio-visual equipment is asked to contact the City Clerk requesting to appear on a future agenda.

A person may only address Council from the podium. Only one person may occupy the podium at a time. All remarks are to be directed to the Mayor and Council. Speakers are not to engage in direct dialog with other meeting attendees.

Any person who violates the Rules of Conduct, disturbs the peace at a meeting, and/or interferes with the meeting may be warned, ordered to be seated, removed, and/or ticketed.

1. Fire Department radios

Fire Chief Conrad stated the Fire Department currently has 10 operational radios available for 20 non-officer staff members. This creates a significant communication and safety gap during emergency responses, as not all personnel are equipped with a radio. The lack of adequate communication tools can hinder coordination, delay response efforts, and increase risk on scene. Acquiring 5 additional Motorola Radios will bring us closer to a 1:1 radio-to-person ratio, enhancing operational effectiveness, accountability and safety for all members during incidents. ## 15 Years of Plastic Free July!

*[Image or figure on this page; see the original.]*

This month marks 15 years of Plastic Free July! This global movement has gained a lot of traction educating others on the importance of going plastic-free with their daily activities. We're talking reusable straws, water bottles, shopping tote bags, and more! When switching to a reusable alternative, you're helping to keep both macroplastics (plastic bags, bottles, etc.) and microplastics out of our environment. That includes our parks, streets, beaches, and waterways, to name a few.

If you're new to making the switch, start with just one plastic disposable item to do away with - plastic water bottles are an easy one. Buy a reusable tumbler (there's hundreds available for cheap at your local secondhand store) and fill it up at home, at work, and on-the-go. You'd be surprised to find that many public spaces now offer bottle-refilling stations! Additionally, reusable water bottles can often be used at cafes and restaurants for drink purchases, as staff will simply fill your bottle instead of a plastic cup. This can save you the trouble of dealing with plastic lids and straws, too!

## Have a Plastic-Free Picnic!

Food brings people together, so what better way to inspire people to refuse single-use plastics than to have a plastic-free picnic? Host one with your friends or family at a local park or in your backyard and remind them to bring food that isn't wrapped in plastic. ## "Can I Recycle All Types of Glass?"

The answer is: Only certain types of glass are accepted.

Not all glass is created equally. Bottles, windows, mirrors, vases, and food storage containers can be made of different types of glass, which means each item needs to be considered before disposing of it. For example, glass bottles and jars of all colors are accepted in your curbside recycling and at the RRRASOC recycling drop-off sites. On the other hand, windows, mirrors, and food storage/baking dishes are manufactured differently and cannot be recycled the same way that bottles and jars are. If you're looking to dispose of glass items that aren't recyclable, consider how you might reuse them or if they could be reused by someone else. Donating to a local secondhand store and reselling reusable items is always a good option. Broken glass should be placed with your household trash.

*[Image or figure on this page; see the original.]*

As a general rule of thumb, remember to rinse your glass bottles and jars before recycling them. Removing food and other contaminants keeps other recyclables like paper and cardboard cleaner for recycling!

# PREVIOUSLY ANNOUNCED PONTIAC TRAIL CLOSURE IN SOUTH LYON AND LYON TOWNSHIP POSTPONED

Beverly Hills, Mich. — The Road Commission for Oakland County (RCOC) has instructed Stante Excavating to postpone its proposed and previously announced closure of Pontiac Trail between 11 Mile and Silver Lake roads in South Lyon and Lyon Twp. until after the end of the current construction season, due to conflicts with other ongoing construction projects.

The closure had previously been planned for Aug. 11 until Aug. 25 to excavate a portion of the road in conjunction with the ongoing installation of a sanitary sewer line under the road. Stante is installing the sanitary sewer on behalf of Robertson Brothers Homes, which is constructing a subdivision nearby.

Once the closure is rescheduled, potentially during the winter, the closure will be announced.
