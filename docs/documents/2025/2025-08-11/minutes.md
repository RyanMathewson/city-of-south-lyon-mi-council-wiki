### 1. Ezee Fiber Metro Act Permit

City Manager Zelenak stated the city received an application from Ezee Fiber Texas, LLC requesting a Metropolitan Extension Telecommunications right-of-way Oversite Act ("METRO Act") permit to place fiber within the city's right of way. Ezee Fiber has provided a route map indicating that they would like permission to place approximately 244,000 linear feet of underground fiber optic cable throughout the city as indicated in the route map included in the permit. The METRO Act requires the city to approve the permit unless the application is incomplete. The permit contains the standard terms provided by the state of Michigan as well as conditions the city has added in the exhibits to require timely restoration of the rights of way and notification to residents about the project. Councilmember Bogert stated that some of the streets on the map are private drives, how will that work. Attorney Kolb stated they have to get easements from the subdivision unless there are already easements in place. Councilmember Kurtzweil stated the contract talks about over lashing, but it talks about underground. City Manager Zelenak states this is in their standard agreements, and sometimes to get the wires overhead when crossing jurisdictions. Councilmember Kurtzweil stated the address and email of a person in the contract is from Texas, we should be able to get a local person to deal with. She is aware of the problems they have been having with AT&T and burying their lines, and they will be coming across 9 Mile. City Manager Zelenak stated he is sure we will have a local contact. Councilmember Kurtzweil stated there should be monitoring when the work is being done. She further stated they are going to sub-contract out and we could end up with a mess like Lyon Township has had with AT&T. She further stated why does the contract say we shall require a bond. City Manager Zelenak states some communities don't, but the city does. Mayor Kennedy stated we went through this a few years ago with Xfinity and there were some rough spots and residents called the City Manager and he would get a hold of the supervisor, and we made sure everything was taken care of. Councilmember Kivell stated we are over a barrel, we don't have anything to lean on to keep them out of here, but he hopes we can monitor them to make sure there isn't anything unreasonable going on and hope they are reasonable and caring people that want to come in and give people things the world needs now. He further stated we need to pay attention. Councilmember Dilg asked why are we making a motion if we can't say no anyway. City Manager Zelenak stated this is technically this is administrative and he could have just approved it, but he wanted Council to be aware of what will be taking place within the community. We get right-of-way permits daily that our DPW monitor and most are complaint based. He further stated this was put in place so people will have options. ## MANAGER'S REPORT

City Manager Zelenak updated everyone on the road project in Eagle Heights. He stated they have completed the installation of the first and second layer of asphalt throughout the subdivision and they have only two major adjustments and repairs to complete the job. He then stated he met with MDOT and RCOC regarding the 2026 Pontiac Trail Road project. He said they discussed bid documents, project quantities, staging, detour routes, project plans, cost estimates which is estimated to cost $11 million. The project is scheduled to be completed between March and November 2026. He then updated everyone on the RFP for waste hauling. The new contract would begin July of 2026. The RFP will go out the first week of September, and will be due by the first week of November, and the selection of the contract will be in early 2026. City Manager Zelenak stated we are working on an E-Bike ordinance which will regulate the use of Class 1, 2 & 3 bikes in the community. He expects the first draft in the next couple weeks. He then stated we are working with VC3 to increase our security for our computers and email system. He further stated Council and our employees should have been contacted by our IT personnel to set up multifactor authentication. Mayor Kennedy asked if during the meeting for the Pontiac Trail project was it mentioned when we will know our portion of the cost. City Manager Zelenak stated we are looking and 1.5 to 2 million dollars. Councilmember Dilg stated she is so concerned about our businesses during the construction, and she knows how hard it was for the Brighton businesses. City Manager Zelenak stated the businesses will have to make their own plan if they can open because there could be concerns with not having sidewalks. He further stated they will be working with the new social media manager, the DDA board and the economic development director on means to find ways to publicize and market our community whether it be for t-shirts, top 10 reasons to shop downtown South Lyon, and it is a long-term project and we know some businesses will suffer, but we will work with them and we'll have informational meetings with updates. He further stated it will be a long project. Councilmember Dilg asked if there are any considerations for moving the events. City Manager Zelenak stated we aren't cancelling our events. He further stated we will have to accommodate the events and some will be on different streets. He stated some events could be on the side streets. We will be working with each organization that's running the event and the timing on where they will be working on the road. They will be working on phases in different areas.
