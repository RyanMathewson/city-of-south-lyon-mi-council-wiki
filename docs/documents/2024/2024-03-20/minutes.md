City of South Lyon

City Council Pre-Budget Meeting

March 20, 2024

Mayor Kennedy opened the meeting at 5:00 p.m.

Mayor Kennedy led those present in the Pledge of Allegiance

ROLL CALL

Roll Call: Mayor Kennedy and Councilmembers: Bogert, Dilg, Hansen, Kivell, Mosier and Kurtzweil, Also present: City Manager Zelenak, Finance and Benefit Administrator Tiernan, and Clerk/Treasurer Deaton

## NEW BUSINESS

1. Presentation of the draft Capital Improvement/Purchase Plan

City Manager Zelenak explained the process of creating a Capital Improvement Plan from scratch. He discussed meeting with Department Heads and discussing current and future needs for the city. He further explained it is a multi-year planning instrument used to identify needs and financing sources for public infrastructure improvements. He also discussed that it is a working document that will change based on needs. The funding sources for the CIP is taxes, water and sewer funds, Act 51 funds, ARPA funds, grants, the state, RCOC bonds and other resources. He stated the expenditures expected in the next 10 years are $52 million dollars.

Mayor Kennedy stated it is a fantastic document and it sets a good framework to forecast what is needed and it is a living document that will have changes as we go along. City Manager Zelenak stated he met with the Department Heads and stated he had to look at needs versus wants and what can be afforded. Mayor Kennedy stated we have to look at it objectively, and why some things need to be done before something else needs to be done. City Manager Zelenak stated everything adds up and we need to realize that when setting the millage rates. Either that or we look at setting special assessment districts in certain areas. He stated General Fund is used for Police, Fire, DPW and some Water and Sewer expenditures. McMunn and Dorothy are federal roads, so we were able to apply for a grant for the repairs of the roads, but at some point, it all has to be funded. Councilmember Kurtzweil stated she brought this up 7 or 8 years ago. She stated the Capital Improvement Plan is a statutory requirement that past city managers didn't deal with. It is a flow chart and it is necessary for budgeting. It has to be kept up every year. She then thanked City Manager Zelenak for getting this done. Councilmember Mosier stated it was a different time 7-8 years ago. City Manager Zelenak then explained the page that reflects all vehicles owned by the city and their age and mileage to show what vehicles will need to be replaced. He then stated that the Police budget is the largest of the city's budget, but the improvements needed for water and sewer, such as meters, lead lines, and other items to keep things going is expensive as well.

Councilmember Hansen asked if they have looked at how long things will last and need to be replaced. City Manager Zelenak stated they have and Superintendent Beason met with HRC to go over things as well. He came up with a plan on how long things should last, but things do get rusty and get damaged. Some discussion was held in regards to the repairs that need to be made with the storm water and water lines underground while Pontiac Trail is being repaired. City Manager Zelenak stated it will be a major disruption to our downtown business owners while the repairs are being made. They will be affected, but we need to try to limit it as much as possible. Councilmember Kivell stated Northville is squeezing down the corridors so its self-regulating. You want people to go downtown for the services offered, not just to drive through it. City Manager Zelenak stated there will be alternate routes, such as Reynold Sweet Parkway, McMunn, Whipple and now that part of Dixboro is paved, more people will use that.
