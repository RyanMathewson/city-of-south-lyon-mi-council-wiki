Shaun Merza stated he is the owner of the Shell Gas Station at 302 S Lafayette Street. He thanked Council for allowing him to speak. He stated he has been in this business for over 20 years, and it is a great city with great people in a great community with your help with support they want to stay and conduct their businesses in town and to pay their bills, pay their taxes and feed their families. He stated he has a problem with the events that take place in downtown throughout the year. Road closures do hurt businesses and a couple times a year, it can be tolerated, but if it happens too many times, it can hurt the businesses, although they love the events. He stated he is asking if the road closures can be minimized as much as possible so it doesn't affect his business. He stated his business is brought in from people driving through town. He stated 50% of his business is from selling gasoline, so if we lose that 50% each time the road is closed, it does hurt the business, and not only that, but deliveries and they have to be rescheduled. He then stated he seems out of touch with the city, and he is asking if he can be notified 10 days before the roads will be closed so he can reschedule. He then stated he is normally notified by his neighbor Jeff Heinanen about road closures if he happens to drive by. He further stated he is asking to be notified ahead of time so conflict can be avoided.

DISCUSSION- Downtown Director Mack stated Ladies Night Out was a successful event, and they added a photo booth so people can take pictures and email them to themselves. He then thanked DPW, Police and Fire, as well as the businesses that stayed open late for the event. He then stated they ran out of gift bags, which could be a good thing or bad thing. He further stated he doesn't have the exact weight, but they were able to fill the back of a truck with donations for Active Faith. Mr. Mack then stated he's been notified that they have received the funding for the EV chargers, and they will be moving forward with that. Councilmember Dilg asked if businesses are notified when the roads will be closed, and if not, can we make that happen. Mr. Mack stated he sends out an email each month with updates on events and road closures. Councilmember Dilg asked if we could think about mapping the events out, to see if it is necessary for all the road closures or if there are too many events that need road closures. Mr. Mack stated they can look at that, and they are trying to minimize the impact to local businesses. He stated he spoke with Jeff Heinanen and they are only going to close the roads for the parade, and the tree lighting. Councilmember Kurtzweil stated some of the local businesses have their own parking lots, and their parking lots are used for events, and the owners spend the next day cleaning up trash from their parking lots. They do that on their own because they want to make sure the city does well with the events. She further stated maybe the DDA can thank them for assisting the DDA and for allowing the public to use their parking lots.

## CDBG PUBLIC HEARING

City Manager Zelenak explained that each year HUD provides funds to jurisdictions throughout the country for projects that meet their national objectives. Here in South Lyon we receive funds from HUD via Oakland County. This coming year our tentative allocation will be $27,931. Last year the city designated the funds to be used for the Senior Center and Active Faith. The 2025 CDBG application deadline for receiving CDBG funds is Friday, December 6th. On October 15th the city published notice of the CDBG public hearing which tonight gives the opportunity for hearing public comments relating to allocating those funds in South Lyon.

Mayor Kennedy opened the public hearing at 6.46 p.m.

No public comment was made.

Mayor Kennedy closed the public hearing at 6:47 p.m.

## NEW BUSINESS

1. Consider CDBG application projects for the City of South Lyon

## MANAGER'S REPORT

City Manager Zelenak stated the 2024 road improvement projects have almost been finalized. Currently they are finishing up some restoration work in Brookfield such as topsoil, grass and repairs and should be completed very soon. He then stated they have met with MERS for our annual actuarial valuation report and they will be presented to Council tat the second meeting in November. He then stated the city was contacted by the group that organized at the Wreathes Across America, and was told the volunteer that normally handles this is unable to this year. He then asked Mayor and Council if they or if they know of someone that would like to volunteer, contact the City Clerk. He then stated we had approximately 78% of our registered voters vote during the November 2024 Presidential Election. He further stated over 96% of all absentee ballots were returned. He further stated the City Clerk and staff worked numerous tireless hours and all the Election Inspectors did an excellent job in processing those. He then thanked the scouts for attending the meeting tonight and wanting to learn more on how the city operates. Mayor Kennedy stated he spoke with some residents during Ladies Night Out, and they would like the pickle ball courts to remain open as long as possible and weather permits.

## COUNCIL COMMENTS

She then reminded everyone that donations can be made to Tunnels to Tower, and it is an incredible organization that supports veterans, and helps to combat homelessness of our vets. She stated unfortunately this is something our government has not handled, and this is being handled by a private organization. She then asked everyone to support Tunnels to Towers, and they are opening a village in Detroit, which will be helping some of our homeless veterans in southeast Michigan.
