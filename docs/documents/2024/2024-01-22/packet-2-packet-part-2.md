# Memorandum

To: Paul Zelenak

From: Laura S. Amtsbuechler

Date: 1/3/2024

Re: Personnel Manual

The City of South Lyon Personnel Manual had not been recently updated, and with the assistance of both you and Patricia Tiernan, I have prepared revisions. This memo is to highlight and discuss some of the changes that have been made to address changes in the law and to update and clarify the Manual. This memo addresses the more substantive changes. There were also other changes that were made to clarify the language and to comply with other changes that have been made with City operations. The changes include changes to the terms, language, and order of topics.

The Manual recognizes the fact that some employees are members of unions and subject to collective bargaining agreements. The Manual applies to all employees, including those who are parties to a CBA, except to the extent that the CBA may have specific governing language that conflicts with the Manual. When revising the Manual, we took care to have consistency to the extent possible. We also clarified the fact that the Personnel Manual is not a contract and is not intended to create contractual rights.

Section 7 has the most substantive changes. We updated the Manual provisions regarding use of electronic devices, use of City equipment and operation of City vehicles. These revisions include prohibitions on use of electronic devices while driving and other changes intended to increase safety and to better protect the City from liability.

The Manual now contains a dress code that defines the type of clothing that is prohibited, and the standards of hygiene expected.

We also expanded the policy related to use of drugs and alcohol in the workplace. Section 7, sub paragraph R, spells out the prohibition on the use of drugs and alcohol.

# SECTION 1 - COVERAGE OF PERSONNEL MANUAL

## GENERAL COVERAGE

All full-time, part-time, temporary and seasonal employees of the City of South Lyon, except where specifically noted, are covered by the following personnel policies and procedures. In the event of conflict between these rules and existing or negotiated union contracts other employment agreements, City ordinance, or state or federal law, the terms of that contract, agreement, ordinance or law shall apply. In the event of the amendment of any ordinance, rule or law incorporated in this document or upon which these provisions rely, these rules shall be deemed amended in conformance with those changes. If union contracts are silent in any of the provisions of this policy, the City policy and procedure will prevail.

## AMENDMENTS

The City Administration may make amendments to this Policy Manual as may be required to comply with changes in the law or changes in the needs of City administration. Any amendments that impact the City budget must be approved by City Council.

## INTENT

It is the intent of the City of South Lyon, by establishing these policies and procedures, to create pleasant working conditions through fair and impartial implementation of personnel policies and procedures. Employment with the City of South Lyon is "at will," which means that employment with the City may be terminated for any reason without notice, progressive discipline or "just cause." These policies are not to be construed as a contract of employment and the policies do not create a contractual right to the provisions contained in this manual. These policies may be modified, altered, or eliminated by the City at any time without the consent of the employees.

## RESPONSIBILITY

It is the responsibility of each employee/appointee to read understand and follow these Policies. Any employee/appointee with questions as to the meaning of any part of this Policy Manual should contact the City Manager.

## SECTION 3 - HOURS OF WORK

### A.  $ \underline{\text{WORK DAY}} $

1.  $ \underline{\text{Length}} $ - For pay computation purposes, the normal City work day is eight (8) hour (excluding the lunch period) in a 24 hour period starting at 12:01 a.m.

<a id="p17"></a>
*[Page 17 of the original](https://www.southlyonmi.org/1-22-24%20Council%20Packet%20Part%202%20-%20online%20only.pdf?t=202401181518240#page=17)* (read by OCR; check the original)

2. Business Hours - While most City business offices shall be open from 8:30 a.m. to 5:00 p.m., the hours of beginning and ending of work shifts vary with the situations facing particular departments at particular times and are the responsibility of the Department Head involved, provided that no scheduled work shift shall be longer than eight hours of work (excluding the lunch period) nor less than seven and one-half hours (excluding the lunch period).

3.  $ \underline{\text{Lunch Period}} $ - No scheduled lunch period shall be longer than one hour.

4.  $ \underline{\text{Relief Periods}} $ - All employees scheduled to work a full shift shall be granted one 15 minute relief period before a one half hour lunch period and one 15 minute relief period after the lunch period. Employees may combine the two 15 minute breaks with the one half hour lunch period in order to take a one hour lunch.

5.  $ \underline{\text{Scheduling}} $ - The scheduling of shifts, lunch periods and relief periods are within the responsibility of the Department Head and shall be designed to provide the least possible disruption of the department's services and not work a hardship on the employee involved.

Department Heads shall clearly inform all employees of their working hours and the timing of lunch hours and must personally approve any exceptions to the normally scheduled routine.

### B.  $ \underline{\text{WORK WEEK}} $

The normal City work week is five days of work in a seven-day period.

### C.  $ \underline{\text{PAY PERIOD}} $

The normal City pay period is ten days of work in a fourteen-day period starting at 12:01 a.m. on the day officially designated by the City Manager as the beginning of the pay period. (Also see Section 4, "Salaries & Compensation")

### D.  $ \underline{\text{INCLEMENT WEATHER}} $

1. Only the City Manager shall have the authority to excuse employees early from their shifts, with pay, because of dangerous or inclement weather. Individual Department Heads shall not have this authority.

2. Only the City Manager shall have the authority to grant time off with pay because employee may have been prevented from getting to work because of weather. In such cases, where possible, all employees on the same shift at the affected work locations shall be treated alike.

# SECTION 4- SALARIES & COMPENSATION

### A.  $ \underline{\text{SALARIES}} $

Pursuant to Section 6.2(a) of the City Charter of the City of South Lyon, the City Manager shall set the compensation for all employees in accordance with budget appropriations.

### B.  $ \underline{\text{SALARY RANGES}} $

1. The salary set for a position shall be called the salary range for that position, whether the salary consists of a single rate or a series of salary rates.

2. The salary rate shall not include Service Increment Pay or night shift differential.

### C.  $ \underline{\text{STARTING SALARY RATE}} $

The salary rate at which an employee begins employment in a given position shall be the base rate of the class, except:

1. When the employee enters the new class by way of promotion or transfer from another City position with a higher salary than the base rate of the new position.

2. When the employee possesses recent directly comparable work experience or academic qualifications which exceed the minimum requirements for the position.

3. When there is an acute shortage of qualified applicants for the position.

### D.  $ \underline{\text{SALARY INCREMENTS}} $

Progression through the salary range to the top of the scale will be based on the recommendation of the Department Head with the concurrence of the City Manager.

### U. AMERICANS WITH DISABILITIES ACT

The federal Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA) and state law prohibit discrimination against qualified individuals with disabilities in job application procedures, hiring, firing, advancement, compensation, job training and other terms, conditions and privileges of employment. The ADA does not alter the City's right to hire the best-qualified applicant, but it does prohibit discrimination against a qualified applicant or employee because of his or her disability, or because of a perceived disability. As a matter of City policy, the City prohibits discrimination of any kind against people with disabilities.

An applicant or employee is considered disabled if he or she (1) has a physical or mental impairment that substantially limits one or more major life activities; (2) has a record or past history of such an impairment; or (3) is regarded or perceived (correctly or incorrectly) as having such impairment.

A qualified employee or applicant with a disability is an individual who satisfies the requisite skill, experience, education and other job-related requirements of the position held or desired, and who, with or without reasonable accommodation, can perform the essential functions of that position.

Reasonable accommodation may include any change in the work environment (or in the way things are usually done) to help a person with a disability apply for a job, perform the duties of a job, or enjoy the benefits and privileges of employment.

Qualified applicants or employees who are disabled should request reasonable accommodation from the City in order to allow them to perform a particular job. If you are disabled and you desire such reasonable accommodation, contact your immediate supervisor, Department Head, or the City Manager. On receipt of your request, we will meet with you to discuss your disability.

We may ask for information from your health care provider(s) regarding the nature of your disability and the nature of your limitations or take other steps necessary to help us determine viable options for reasonable accommodation. We will then work with you to determine whether your disability can be reasonably accommodated, and if it can be accommodated, we will explore alternatives with you.

## SECTION 9 – EMPLOYMENT OF RELATIVES

## NEPOTISM AND PERSONAL RELATIONSHIP POLICY

South Lyon prohibits employment of a relative into a position where the employee and supervisor are related. This prohibition includes direct and indirect supervision. For purposes of this provisions, "relative" includes: spouses, domestics partners, siblings, parents, children, grandparents, grandchildren and the "step" or "in law" version of each.

South Lyon prohibits romantic or sexual relationships between a supervisory employee and an employee who reports directly or indirectly to that person because such relationships tend to create compromising conflicts of interest or the appearance of such conflicts. In addition, such a relationship may give rise to the perception by others that

<a id="p48"></a>
*[Page 48 of the original](https://www.southlyonmi.org/1-22-24%20Council%20Packet%20Part%202%20-%20online%20only.pdf?t=202401181518240#page=48)* (read by OCR; check the original)

there is favoritism or bias in employment decisions affecting the staff employee. Moreover, given the uneven balance of power within such relationships, consent by a staff member is suspect and may be viewed by others, or at a later date by the staff member, as having been given as the result of coercion or intimidation. The atmosphere created by such may be one where there is an appearance of bias, favoritism, intimidation, coercion or exploitation, which undermines the spirit of trust and mutual respect that is essential to a healthy work environment. If there is such a relationship, the parties need to be aware that one or both may be moved to a different department or other actions may be taken.

If any employee of South Lyon enters into a consensual relationship that is romantic or sexual in nature with an employee who reports directly or indirectly to that employee, or if one of the parties is in a supervisory capacity in the same department in which the other party works, the parties must notify the City Manager or the designated Human Resources officer. Because of potential issues regarding quid pro quo harassment, South Lyon has made reporting mandatory. This requirement does not apply to employees who do not work in the same department or parties where neither one supervises or otherwise manages responsibilities over the other.

Once such a relationship is made known to South Lyon, the City Manager will review the situation light of all the facts (reporting relationship between the parties, effect on coworkers, job titles of the parties, etc.) and will determine whether one or both parties need to be moved to another job or department. If it is determined that one party must be moved and there are jobs in other departments available for both, the parties may decide who will be the one to apply for a new position. If the parties cannot amicable come to a decision, or the party is not chosen for the position to which he or she applied, the City Manager, in consultation with the City's designated Human Resources officer will decide which party will be moved. That decision will be based on which move will be least disruptive to the organization as a whole. If no other jobs are available for either party, the parties will be given the option of terminating their relationship or resigning.

The City will not hire elected official's relatives, as defined above, during the elected official's term of office.

Due to the uniqueness of the paid on-call fire services, the Fire Department is exempt from the nepotism policy. Furthermore, given the uniqueness of the election inspectors' and crossing guard's work, all election inspectors and crossing guards are also exempt.

## SECTION 10 – DISCIPLINARY ACTIONS

Disciplinary action may be taken for misconduct or failure to perform job duties, including but not limited to employee violation of this Manual, the Code of Conduct or violation of departmental policies.

The following is to serve as an explanation of different types of discipline. It is not intended to require progressive discipline. The types of disciplinary action may be implemented in any order and at the discretion of the Department Head or City Manager, depending on the severity of the offense.

### A.  $ \underline{\text{VERBAL REPRIMAND}} $

This is an action taken by a Department Head where he/she tells an employee about an action or behavior of the employee which he/she, as the Department Head, finds objectionable or wishes corrected. The verbal reprimand may be documented in writing.

### B.  $ \underline{\text{WRITTEN REPRIMAND}} $

This is an action taken by a Department Head in which he/she places in writing the action or behavior, which he/she wishes the employee to change, cease or begin. The written reprimand will describe in detail the behavior to be corrected and will give direct and concrete orders for the future and point out the consequences of repeating the actions which brought about the written reprimand.

### C.  $ \underline{\text{SUSPENSION WITHOUT PAY}} $

This is an action taken by a Department Head, which removes an employee from employment in his/her department and from the City payroll for a definite period of time.

1. This action does not require the employee's consent to place him/her on such leave without pay.

2. The employee does not accrue salary, Annual Leave credit or Sick Days credit during the time he/she is suspended, nor can he/she apply such time to the period of suspension.

### D.  $ \underline{\text{DEMOTION}} $

This is an action taken by a Department Head which reduces an employee's classification to a classification with a lower maximum salary.

1. The employee's salary must be reduced to fall within the range of the new classification but to no lower step designation than the one held in the

<a id="p50"></a>

higher classification unless the new classification has fewer salary steps, in which case the employee should be placed at the highest step.

2. The employee's duties and responsibilities must be reduced to those of the lower classification.

3. The reason for the demotion must be given.

### E.  $ \underline{\text{DISMISSAL}} $

This is an action taken by a Department Head, approved by the City Manager, or taken by the City Manager, which permanently removes an employee from employment in his/her department and from the City payroll.

1. Dismissed employees need not be kept in employment or be paid for any time after the completion of their normal working day on the date they are dismissed.

2. Dismissed employees shall be treated the same as employees separated for reasons other than retirement, in the matters of paying for unused accumulated annual leave, compensatory time and unused sick leave accumulation.

### F.  $ \underline{\text{NOTIFICATION OF WRITTEN REPRIMAND, SUSPENSION WITHOUT PAY, DEMOTION, OR DISMISSAL}} $

A written notice of action that clearly states the disciplinary action and the effective dates and conditions of the action, which must be signed by the Department Head or his/her authorized subordinate will be presented to the employee in person by the Department Head or his/her authorized subordinate, or else sent to the employee's last known address by registered mail, restricted delivery, with return receipt requested, on/or prior to the effective date of action.

If the written notice is presented in person, the employee should sign all copies to acknowledge receipt. If he/she refuses, the presenter should note it on the form.

### G.  $ \underline{\text{DISCIPLINARY ACTION}} $

An employee shall receive disciplinary action, whether an oral reprimand, a written reprimand, a suspension without pay, a demotion, or a dismissal, for a specific, clearly described reason. The written notice of action provided to the employee shall specify and identify with particularity the specific reason or reasons for disciplinary action.

Please note, however, that the City reserves the right to modify this procedure at any time and nothing in this procedure should be construed to constitute a contract between you and the City or to constitute any part of a contract between you and the City.

Any employment related dispute between you and the City may be resolved using this grievance procedure, with the exception of verbal reprimands.

A grievance is a difference between the Employer and any Employee which concerns (1) discipline, or (2) the interpretation or application or any provision of personnel rules and regulations. Interpretation grievances may be processed directly to Step Two (2) of the Grievance Procedure.

The time elements in the Steps can be shortened or extended by mutual written agreement.

For the purpose of processing grievances, working days shall be defined as Monday through Friday, excluding all paid holidays.

Any employee grievance not presented for disposition through the Grievance Procedure within five (5) working days of the date of the occurrence giving rise to the grievance, or within five (5) working days of the date it is reasonable to assume that the employee first became aware of the conditions giving rise to the grievance, unless the circumstances made it impossible for the employee to know prior to that date that there were grounds for such a claim, shall not be considered or processed as a grievance under this policy.

## SECTION 16 – LEAVES OF ABSENCE WITHOUT PAY

### A.  $ \underline{\text{DEFINITION OF LEAVE OF ABSENCE WITHOUT PAY}} $

1. A Leave of Absence Without Pay is a predetermined amount of time off from work, without pay, which has been recommended by the employee's Department Head. All time, for which a full time City employee is to be continued as an employee, but not paid, should be considered as a Leave of Absence Without Pay, whether it be one day or the maximum time allowable under the reason for the leave.

2. With the exception of covering time off from work for an employee with a Family Medical Leave Act qualifying condition, or other Leave without pay required by law, the fact that a Leave is possible under these regulations does not mean that the requested leave must be granted. A Leave of Absence deprives the employee's department of the services of an employee, who it is assumed is needed if the department is to properly do its job. Leave of Absence Without Pay, except in the case of disciplinary leave, should be considered as a privilege and the best interests of the department and the City service must be the determining factors in whether such leave is granted or not.

3. When an employee is granted a Leave of Absence Without Pay, the Department Head commits him/herself to allowing the employee to return to work at the end of the leave to the same duties and the same salary that the employee was performing and earning when he/she went on leave. Any substitutes hired to fill in for the employees on Leave of Absence Without Pay, should be hired to fill in for employees on Leave of Absence Without Pay, should be hired accordingly.

4. When granted a Leave of Absence Without Pay, the employee commits him/herself to returning to work at the end of the Leave.

## SECTION 18- FAMILY AND MEDICAL LEAVE

The federal Family and Medical Leave Act (FMLA) allows certain employees to take up to 12 weeks of unpaid leave per year for the serious health condition of the employee or an immediate family member, or for childbirth or adoption. An employee who assumes the role of caring for a child is also entitled to receive parental rights to family leave, regardless of the legal or biological relationship. Either day-to-day care or financial support may establish a parental relationship when the employee intends to assume the responsibilities of a parent with regard to a child.

An employee of the City is eligible for leave under the Family and Medical Leave Act ("FMLA") if he/she: (a) has worked for the City for at least a total of twelve (12) months; and (b) has worked at least 1,250 hours over the twelve (12) month period immediately preceding the employee's request for leave or the date on which the leave commences, whichever comes first.

## Leave Entitlement

Upon request, an eligible employee will be granted up to a total of twelve (12) workweeks of unpaid leave during a twelve (12) month period for one or more of the following reasons:

For the birth of a child, or placement of a child for adoption or foster care.

- To care for an immediate family member (spouse, child, or parent) with a serious health condition; or

Because of a serious health condition of the employee which renders him/her unable to perform the functions of his/her position.

The City shall calculate the twelve (12) month period during which the employee is entitled to FMLA leave by a "rolling year". Each request for FMLA leave will be evaluated based on the amount of the FMLA leave taken in the twelve (12) month period prior to the start of the new requested leave.

Spouses employed by the City are entitled to a combined total of twelve (12) work-weeks of family leave for the birth or placement of a child for adoption or foster care, and to care for a parent (but not a parent-in-law) who has a serious health condition. Each employee may use up to twelve (12) workweeks of unpaid leave during any twelve (12) month period to care for a child or spouse who is suffering from a serious health condition.

Leave for birth or adoption (including foster care placement) must conclude within twelve (12) months of the birth or placement.

To take FMLA leave, you must provide the City with appropriate notice. If you know in advance that you will need FMLA leave, you must notify the Finance and Benefits Administrator at least 30 days in advance. If you learn of your need for leave less than 30 days in advance, you must give notice as soon as you can (generally either the day you learn of the need or the next workday). When you need FMLA leave unexpectedly (for example, if a family member is injured in an accident), you must inform the Finance and Benefits Administrator as soon as you can.

# Non-Homestead Millage Renewal & Restoration Election 2024 FAQ's

## What is a school district operating millage?

As a result of Proposal A, the statewide school finance reform that voters passed in 1994, the vast majority of Michigan school districts must levy an 18 mills local property tax on non-homestead property in order to receive their full per-pupil funding. The operating millage provides critical funds to support the day-to-day operations of schools, including but not limited to staffing costs, instructional programs, instructional materials, transportation and maintenance. This money cannot credibly be replaced by other sources.

## What is a mill?

A mill is $1.00 for every $1,000 of taxable valuation on property. Example: If a commercial property has a $100,000 taxable value, then the 18 mills operating millage would cost $1,800 on that property.

## How does this operating millage impact the funding that the district receives?

Per pupil funding in the State of Michigan is a combination of state and local funds. The South Lyon Community School District's per pupil funding is $9,608 per student.

## Total State Per Pupil Funding $ 9,608

## What is a non-homestead property?

A non-homestead property is any property that is not an owned primary residence. Examples of non-homestead properties include vacation homes, industrial, commercial, business, investment and rental properties.

## Will my primary home property taxes go up if this passes?

No. Your primary home property tax rate would be unaffected because the non-homestead tax does not apply to primary residences. A person's owned principal residence (and other qualified properties) is exempted by law from non-homestead taxation.

Do businesses and commercial entities pay this operating tax in other school districts?

Yes. Regardless of which district a business may be located within Michigan, a school district must levy the operating tax in order for it to receive its full funding from the State of Michigan.

### Why are you asking for 19.2151 mills for non-homestead properties if you are only allowed to levy 18 mills?

The 19.2151 (current mills 18.7151 plus .50 mill increase = 19.2151 total) mills includes an extra 1.2151 mills to allow the district to offset future shortages. The district will never be able to collect more than 18 mills, but the extra 1.2151 mills is expected to prevent the district from taking the issue back to the voters within the proposal's 10-year period.

## How many years will the operating millages be renewed for?

The millage renewal is for 10 years, from 2025 through 2034.

How much money does this millage generate for the operations of the school district?

If renewed, this operating millage would maintain an estimated $15 million for the operations of the school district in 2025. These funds allow the district to retain our teachers and staff members, as well as offer services such as transportation, extracurricular activities, and core academic programs.

## How much is the current General Fund operating budget?

The 2023-24 General Fund budget is approximately $116 million. The operating renewal would generate approximately 13% of the operating budget. Most funding for our school comes from the State of Michigan, but we still greatly rely on the operating millage to fund our operation.

## If the operating millage renewal does not pass, how will it affect the operations of the school district?

If the operating millage renewal fails, every operation of the school district would be closely analyzed in order to cut from the future budget. The district relies on these funds because we can use them to fund services and our staff unlike bond dollars which can only be used on facilities.

## What is a Headlee Rollback?

In 1978, Michigan voters approved the "Headlee" tax limitation amendment to the Michigan Constitution. It requires local units of government, including school districts, to reduce millages when annual property values increase greater than the rate of inflation.

## How can South Lyon Community School District avoid a Headlee Rollback?

Districts cannot avoid a Headlee Rollback. However, in an effort to combat the effects of a Headlee Rollback, school districts can seek voter authorization for more than the 18 mills levy and/or a shorter authorization period. South Lyon Community School District is asking voters to consider 19.2151 mills over a 10-year period. If approved, 19.2151 mills allow the additional 1.2151 mills to offset up to 1.2151 mills worth of Headlee Rollbacks over the next decade to keep the millage at 18 mills or as close as possible if the rollbacks exceed 1 mill. (Note: This does not allow a school district to levy more than 18 mills at any time.)
