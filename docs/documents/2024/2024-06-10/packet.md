# Rules of Conduct for Public Comment at Council Meetings $ ^{*} $

Members of the public may speak at a Council meeting upon recognition by the Mayor. Public comment may only occur during periods designated on the agenda for public comment or a public hearing. A person may speak for up to two (2) minutes during each of the two public comment periods on agenda items or non-agenda items. Waivers of the time requirement may only be granted in the discretion of the Mayor, and waivers to speak at a time other than a designated comment period may only be granted by the Council. Any person wishing to make a presentation longer than two minutes or requiring audio-visual equipment is asked to contact the City Clerk requesting to appear on a future agenda.

A person may only address Council from the podium. Only one person may occupy the podium at a time. All remarks are to be directed to the Mayor and Council. Speakers are not to engage in direct dialog with other meeting attendees.

Any person who violates the Rules of Conduct, disturbs the peace at the meeting, and/or interferes with the meeting may be warned, ordered to be seated, removed, and/or ticketed.

Planning and Zoning Director Hannah Smith states that this is the first reading for the conditional rezoning of the parcel located at 26800 Pontiac Trail. The property is currently zoned R1A, single family district and is currently Suzi's Bar and Grill. The applicant is asking to conditionally rezone to the O, Office District for the construction of 2 office/commercial buildings. Smith reminds Council that a conditional rezoning is different from a traditional rezoning. It entails a conditional rezoning agreement, that if approved is entered in to by the city and the applicant and outlines the terms of the rezoning, including the voluntarily offered conditions, time limits of the project and other terms. In addition, a conditional rezoning has a plan tied to it, which if the rezoning is approved, no other use or site plan development can occur other than the plan that is attached. The City Attorney and the applicant team have been working together to prepare a rezoning draft agreement which is included in the Council's packet. She states that the agreement is included verbatim in the ordinance document that you are considering tonight, but the council will approve the agreement itself separately at a later date if the first reading is approved tonight. Smith states that listed in the draft agreement in our review letter are the conditions being offered by the applicant. They are offering as a condition of the rezoning a list of uses permitted in the O District that the property will not be developed with, these include: restaurants and bars, banquet and conference centers, private clubs, hospitals, animal boarding establishments, funeral homes and mortuaries, and hotels and motels.

Smith states that attached to the request and the agreement right now is a concept plan, which shows the general building layout, parking, building design and other general details. If the rezoning is approved, the applicant will have to come back for final site plan approval, with a full detailed site plan showing all required information, which would go before the Planning Commission for approval. The final site plan is required to be in substantial conformance with the concept plan and as noted, the development of the

## TO LOCAL POLITICAL SUBDIVISIONS: THIS NOTICE CONTAINS IMPORTANT INFORMATION ABOUT A NEW NATIONAL OPIOID SETTLEMENT

### KROGER CO. SETTLEMENT OVERVIEW

A proposed nationwide settlement agreement ("Settlement") has been reached that would resolve the legal claims of states and local political subdivisions against regional supermarket pharmacy Kroger Co. related to alleged misconduct related to opioids.

The Settlement requires Kroger Co. to pay over a billion dollars to abate the opioid epidemic. Of this amount, approximately $1.2 billion will be used by participating states and subdivisions to remediate and abate the impacts of the opioid crisis. Depending on participation by states and subdivisions, the Settlement requires payments over eleven years after its effective date.

The proposed settlement has two key participation steps.

The Settlement also contains injunctive relief governing opioid dispensing practices and requires Kroger Co. to implement safeguards to prevent diversion of prescription opioids.

First, each eligible state decides whether to participate in each Settlement. A list of participating states for each settlement can be found at https://nationalopioidsettlement.com.

Second, eligible subdivisions within each participating state decide whether to participate in the Settlement. The more subdivisions that participate, the more funds flow to that state and its subdivisions. Any subdivision that does not participate cannot directly share in any of the settlement funds, even if the subdivision's state is settling and other participating subdivisions are sharing in settlement funds. If the state does not participate, the subdivisions in that state are not eligible to participate in the Settlement.

### WHO IS RUBRIS INC. AND WHAT IS THE IMPLEMENTATION ADMINISTRATOR?

The Settlement provides that an Implementation Administrator will provide notice and manage the collection of participation forms. Rubris Inc. is the Implementation Administrator for this new Settlement and was also retained for the prior national opioid settlements.

AGENDA TOPIC: The agenda topic contains two parts as follows:

Second Reading of Ordinance to Amend the Official Zoning Map of the City of South Lyon to conditionally rezone the 3.73-acre parcel commonly known as 26800 Pontiac Trail, parcel #21-17-301-030, from R1-A, One-Family Residential, to O, Office, District.

Conditional Rezoning Agreement between the Trail Building, LLC, SK Realty LLC, and the City of South Lyon for the parcel commonly known as 26800 Pontiac Trail, parcel #21-17-301-030.

EXPLANATION OF TOPIC: Neil Ganshorn – Rand Construction Engineering Inc. has petitioned the City and requested a Conditional Rezoning of the 3.73-acre parcel at 26800 Pontiac Trail, parcel #21-17-301-030, to allow the development of two office/commercial buildings. The petitioner's request is pursuant to MCL 125.3405, which authorizes rezoning with conditions voluntarily offered by the owner of the property.

A conditional rezoning agreement has been prepared by the City Attorney and applicant attorney, which requires approval with the second reading of the ordinance. The application includes a Concept Plan, which is incorporated as an exhibit of the conditional rezoning agreement. If the rezoning is approved, the applicant would be required to submit for final site plan review and approval, which must be consistent with the concept plan included in the agreement.

The conditions voluntarily offered by the applicant are listed under Paragraph 2 of the conditional rezoning agreement.

If the conditional rezoning request is approved, the development and use of the property must be in conformance with the concept plan approved as an exhibit of the agreement, and must be in compliance with the conditions of the rezoning as written in the conditional rezoning agreement.

The process of the conditional rezoning is a four-step process:

1. Public hearing and review of the conditional rezoning request and concept plan by the Planning Commission with a recommendation to City Council;

2. Review and action by the City Council on the conditional rezoning request. If City Council approves, first reading of the Zoning Ordinance amendment;

3. Second reading of the Zoning Ordinance amendment and final approval of the Conditional Rezoning Agreement by City Council;

Review and action on final site plan by the Planning Commission.

No other use or  $ \underline{\text{site plan development}} $ may occur on a conditionally rezoned parcel other than those agreed upon within the Conditional Rezoning Agreement and shown in the concept plan, which is approved as part of the agreement. While the plan attached to the agreement is a concept plan, the agreement requires that the applicant submit for full site plan review and approval following approval of the rezoning and agreement.

The concept plan approval provides general acceptance of the building layout and sizes, parking layout, preliminary landscaping, and building designs. After a public hearing has been held for the Conditional Rezoning (including the concept plan), a recommendation from the Planning Commission to the City Council is made. If the Conditional Rezoning and corresponding concept plan are approved by the City Council, the applicant will return to the Planning Commission for the full site plan review and approval. All of the requirements for site plan submissions in Article IV of the Zoning Ordinance must be complied with at that time.
