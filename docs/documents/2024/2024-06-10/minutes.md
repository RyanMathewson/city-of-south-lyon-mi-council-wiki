##  $ \underline{\text{DISCUSSION-}} $ Downtown

DDA Director Mack stated the Food Truck Rally at the Farmers Market went really well and the vendors were very happy. He stated the road was shut down so people could walk around comfortably. He also stated that all the food trucks sold out except for one. He further stated they are looking at potentially doing another food truck rally in August, it will be discussed at the DDA meeting on Thursday. They will be approving the bike racks at 6 locations. He further stated he and the Planner met with the building owner at 228 S Lafayette Street to discuss what is allowed for window coverings on the front of their building. Councilmember Hansen stated he was pleased to see movement on the vehicle chargers. He asked if there is any additional news. Mr. Mack stated 3 of the 5 sites can be done, but 2 of the 5 will take time due to infrastructure being needed at 390 S Lafayette. Councilmember Bogert asked if he found a resolution for the problem with the trash during the food truck rally. Mr. Mack stated there were some issues with GFL and they were contacted and we'll keep a better eye on that. Councilmember Mosier asked if there has been any thought to having a food truck rally each month of the Farmers Market in the future. There was a huge interest in it. Mr. Mack stated they will definitely look into it. Councilmember Kivell asked if they have already decided what size racks they will put in, or is it decided by location. Mr. Mack stated specific to locations, one is an inverted U, and there are bigger ones at the market location, and some decorative ones for another location.

## POLICE CHIEF REPORT

Chief Baaki stated they conducted an oral board with a potential new officer and we gave him a potential offer pending the background check, so he hopes to have another officer soon. He then stated they did some recruiting at Oakland Police Academy, and they spoke with some service people that are in the pre-academy, and they are hoping to get some interest from them as well. Chief Baaki then stated Grand Valley reached out to them with some potential interests. He further stated they will reach out to Macomb in July to do other recruiting sessions. He then stated MCOLES is now going around the state assessing departments and there were 12 in Oakland County. He further stated they are looking at the empowerment paperwork when we bring people on and the separation paperwork when people leave, and background checks. He further stated they haven't done this in 7 years and we are the 6th in Michigan and everything went well with the assessor. He stated there are a few things that need to be cleaned up, but things went mostly well, they were happy with the hiring and the background checks. He further stated he was pleased and he credits that to the accreditation they did with all their paperwork and policies. Councilmember Bogert stated he has seen a lot of complaints about trucks making left turns downtown. Chief Baaki stated he has had an officer sit there and has pulled over a couple. He further stated they do monitor it but it will always happen, but they will continue to monitor it and stop them and cite them if need be. Councilmember Bogert stated he spoke with the crossing guard Randy, and he said there were a couple of times people are turning on red. Chief Baaki stated he will keep an eye on that and he will talk with Randy as well and they will keep an eye on it. He further stated during school time, the officers are at the school, but we will keep an eye on that as well.

### 3. Resolution to authorize EGLE access to 390 S Lafayette

City Manager Zelenak stated the city has received a letter from EGLE a part 213 consent to enter private property form to access 390 S Lafayette to perform an environmental investigation to determine if contaminants from the former Clark gas station at 302 S Lafayette Street have migrated. Councilmember Kurtzweil asked what prompted the letter, was there a reported release? City Manager Zelenak stated there was not a reported release, this is a normal process when there was previously a gas station. Councilmember Kurtzweil asked if there is leakage onto the property, how would EGLE know if it was from the previous owner, or the current owner. City Manager Zelenak stated they would make that determination based upon the chemicals that are in the gas, there were certain chemicals that were used during certain dates. Attorney Hamameh pointed out a report in the packet that in September 2020, there was an administrative order of consent and then a correction order that all the companies have to do, and then the inspections to ensure there was no migration. Further discussion was held regarding the possibility of a leech.

### 4. Discussion- city energy audit and possible action plan

Councilmember Hansen stated he wanted to gauge Council's interest on this and he and Councilmember Kivell have been discussing this. He stated they have met with a few companies that help municipalities apply for grants, explore carbon neutral options, carbon plans to reduce greenhouse gases as well as to lessen the carbon footprint of the municipality. He stated the discussion they have had were about solar charging stations, solar car ports, maybe adding solar to the fire stations, but without knowing how much money it would cost to find out, it wouldn't be so much to spend that much money not knowing what impact it would be having. He further stated on energy bills, and will there be a decent enough impact to warrant the cost. He stated they have had several conversations with a couple of companies and come to

<a id="p6"></a>
*[Page 6 of the original](https://www.southlyonmi.org/6-10-24%20CM%20Approved.pdf?t=202406251115310#page=6)* (read by OCR; check the original)

find out the method that we would need to do is to analyze the city's usage with an energy audit. After that, we could have a study done for a climate action plan. A feasibility study on reducing our carbon emissions to zero. He further stated there is a grant currently available, due on June 30th, which we probably wouldn't have time to do. He then stated there are other energy related projects with grants up to $100,000 which could cover the cost of the energy action plan. Councilmember Kivell stated taking on something big like the water treatment plant would be substantial, but he would like the city to get a plan and figure out what our energy use is, and to see what our best interest is and where we can get the best bang for the time to find the most out of solar arrays and get away from gas and electric. He further stated there is a lot of money that is available and he would like the city to take it seriously and see what would be the right scale for us. City Manager Zelenak stated a few years ago, the city was working with HRC and looking at the energy use at the wastewater plant, and looking at possibly using solar to power the building, but it wasn't feasible at the time for a building of that size, not only due to the size, but the cost of the equipment, and the area that would be needed. He further stated he emailed with our water department staff and engineering firm for getting information of what was looked at to see if some of the things could be done on a smaller scale. He stated they have been looking at the square footage of our buildings. He stated the city owns 50,000-75,00 square foot in buildings, and the cost is .50-.55 per square footage, so there would be an expenditure of $25,000 to $30,000 to have an energy audit done. He then stated there are certain requirements for grants and they look for specific information before any money would be rewarded. He stated if you already had the energy audit done, you could potentially receive some money. He further stated there is some funds available to perform the energy audit, but you have to allocate the funds, then get reimbursed. He stated there are other requirements as well such as RFPs in order to do some of the things, and for the city to just hire one company to hurry up and prepare the RFP, or the grant information and he requested one company to let him know how much it will cost, and staff time needed. He stated it is a good idea to keep looking at this, and this year we haven't allocated any money in the budget for the upcoming budget. Councilmember Kurtzweil stated she is in favor of this and for the simple reason if we are going to engage in policy making, we need to have the facts in front of us. She further stated we should look at having the audit done and then we can move forward to making a policy. City Manager Zelenak stated we have to look at what is involved with the level 2 audit. Mayor Kennedy stated typically when we have items on the agenda, we have some background information ahead of time, such as the cost, and the return of the investment, what areas are acceptable for an energy audit. There is no money allocated in the upcoming 2 budget years. This is a good idea to look at in the future. Councilmember Kivell stated they were looking to see if council was interested in pursuing this. He further stated there is a lot of opportunities out there and our world is growing like crazy and he hopes we don't miss anything because we didn't get engaged. Mayor Kennedy stated that what most companies would do is provide the city with case studies to show what other communities have done and what their savings ended up being. He would like that type of information. Councilmember Kurtzweil asked if we have the information now, or are we at the very beginning. City Manager Zelenak stated we aren't at square one, but it has just been introduced and we discussed energy uses in buildings, and working with utility companies. He further stated when one company approaches councilmembers, we need to look at other companies as well, and look at the case studies to see what other communities have done. Councilmember Kurtzweil stated she would like to see the plan and compare bids to see what fits. She further stated we would get some bids, and she wants to see their plan. City Manager Zelenak stated it is premature to go out for bids until we have more information on the energy audit. Part of this is getting educated from other companies and he proposes getting staff and council educated to see what our options are.
