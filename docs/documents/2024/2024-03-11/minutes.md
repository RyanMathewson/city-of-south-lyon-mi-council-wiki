Suzann Martin, six zero two West Lake Street - Regarding the West Lake Planned Urban Development, only a handful of residents were notified about this project. Residents with homes within 50 feet of the property. If all the residents of the city were notified, there would be more in opposition to the wetland encroachments.

I am opposed to building on the wetlands - three homes and a boulevard are planned.

I inquired to Kris Olsson, Wetland Specialist and Watershed Ecologist, with the Huron River Watershed Council, about the ten acres of wetlands on this property owned by another party. We discussed the following important message:

"If there are feasible alternatives to going through the wetlands, the Huron River Watershed Council's position is to maintain the Huron River Watershed's health, to encourage higher density where built infrastructure already exists, and preserve natural areas where "Green Infrastructure" exists so it can continue to provide ecological services necessary to maintain quality of water, air, land, and life. Examples of green infrastructure are wetlands, forests, creeks, and lakes.

The Huron River Watershed Council has a general rule, the biggest focus is to make sure the development is built in a way that is protective of water quality and natural features. So, if a development is going in, they would recommend good storm water management and protection of existing wetlands and forests, so that polluted runoff does not affect waterways. Also important would be a setback from the creek or any other waterways or wetlands to protect those important features. Higher and taller density is better from a storm water perspective, since the amount of impervious surfaces created per household is minimized, yet accommodates more people, which would relieve development pressure on outlying rural areas. While development in a city is generally appropriate,  $ \underline{\text{it is never appropriate to destroy wetlands and other natural features like riparian buffers and mature forested areas}} $. Wetlands are especially important to protect in urban areas for their ability to soak up runoff and provide habitat to what wildlife has remained in the city."

Some of Kris Olsson's, and the Huron River Watershed Council, points were to avoid damage to any wetlands and preserve as many trees and as much of the natural topography as possible and group the built areas together to minimize paving and destruction of natural areas. Protect natural features like woods, wetlands, steep slopes, and waterways, and control and treat storm water.

Wetlands in themselves are a threatened waterbody in the United States of America due to development.

Emergent species are: Narrow Leaved Cattails, Canary Gross, Sensitive Fern, Skunk Cabbage and Spotted Touch Me Nots. These were found on and around the wetlands.
