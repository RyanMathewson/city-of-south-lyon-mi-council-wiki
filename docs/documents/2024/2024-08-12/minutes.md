## ELECTION REPORT

Clerk Deaton stated in the City of South Lyon, we had 82 people that participated in the 9 days early voting. Our cost of our Election Inspectors wages for that time period is $5,000.00. The 9 days early voting is mandated through the proposal 22-2 which states all jurisdictions must offer 9 days of early voting for all State and General Elections. When the grant is available she will be applying for reimbursement for the wages of our inspectors, as well as the overtime of our Deputy Clerk and Administrative Assistant. We are confident the 9 days early voting will be utilized at a much higher rate for the November Election. The absentee ballots had a good return rate. We issued 2,481 and received 1,686 which is a 67.96% return rate. The average in-person voter turnout rate was 23.10%. She stated she wants to thank all of the Chairpersons and Inspectors, the office staff at city hall, as well as the DPW. Everyone did a great job working as a team, as always. Councilmember Hansen stated he spoke with some of the poll workers, and asked if there is a way to educate the public about getting out to vote ahead of time. Clerk Deaton stated we add a notice on their water bills, it's on the Downtown Facebook page, its posted at City Hall, it's posted on our website, as well as Instagram. She further stated they were posted on each of the dividers on everyone's desk for the entire month of July because we have a lot of people that come in to pay their water bills. She further stated they are doing the best they can to let people know, and it is new, but it will get better as we move along. Councilmember Dilg stated she appreciates the fact she works here for the polls, and she sees all the work the clerk's office does, and the patience and kindness and helpfulness that all the staff show and all the knowledge you have to make things run smoothly is impressive. All that does is provide an option and an alternative to voting on election day, if we don't get big numbers during early voting, it doesn't mean we failed at notifying the voters, it just means the behavior of the voter is they want to vote absentee or they want to vote on election day. She further stated the clerk's office have done a great job and she's said it many times, your vote counts in South Lyon, and we have very safe elections, and they are run very well, and she then commended the staff and everything they do to stay committed to election integrity.

## MANAGER'S REPORT

City Manager Zelenak stated the road improvements are continuing and they have paved both sides in Eagle Pointe with the base course, and they are expected to complete the project within the next couple of weeks. He then stated the Brookfield pavement removal continues, and they are pouring additional sections as well. He then stated the cemetery improvements are moving forward and we will be bringing items to council for approval for plot prices, the purchase of a columbarium and opening section 11 along with new rules. He then stated the city received marijuana petitions and we challenged the language and filed a lawsuit and the judge decided in our favor along with other communities. Attorney McClorey McLaughlin of RSJALAW stated she was the principal litigator and the background is the City Clerk received petitions for a ballot proposal to put before the voters to change the city charter a complete regulatory scheme for marijuana licenses in the city. She further explained the ballot committee was named the Couth Lyon Survey Committee. She further stated in addition to providing for the number of permissible marijuana establishments, the proposal gave preferences to property owners that already owned property in the city and who have been pre-qualified by the cannabis regulatory agency that would limit the approvable and available licenses to the people interested in this ballot proposal, and would preclude anyone else that is interested in leasing, buying or having an interest in the property and applying and competing for a marijuana license. She further stated under the Michigan Regulation and Taxation of Marijuana Act, individuals may propose an initiative for an Ordinance, either to set the number of establishments, or to prohibit them all together. That is the extent of the act. The marijuana industry doesn't want to be limited to only a number, they want to set the terms of the regulatory scheme that would bind cities and various communities under terms that are only favorable to them, which is unlike any other type of commercial business in the city. They have recently pivoted away from simply proposing an ordinance, so in the past few years, many cities have received ballot proposals and they go beyond the number and the courts have held onto the MRTMA that states you can only say how many. She further stated other smaller clients of her firm received virtually the same proposal that was presented to South Lyon by a survey committee named after each community. She further stated ordinarily a petition would simply be rejected and then the petition circulators would sue the city or the Clerk or both to force the city or the Clerk to put the language on the ballot. She then stated because they had so many clients that were involved with getting the ballot proposals, they went a different way and filed suit with the approval of communities and councils, so along with the cities, of Farmington, Sylvan Lake and

<a id="p5"></a>
*[Page 5 of the original](https://www.southlyonmi.org/8-12-24%20CM%20approved.pdf?t=202408271255440#page=5)* (read by OCR; check the original)

Wixom, the filed a complaint seeking an injunctive and declaratory relief to declare the petitions weren't eligible for the November ballot. She further stated the committees filed their own complaints against the city and clerks. She stated her firm filed for a preliminary injunction and the other party filed a motion to have a ruling declared in their favor. She stated the hearing happened on April 7th, and the judge inquired because sometimes the court isn't willing to consider the substance before it goes on the ballot, their preference is to let it go to the voters and see what happens. She further stated the judge asked her why she should rule on this case, and she was provided with a case she ruled over a few years ago that stated if you go beyond the MRTMA and you go beyond the number, the proposal isn't valid to go on the ballot, and the ruling was upheld by the Michigan Appeals Court. Attorney McClorey McLaughlin then stated the judge granted the ruling the next day saying under MRTMA you are only allowed to use the ordinance amendment, not the charter. She further stated on Friday, the survey committees filed an appeal to the Michigan Court of Appeals. She then stated they filed a motion for immediate consideration so the decision can be made before the deadlines for the proposals for the November Election ballot. She further stated it will be quick process, they have been dealing with this since 2018 almost annually. She stated they expect an answer within the next few weeks. Councilmember Kivell stated it seems lopsided, I can see them asking about it but for them to be sanctioned to do that and it doesn't seem like the Judge is interested in that either. Attorney McClorey McLaughlin stated the law states they can try for an initiative for an ordinance, and that would apply to any municipality, but to put this in a charter, it would treat them differently. That was one of the points they made and Judge Grant latched onto that. The law doesn't say city's can be treated differently. She said it would put cities at a disadvantage and they would be dictating the rules, and very favorable to the industry and the property owners that have been approved by the state. Councilmember Kurtzweil stated it is important to bring up two other issues. The individuals were proposing who in the city would be able to grant licenses. She further stated the language was taking away the authority of City Council, and the authority of the City Manager to determine his personnel and their job description. She further stated it would now be controlled by the marijuana industry as to who we would allow in the city, who we would hire to make sure that individual approved the licenses and that person would have to respond to them, not to Council. She further stated there were additional problems with the ballot language that included the zoning issues. The ballot language was going to upheave the zoning ordinance in South Lyon, and it was going to take away zoning restrictions on where you could put dispensaries, and places that sell marijuana in retail. She further stated they would no longer be able to say they couldn't go near a school or church. The ballot language would allow for them to tell Council how they were going to zone their city. She further stated it was clearly intended to destroying the unfastructure of local government, because they wanted to come in and tell us how to run the city. Attorney McClorey McLaughlin stated those points were also part of the lawsuit that was filed. Mayor Kennedy asked the City Manager if we have notified the owner of the billboard that we aren't going to renew the agreement next year. City Manager Zelenak stated the company has been notified.
