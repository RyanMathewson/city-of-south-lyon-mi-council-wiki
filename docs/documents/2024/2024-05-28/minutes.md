### 1. No Mow May

City Manager, Paul Zelenak states that last September at a City Council Meeting, Council discussed the topic of No Mow May. At this meeting, Council discussed the pros and cons, but did not come to any consensus or motion on how to proceed. At our last Council Meeting, it was requested to bring this item back on the agenda for further discussion tonight. Zelenak states that we will ask Council tonight to bring up discussion and at this time there is no suggested motion, but we will proceed based upon your discussions this evening.

Council Member Kurtzweil leads the discussion. She states that she doesn't want to take too much time, as she spent a lot of time doing research on this topic for the September 2023 meeting, where she provided the factual counter arguments to No Mow May. She states that she thinks the discussion of how we are stewards with our environment is an important discussion to have. Both as residents of the City of South Lyon and as policy makers. As policy makers, we have an opportunity to provide a quality of life which includes that relationship with how we respect nature. For her, No Mow May has been driven by the facts. Being a Master Gardener and certified through Michigan State University, she has had an opportunity to speak with individuals throughout the country on the No Mow May concept and provided some information to City Council. Kurtzweil begins with the information packet from Cornell University.

Kurtzweil adds that if your argument is that No Mow May is a social program of land management that benefits bees, you do not have your facts correct and suggests that maybe you spend some time reading the information that is out there. She adds that it is very important that when you come before Council insisting that a policy be adopted, because you have an inclination or a good feeling about bees, I think you need to understand why No Mow May fails. With No Mow May, all you are doing is providing a temporary solution to a long-term problem. The long-term problem is providing habitat and nectar for beyond the two weeks that you don't cut your grass. You need to provide a habitat all year round. Kurtzweil states that she does design Pollinator Gardens, she has had the Cottongims over to her house and they saw a very sophisticated Pollinator Garden, which is designed to feed bees from March to October and sometimes even in to November. No Mow May started in 2019 over in Britain and the United Kingdom as England was losing its native flowers, bees were a secondary issue. The program moved across the pond to Appleton, Wisconsin, where they also did a community project and they had No Mow May activists that did a research paper and it was eventually debunked scientifically. She encourages people to read the research, understand it and come with a more permanent solution to the problem rather than a temporary solution two weeks in May.

Kurtzweil goes on to discuss carbon sequestration is that we have carbon emissions that are occurring in the atmosphere and these are creating greenhouse gases and contributing to climate change. The argument is that if we quit cutting our grass, then we will not be putting carbon in to the atmosphere through the gasoline of our lawnmowers. They think that 6" of grass will be able to sequester enough carbon in order to significantly influence carbon gases, which is not true. She has researched this issue and nobody, including those who have come to the microphone tonight, have been able to point to any research that says, not cutting your grass in your lawn contributes significantly to a reduction of greenhouse gases, however, there are projects out there that are engaged in containing carbon and some of it is done geologically storing carbon underground and then there are programs that are going through the U.S. Forestry which are planting tall grasses in acres where the grasses are specifically being bred to absorb more carbon and when a plant absorbs carbon, it uses that carbon to assist in photosynthesis. On her property, this is done through pine trees. You will see Austrian pine trees along the highway and it is very capable of sequestering the carbon that comes from the traffic on the highways. White pines are the predominant pine tree for sequestration. Kurtzweil adds that she has been able to assist the Pollinator population, she's been able to sequester carbon and comply with the city ordinance to keep her grass cut at the level that is appropriate. She adds that there has been many in the city who have been active in Pollinator Gardens. She applauds the Cottongims for coming forward, they are a new generation understanding the necessity of this relationship. However, many of us that are older, have been raised from an agricultural background. We grew up with composting and with Pollinator Gardens. There is an ability to balance this with the ordinances that we have in the city and for those reasons, I am not in favor of No Mow May, the agenda is not correct and not factually based and I am an example of having both the ordinance and a very lively bee population on your property 7 to 8 months out of the year.

Planning and Zoning Director Hannah Smith states that this is the first reading for the conditional rezoning of the parcel located at 26800 Pontiac Trail. The property is currently zoned R1A, single family district and is currently Suzi's Bar and Grill. The applicant is asking to conditionally rezone to the O, Office District for the construction of 2 office/commercial buildings. Smith reminds Council that a conditional rezoning is different from a traditional rezoning. It entails a conditional rezoning agreement, that if approved is entered in to by the city and the applicant and outlines the terms of the rezoning, including the voluntarily offered conditions, time limits of the project and other terms. In addition, a conditional rezoning has a plan tied to it, which if the rezoning is approved, no other use or site plan development can occur other than the plan that is attached. The City Attorney and the applicant team have been working together to prepare a rezoning draft agreement which is included in the Council's packet. She states that the agreement is included verbatim in the ordinance document that you are considering tonight, but the council will approve the agreement itself separately at a later date if the first reading is approved tonight. Smith states that listed in the draft agreement in our review letter are the conditions being offered by the applicant. They are offering as a condition of the rezoning a list of uses permitted in the O District that the property will not be developed with, these include: restaurants and bars, banquet and conference centers, private clubs, hospitals, animal boarding establishments, funeral homes and mortuaries, and hotels and motels.

Smith states that attached to the request and the agreement right now is a concept plan, which shows the general building layout, parking, building design and other general details. If the rezoning is approved, the applicant will have to come back for final site plan approval, with a full detailed site plan showing all required information, which would go before the Planning Commission for approval. The final site plan is required to be in substantial conformance with the concept plan and as noted, the development of the
