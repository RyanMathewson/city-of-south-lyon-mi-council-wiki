1. Bid Proposalsa. Witch’s Hat Roof- removal/replacement

Superintendent Renwick stated that we advertised and sent out bids to ten different companies. We
received six responses. They came in very low and very high. The low bid was from Bear Roofing. He
did check them. They have been in business for a number of years and have a good clientele. They are a
licensed builder and have all the necessary paperwork. He would recommend the low bid from Bear

not.

stated that he does.

Mayor Doyle questioned whether a contingency amount is necessary. Superintendent Renwick stated
that we could add 5%, but we do not foresee any additions. Consensus of Council was to leave the bid
amount as is, and any additions would be brought back to Council for approval.

2. Contract for Police/Fire Dispatch- City of Novi- for the period 7/1/08- 6/30/09

Chief Collins stated that the City of Novi directed their City Manager to commission a study to try to assess
the actual cost per user of the dispatch services. The CPA firm that they hired came back and said for this
year the City of South Lyon should pay Novi over $139,000 for dispatch and lock-up services. The City
Manager directed him to speak with Chief David Molloy, and after much discussion, came up with a three
year contract with a $15,000 increase the first year, $18,000 the second and $13,000 the third. Chief
Molloy also agreed to include the ability for reverse 911 service, which is a way for us to get emergency
information by telephone out to our residents in case of a wide-spread HAZMAT or other situation where
we wanted to contact a large number of residents in a short period of time. Even though it is a significant
increase, we did negotiate with them thousands of dollars less. It is far cheaper than if we had our own
dispatch both in terms of personnel cost and initial equipment cost. Therefore, he would recommend
approval of the contract.

Councilman Morelli asked how a call is handled if someone calls 911 from a cell phone. Chief Collins
stated that sometimes it will go directly to Novi dispatch, but sometimes it will be routed through Livingston
County.

Councilman Wedell asked if anyone from our staff looked at the study. City Manager Cook stated that we
did look at it, however did not agree with how they arrived at the large numbers.

3. City Manager Contract- David Murphy

Mayor Doyle stated that before Council is the contract with David Murphy as prepared by Parvin Lee.

Attorney Lee stated that the document was prepared in line with the offer sheet. The offer sheet will be
attached to the contract as Exhibit #1. He stated that our Charter says that the City Manager serves at the
pleasure of Council, which is even stronger than “at-will”. If there is not compatibility between Council and
Manager you can terminate the relationship. If you start spelling out the purposes to terminate the
relationship, you can get into an issue that could go to court. He further explained the possible legal
issues. This is cleaner and simpler. It would be better to separate and pay the severance.

**COUNCIL COMMENTS**

Councilman Morelli stated that with the storms over this past weekend, the storm sirens went off out near
Peer Road and within a few minutes the storm went by. Five minutes later they heard South Lyon sirens
go off, but by that time the storms already went by. He asked how we get notified before the storm hits
rather than after. Chief Collins stated that we do have independent siren activation in place. The way the
system is designed to work is when the Livingston County or Washtenaw County sirens are activated, we
can do a local activation of our sirens. In this instance, the tornado warning was for the Fenton area
moving east or northeast. Dispatch, in error, decided to contact the Fire Chief who was not available so
they contacted the Assistant Fire Chief who made a decision to not activate the sirens. He has spoken to
the Dispatch Manager and reminded him of the policy and indicated that as Emergency Management
Coordinator, he would make that call. He would not have made a decision to not activate the sirens.

Councilman Morelli stated that he would like to thank the Fire Department and Ambulance service. His
son was in need of the service and they responded. He stated that his son called 911 and it took about 34 minutes for an officer to call him back. He stated that he is not sure if that was because of how the cell
call was routed. Chief Collins stated that people can also call the dispatch direct.

Council Member Maida thanked the DPW for painting the light posts downtown.

Councilman Selden discussed the tree damage in Colonial Acres and throughout town due to the storms.

Council Member Kopkowski stated that Milford’s sirens went off before Green Oak. She stated that a lot
of people could not hear ours. She asked if ours are updated and tested to know if people can hear them.
It was stated that we have three sirens and they are tested monthly. Chief Collins stated that the sirens
are designed as an outdoor warning system. If you are indoors with windows closed and air conditioning
running, etc. it would be hit or miss with the design of the system.

Council Member Kopkowski asked Chief Collins how he decides when you put a traffic officer out. The
railroad crossing was down for a considerable amount of time over the weekend. Sometimes it was
smooth because there was an officer there and sometimes it was congested because there was no officer.
Chief Collins stated that it would depend on staffing availability. There were times when there was
emergency calls where we had to pull them off of the traffic detail to handle a priority call. He stated that if
we had three officers working we could have an officer at both of the crossings and still have the third
officer to respond to the calls for service. Further discussion was held on the problems with the crossings.
City Manager Cook stated that this is a continuous problem we have had over the years. At one time
there was a problem with trucks going over the tracks that would trigger the problem. When they put the
crossings in they did not deal with the appropriate drainage to drain the tracks which triggers the system.
We have been trying to get them to correct it.  The question was asked if they pay us anything for having
our officers out there. Chief Collins stated that generally, we do bill them, but because of the National

enforce it. Attorney Lee stated that until 1998 we regularly ticketed them if they had the gates down for
