## PUBLIC COMMENT

Jeff Heinanen of 350 S Lafayette stated he is here for the Zoning Ordinance Amendment on the agenda. He stated he supports the extension of the central business district that is currently B-3 zoning. This allows for more development or re-development in the downtown. He recently had an application before the Zoning Board of Appeals that was denied. During the process we spent a significant amount of time reviewing the city's ordinances and zoning ordinances and a few other communities. There are a couple of items that he is requesting Council to consider. Loading is only allowed in the back of a building, with the exception that it could be done in the side yard, if there's a minimum of 50 feet. He is requesting Council to review that and add language allowing for loading in front yards in the central business district. He stated a CBD typically consists of smaller lots and rear loading isn't always feasible. It is understandable the requirement is for big box stores, but it doesn't always make sense in smaller downtowns. He then stated the second item, is for the City to consider allowing flag lots. ### 3. Intergovernmental agreement

City Manager Zelenak stated the City of South Lyon, Lyon Township and South Lyon Schools explored the opportunity to work together a plan to provide sidewalks and crosswalks in association with the Safe Routes to School program near Pontiac Trail and Marjorie Ann. The civil engineering design work was completed, but since then the High School has pulled away from the plan and the project was never completed. After numerous conversations with Township Officials, we have again initiated a much smaller project to install just the sidewalks and crosswalks at Marjorie Ann and Pontiac Trail. Because the original bids were taken in 2017, we are proposing to work on this project with the Township engineer to create new documents and adopt the interlocal governmental agreement between the City of South Lyon and Lyon Township. We are now budgeting $35,000 for our version of the scaled down project. Councilmember Kennedy stated he wanted to applaud City Manager Zelenak for getting the ball rolling on this project, it's a shame it wasn't completed earlier, but it is a step in the right direction, not only here, but for other places and the more we can work together and the City is more walkable, the better it'll be for all the other residents whether they live in the City, Lyon or Green Oak Township. Councilmember Kurtzweil thanked the City Manager for working on this and for getting the price level that is a lot more palpable than the one previous. She further stated it isn't just for the school children, the real beneficiary is the Methodist Church and St. Joes because a lot of individuals that live in that area attend both of those Churches. They are part of the community and donate to the community and they are just as much part of our community as anyone else. Mayor Pelchat stated anytime the City and the Township can come together to better both of our communities, especially when it is as important as the public health and safety of the residents and school kids. He stated he works in that area daily and this is a definite need and he is thankful for everyone's effort.
