# Rules of Conduct for Public Comment at Council Meetings $ ^{*} $

Members of the public may speak at a Council meeting upon recognition of the Mayor. Public comment may only occur during periods designated on the agenda for public comment or a public hearing. A person may speak for up to two (2) minutes during each of the two public comment periods on agenda items or non-agenda items. Waivers of the time requirement may only be granted in the discretion of the Mayor, and waivers to speak at a time other than a designated comment period may only be granted by the Council. Any person wishing to make a presentation longer than two minutes or requiring audio-visual equipment is asked to contact the City Clerk requesting to appear on a future agenda.

A person may only address Council from the podium. Only one person may occupy the podium at a time. All remarks are to be directed to the Mayor and Council. Speakers are not to engage in direct dialog with other meeting attendees.

Any person who violates the Rules of Conduct, disturbs the peace at the meeting, and/or interferes with the meeting may be warned, ordered to be seated, removed, and/or ticketed.

AGENDA TOPIC: Council Meeting Conduct – Council Rules and Procedures

EXPLANATION OF TOPIC: At the January 22, 2018 meeting, Council considered rules relating to Council meeting conduct including public comment. Council separated consideration of public comment rules by postponing that matter to February 12, 2018, and postponing consideration of rules pertaining to Council conduct to June 25, 2018. At its May 29, 2018 meeting, Council approved Resolution 04-18 establishing Rules of Conduct for Public Comment at City Council Meetings. At the June 11, 2018 meeting, City Council approved the second reading a new Code of Ordinances Section 58-53, "Interference with conduct of business; interference with meetings of a public body," to establish penalties for any person disturbing the peace at a public meeting or failing to comply with an order or directive of a meeting chair. Having completed its consideration of public comment procedures, the next phase of the Council's rules review relates to rules and procedures governing Council debate and meeting procedures. Samples of meeting rules and procedures have previously been provided.

The following is a listing of subjects commonly addressed in Council rules and procedures that the Council may wish to consider. Included within each item is a summary of existing South Lyon rules or ordinances on the topic and/or a discussion of other communities' approaches to the subject.

Format of Rules: Applicability of Robert's Rules of Order: One of the first questions the Council may wish to consider is the scope of its rules review project. Specifically, is it the Council's wish to create a resolution comparable to the public comment resolution, but geared toward Council debate procedures, or does it wish to embark on crafting a more wide-ranging rulebook that looks more like a local version of Robert's Rules of Order? Other communities are essentially split in the approaches that they take, with some having rather streamlined rules that focus on the items most critical to ensuring orderly meetings, while others have bylaws-style documents.

Presently, the Council’s existing Resolution Setting Procedures for Council Deliberations Dated 1/23/2006 [“2006 Resolution”], Part 5, provides that Council deliberations shall be governed by Robert’s Rules of Order” unless the requirements are waived by affirmative vote of four (4) Council Members. This is a strong level of deference to Robert’s Rules, which is reflective of the fact that the Council does not presently have an extensive set of customized rules. Many other communities provide for Robert’s Rules to control except to the extent that their local rules control. It is also common for communities to provide for Robert’s Rules to control to the extent feasible, perhaps recognizing that strict adherence to all of the nuances of Robert’s Rules is unlikely, and to give room for the public body to develop its own customs.

The suggested topics for discussion below are not meant to be an exhaustive list. These have been included to the extent that they are relevant to the concerns about orderly debate and decorum that have been a focus of Council's effort to review its rules. If the Council focuses on these matters, another resolution-style document will likely be in order.

Beyond the identified topics, the Council may also wish to include a variety of other topics either to modify the corresponding Robert's Rules on the topic, or simply to provide a readily-accessible reference for the current and future Councils. These additional topics may include items such as detailing motion procedures, voting procedures, committee procedures, agenda structure, public hearing procedures, handling calls for the question and points of order, and other organizational or procedural matters. If such an approach is taken, the resulting document would look more like a set of bylaws, and could also incorporate the previous public comment rules by reference.

Procedure for Placing Items on Future Agendas: Council rules commonly outline the general procedure for setting the Council Agenda, including noting whether council members must follow the same procedure or may add items through another means. South Lyon's 2006 Resolution, Part 2(b), provides that, during "Public Comment," a Council Member may "add an item or subject to a future meeting's agenda." This is in contrast to the Resolution's rule for placing items on the current agenda at Part 3, which requires a simple majority vote to place items on the agenda for which no action will be taken, or which are emergency items initiated by the City Manager, and a super-majority vote of five (5) Council Members for all other items. Council may wish to consider whether it desires to establish more specific requirements for Council Members requesting future agenda items, and this may or may not be in conjunction with spelling out the overall agenda-setting process. Some communities require that Council Members follow the same procedure for requesting an item as any other person inside or outside of the government. Others require it to be submitted via the City Manager. It also is not unusual in some communities for council members to make requests of the administration at meetings to investigate certain issues which may then be brought to the Council for review as a discussion or action item. The Council may also wish to adopt some hybrid version of these procedures based on the type or urgency of the issue.

Council Comments (agenda items): The Council does not presently have specific rules governing its procedures for debating agenda items. Other communities' rules run a spectrum from simply defaulting to Robert's Rules to establishing more elaborate guidelines similar to those the Council adopted for public comment. Among the rules Council might wish to consider:

Manner of Being Recognized: As with public comment, and consistent with Robert's Rules, Council procedures typically provide that a member may only speak upon recognition by the Mayor. Common practice is for a member to seek recognition by addressing the Mayor as "Mr. (or Madame) Mayor" at a time when no other member is speaking, and then speaking only upon being granted permission by the Mayor to speak either through a verbal indication or other gesture. The Council could also consider hand-raising as an alternative means seeking recognition. When multiple members seek recognition simultaneously, the Mayor would have discretion to determine the order of speaking.

*[Page 30 of the original](https://www.southlyonmi.org/6-25-18%20packet.pdf?t=202108310733120#page=30)* (read by OCR; check the original)

Order of Speaking: Some communities order discussion by allowing the maker of a motion the opportunity to speak first, and then each additional council member who wishes to do so may speak, which is consistent with Robert's Rules. It is also common for communities to provide that no Council member speak a second time until all members who wish to speak have had a chance to do so, and so on until debate has ended. If the Council chooses to pursue such a limitation, it may also wish to consider how the Mayor will discern whether all Council members who wish to speak in a given round of discussion have had the opportunity to do so. An efficient and common way of making this determination is simply for the Chair to call for discussion and ensure that no additional members are seeking recognition before allowing a member to make additional comments. Other options include offering discussion opportunities in a round-robin style based on seniority, name, or seating position, perhaps with the option to stagger the call for discussion from one item to the next so that all Council Members have equal opportunities to speak early in discussions. If some form of restriction on speaking again before others have spoken is adopted, then to the extent that Council Members wish to direct comments and questions to each other, they would effectively do so via the Mayor, and the result may not always be an instantaneous back-and-forth exchange. This could be a disadvantage if it breaks up the continuity of the discussion, but also could have a positive effect of preventing any two Council Members from dominating a discussion at a given time, and/or preventing the escalation of any tensions that may exist in the exchange.

Time or Frequency Limits on Members: Some communities have adopted time limits or limits on the number of times an individual Council member may speak on the same agenda item, similar to this Council's rules for public comment. Robert's Rules of Order also envision a member of a public body "exhausting" his or her ability to speak on any given issue. To the extent that the Council might wish to adopt some limits on debate, it may include a waiver procedure comparable to the public comment procedures. If the Council desires to pursue such limits, it should be thoughtful about establishing rules that balance its interest in promoting debate and developing consensus on issues with the goals it identifies for limiting discussion. It should also ensure that any rules that are created are capable of consistent enforcement.

Germaneness: As with public comment on agenda items, Robert's Rules and those adopted by other communities commonly require that council members' comments be relevant to the issue up for debate at any given time.

Council Comment (non-agenda items): Presently, the 2006 Resolution, Part 1 provides for a “Council Comment” portion of the agenda after “New Business.” 2006 Resolution, Part 2 further provides that during “Public Comment” the Council Members may a) make comments or direct questions to other City officials, or make general announcements to the community, and b) add an item to a future meeting’s agenda. (It appears likely that Part 2 meant to refer to the “Council Comment” portion of the meeting established in Part 1, not “Public Comment.”) Beyond this, no specific rules govern the Council Comment period. If it wishes to develop additional rules for the “Council Comment” period, the Council could consider any of the same mechanisms discussed with respect to agenda items.

Council Interaction with Public Commenters: Since the recently-approved Rules of Conduct at Public Comment for City Council Meetings were directed at members of the public who wish to participate in comment at meetings, an item that remains open for discussion is whether and how the Council wishes to organize its responses to public comment. Many communities' rules are silent on this matter. However, some communities provide that the council will not interact with or respond to public comments except as necessary to maintain order. Alternatively, a community may only allow interaction upon a member receiving the Mayor's permission, pursuant to normal procedures for being recognized. Another community simply gives the legislative body discretion to decide whether or not to respond. Communities with a "no-response" rule may make a statement at the beginning of the meeting or public comment clarifying that public comment is a time for the public to speak, and the Council will listen, but it is the Council's policy to not respond. Of course, if the Council limits responses to public comment during public comment periods, members would retain the right to address public comments during their council comments.

With these ideas in mind, relevant considerations related to this matter include: 1) whether the Council wants to be able to respond to public comment at all within the public comment period; 2) whether the Council wants to allow Council members to answer questions posed by the public commenter before the public commenter is done speaking, which could affect how the speaker's time limit is calculated; 3) whether the Council wishes to allow direct interaction between Council Members and the public commenter, or whether all comments will be channeled through the Mayor; and, 4) how to refer public requests for the Mayor, Council, or administration investigate an issue. This last consideration may also relate to agenda-setting rules.

Council Decorum: Many communities, and Robert's Rules, pay special attention to the manner in which Council Members address each other. They include requirements that the members be courteous toward one another, refrain from personal comments, avoid "personalities," and even refrain from calling each other by name – favoring instead addressing each other by title. At least one community has even gone so far as to include in its rules a prohibition on criticizing past decisions of the Council. In essence, these types of rules are designed with the goal of maintaining the integrity, legitimacy, and efficiency of the Council as an institution. The ability of a Council to regulate its own discussion and conduct more strictly than the public's flows from the fact that the First Amendment treats government self-regulation differently than government regulation of citizens. That said, it remains advisable to avoid anything that looks like a content-based restriction, and proposals limiting Council's ability to engage in debate should be balanced against considerations of how the restriction might limit the Council's legislative function, information-gathering function, and the individual members' roles as representatives of the public. Regardless of the vigor of the regulations, it can be expected that a Council Member could be equally subject to discipline for violations of an order of the Chair and the Council's rules as provided by any rules created by the Council, Robert's Rules, and Ordinance Section 58-53.

Time Limits for Meetings: Some communities place a time limit on the length of their meetings or have a rule that no new agenda item will be started after a certain time. Where they exist, these limits can typically be overridden by a vote of the Council.

Conflicts of Interest: South Lyon already has a substantial ethics ordinance that includes detailed definitions and procedures for addressing conflicts of interest. Specifically, in the event a Council Member has a conflict of interest, the Member has a duty of disclosure. Where the Member has a financial interest as defined in the Ordinance, Ordinance Sec. 2-76(e)(1) provides that the Member must refrain from participating in any discussion, voting or action on the item for which he or she has a conflict. Therefore, as the Ordinance is written, no Council action is required upon a Member declaring a conflict and offering to abstain from the discussion and vote. Some communities require a vote either to recuse the member, or, alternatively allow the Council to vote to override a member's request to be recused.

# Resolution Setting Procedures for Council Deliberations

Motion by Kivell, supported by Wedell

Whereas, the City Council desires to promulgate guidelines and rules for its proceedings and deliberations, and

Whereas, the Council pursuant to the "Home Rule City" Act may control its deliberations as provided by State Law.

Now Therefore, the City Council adopts the following rules to govern its proceedings

1) A "Council Comment" section shall be added to the agenda following "New Business".

2) That during the "Public Comment" portion of the meeting, Council Members will use this period as their opportunity to:

a. Make comments or direct questions to the Mayor, other Council Members, City Manager, Department Heads, Clerk, City Attorney, or make general announcements to the community.

b. Add an item or subject to a future meeting's agenda.

3) An item may be added to the current agenda upon approval of a simple majority of Council Members under the following conditions:

a. An item as a discussion piece, meaning an item where no action is to be taken, or

b. An item as an emergency situation initiated by the City Manager or his representative, meaning an item that was unforeseeable and / or must be acted on prior to the next scheduled meeting, or

c. All other items upon approval of a super-majority of five (5) of the Council Members.

4) Immediately subsequent to a vote, any member of the council may request a roll call vote (recording of individual votes of Council Members) on any matter before Council.

5) All deliberations of Council shall be governed by “Robert's Rules of Order” including the most recent amendments unless the requirements are waived by affirmative vote of four (4) Council Members

## RE: Playground Equipment Review

Dear Ms. Ladner:

Thank you for meeting with me on October 17, 2017. The purpose of the visit was to conduct a risk review of the city owned playground equipment. This review is intended to augment your organization's risk management program. The scope of my review addressed primarily the following exposures:

Recommendations:

Develop a pre-season/seasonal playground site safety inspection program. Document each inspection as conducted.

- When repairing a piece of equipment, access to the piece should be closed. Post signage stating "No Access - Under Construction".

Level the material under the swing seats. Raise the swing seats to reduce the amount of material kick out.

The rubber coating over the metal on the clatter bridge is cracked and enhances the chance for rust. Tighten up the connection pieces to reduce the gap between the platform and the bridge. This is a pinch hazard.

The blue colored slide should have a covered chute at the entrance to the slide.

The tube slide is separated from the platform. Clean out the debris in the gaps and caulk them with silicone.

The metal slide is separated from the platform. Clean out the debris in the gaps and caulk them with silicone. A metal slide should be positioned east. North/south position allows more sun exposure on slide bed surface with increased potential to burn a user.

The play structures metal platforms and stairways have a cracked coating that enhances metal degradation. Repair/replace the metal platforms. Contact the manufacturer for recommendations as discussed during my review.

# CITY OF SOUTH LYON BANNER POLICY FOR LIGHT POLES & OVER-THE-ROAD BANNERS

1.  $ \underline{\text{ELIGIBILITY}} $: The organization requesting the banner and holding the event or activity must be a non-profit or governmental entity (Group/Organization).

2. GROUP/ORGANIZATION PRIORITY: The banner space availability will be based on the prioritization listed below. Space is on a first come basis under this prioritization ladder:

A. Level 1: Non Profit (NP) Group based in South Lyon, event held in Downtown South Lyon

B. Level 2: NP Group based in South Lyon, event held anywhere in the City of South Lyon

C. Level 3: South Lyon School District Events at South Lyon and South Lyon East High Schools

D. Level 4: NP Group based outside of South Lyon, with the event held in the City of South Lyon

NOTE: In the event that the City of South Lyon or the DDA seek to hang a banner during a time that has been requested by another organization, the City or DDA shall receive priority (See Pg. 3)

3.  $ \underline{\text{PERMITS AND FEES}} $:

A. Temporary Banner Permits are valid for thirty (30) days from the date of issue, or for a defined season, ninety (90) days, with start and end dates.

B. Three (3) temporary banner permits may be obtained within a twelve (12) month period.

C. The application fee for each permit request (not for each banner as in the case of multiple light pole banners), as established by the City of South Lyon, shall be as follows:

1)  $ \underline{\text{Light Pole Banners/One-Block}} $: The fee for installation and removal of "Light Pole Banners",  $ \underline{\text{for One-Block}} $, every other banner armed pole*, both sides of the street, by the South Lyon Department of Public Works (DPW) shall be  $ \underline{\text{90.00}} $.

2)  $ \underline{\text{Light Pole Banners/Multiple Blocks}} $: The fee for installation and removal of "Light Pole Banners" for Multiple Blocks, every other banner armed pole*, both sides of the street, by the DPW shall be  $ \underline{\text{180.00.}} $

3)  $ \underline{\text{Over-The-Road Banners}} $: The fee for installation and removal of the "Over-The-Road" Banner on N. Lafayette Street, by the South Lyon Fire Department (SLFD) shall be  $ \underline{\text{180.00.}} $

*Light poles with banner arms are located at every other pole. Banners by Non-Profit Organization banners will be placed on every other banner arm pole (which is every fourth pole).

4.  $ \underline{\text{DEFINITIONS}} $: There are two (2) types of banners governed by this policy.

A.  $ \underline{\text{“Light Pole Banners”}} $ are those banners which can be placed on pedestrian light poles within the DDA District. They are vertical in design and orientation and they are placed on two arms attached to the light pole. The bottom of the banner must be at least 8 feet from the surface of the sidewalk.

B. "Over-The-Road Banners" can be placed horizontally over N. Lafayette Street. That is the only location now available in the City for this type of banner. The bottom of the banner must be no

1) Light Pole Banner height must be 36 inches high and a width of 18 inches (or banner will not be hung).

# South Lyon High School Cheer Boosters By-Laws

## Article I – Name of Organization

The name of this organization shall be the South Lyon High School Cheer Boosters.

## Article II - Purpose

The purpose of the organization is to:

1. Build and maintain an organization, in cooperation with those in charge of the Cheerleaders, to help promote the activities and further the goals of all the Cheerleaders.

2. Promote and support the educational and safety aspects of cheerleading and stunting.

3. Lend all possible support to the South Lyon High School Cheerleaders by promoting excellence in cheer and tumbling, high moral standards, and school spirit among the Cheerleaders.

4. Encourage and maintain an enthusiastic interest by parents and community members participating in the various activities of the Cheerleaders.

5. Support fundraising activities to assist in funding additional training or equipment needs, and enhance their cheerleading experience

6. The SLHS Cheerleader Booster Club shall specifically refrain from being involved in or attempting to intervene in any decisions concerning;

Selection of uniforms

• Selection of officers among cheerleaders

• Squad awards

• Performance, tryouts, formations, stunts or choreography

• Selection of cheer camps

- Selection of added clothing to be worn by cheerleaders during camp, school or other events

• Discipline

- Additional practices that will be mandated by the coaches. The activities of this organization shall not conflict with the policies of the SLCS and SLHS Booster Club guidelines.

## Article III – Management

The business, property and affairs of the Corporation shall be managed by the Board of Directors. There shall be no less than 3, nor more than 6 Directors serving at any time.

The Board of Directors shall serve upon the approval of the South Lyon High School Principal and the South Lyon High School Cheerleading Coaches.
