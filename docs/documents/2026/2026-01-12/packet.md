##  $ \underline{\text{PRESENTATION-}} $ Life saving award

Chief Unruh asked Officer Osbourne, Officer Stevens and Mr. Meinecke and Ms. Bizek to come forward. She stated on October 17th Officer Osbourne and Officer Stevens were dispatched to Quail Run for an accident. A vehicle struck a building and the driver was unconscious and the car was still in gear. Officer Stevens arrived after 3 minutes of being dispatched to the scene. He then saw the driver was unconscious and not breathing. Without hesitation, Officer Stevens broke the window to gain access to the driver. He rendered the vehicle safe, and removed the driver from the vehicle. She then stated two citizens bystanders were present and willing to help. Officer Stevens had the good Samaritans begin CPR on the man as Officer Stevens retrieved the first aid equipment from the vehicle. Officer Osbourne arrived on the scene and began assisting with CPR. Officer Stevens retrieved an automated external defibrillator from his patrol vehicle and applied it to the patient. The AED completed an assessment and advised that a shock was needed. The shock was given and the officers continued to perform CPR. The patient began breathing unassisted shortly after. Officer Stevens and Officer Osbourne continued rendering aid until they were relieved by the South Lyon Fire Department and Huron Valley Ambulance. The patient was stabilized and transported to the hospital for further treatment. She then stated Kathryn Bizek and Frederick Meinecke both heard the accident and left their homes. While Officer Stevens was retrieving the AED out of his vehicle, both Kathryn Bizek and Frederick Meinecke performed CPR on the unconscious man. Once able, the Officers took over CPR and cared for the patient. She then stated without these citizens, the man would have went without CPR while Officer Stevens was going to the car to get the AED. It's very critical during every second that CPR is being delivered or the AED is applied. It is amazing to have citizens to step forward and offer to help us. ## The Problem: We are not using the safest, most effective method for mosquito control

The City of South Lyon used a chemical called bifenthrin in our parks last year.

The U.S. EPA classifies bifenthrin as a possible human carcinogen. (9).

## What happens to bifenthrin in the environment?

Bifenthrin is not likely to reach groundwater because it binds tightly to soil. However, soil-bound bifenthrin has the potential to contaminate surface waters through runoff.

## Can bifenthrin affect birds, fish, or other wildlife?

Bifenthrin is low in toxicity to birds. There are potential risks for birds and mammals that eat aquatic organisms because bifenthrin can last a long time in the environment and it may accumulate in fish. Bifenthrin is highly toxic to fish and small aquatic organisms. It's also very highly toxic to bees. (9)

Bifenthrin is not legal for agricultural use in the European Union and Canada has restricted its use.

It doesn't harm beneficial insects.

It's less expensive, saving money and pollinators.

# South Lyon Boys Basketball Boosters Bylaws Created 4/24/2024

## Article I - NAME:

A. The official name of the organization shall be South Lyon Boys Basketball Boosters hereinafter referred to as South Lyon Boys Basketball Boosters or SLBB. South Lyon Boys Basketball Boosters is a 501C3 with EIN 81-4805235. The organization will remain nonprofit forever.

## Article II - PURPOSE:

A. South Lyon Boys Basketball Boosters are a non-profit parent-based volunteer organization which provides support to the South Lyon High School boys basketball program. The boosters will raise funds to support the South Lyon boys' basketball program by facilitating consistent communications and by leveraging volunteer efforts.

B. The Boosters will aid and assist the boys' basketball program associated with South Lyon High School by providing fundraising activities to provide financial support not available through the school funds. All donations and other monies received or earned by this organization shall only be used to achieve those aims and purposes as set out. The Boosters shall have no salaried officers.

a. All monies of the Boosters shall be deposited into the SLLB account under the direction of the Treasurer.

## Article III - OFFICERS:

A. The officers of the Boosters shall be President, Vice-President, Secretary, Treasurer, Public Relations,

a. Nominations shall be solicited from the membership of the South Lyon Boys Basketball Boosters program.

b. Those nominated shall be present and give consent to their nomination at the regular meeting in May. Voting will be at the June meeting and shall be by anonymous vote from all those in attendance at the meeting. There will be no voting by proxy.

c. All positions are volunteer positions.

d. All positions shall serve a term of one-year. Terms run from July 1st to June 30th. There shall be no limits on the number of terms a board member may serve.

## Background and Overview

The existing service contract between the City and Duncan Disposal went into effect in 2008, following an RFP issued on the City's behalf by RRRASOC. The City extended the contract in 2013. The contract was transferred to Rizzo Environmental Services (RES) when they acquired Duncan Disposal in 2016. RES was acquired by GFL that same year. The City subsequently extended the contract in 2018 and 2022, with a current expiration date of June 30, 2026.

Priority Waste acquired the GFL residential service in the region in 2024, and the South Lyon City Council assigned the contract to Priority Waste on June 24, 2024.

At the direction of the former City Manager, RRRASOC prepared an RFP for issue by the City to solicit proposals for a five-year service period to begin July 1, 2026. The RFP was reviewed by the former City Manager and the City's legal counsel before being released by the City on September 2, 2025. Responses were due on November 3, 2025.

The RFP requested pricing for "As-Is" service, as well as alternatives for "Cart Plus" and "Cart Only" service. The attached tabulation page shows the submitted pricing for the three options.

Following the receipt of the proposals and the tabulation, the Mayor and the City administration concluded that continuing with the "As-Is" service was most consistent with the needs of the residents and the City. The attached tabulation page shows the cost implications for the City.

Subsequently, Priority Waste officials were interviewed by City officials and RRRASOC, after which a draft agreement was prepared for further review. The draft agreement is based on the sample included in the RFP, incorporating some exceptions indicated by Priority Waste, additional recommendations by the City's legal counsel, and the Mayor's request that Priority Waste provide the City's homeowners another 250 recycling carts, upon request, at no cost.

The draft agreement was further reviewed by City officials prior to it being forwarded to Priority Waste for additional review and comment. A final draft will be provided to the City for consideration.
