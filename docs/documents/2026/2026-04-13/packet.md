## FIRE CHIEF REPORT

Chief Conrad stated since the last council meeting, they have had 56 calls. 37 EMS, 3 false alarms, 10 public assist, 4 hazardous conditions, 1 assist to law enforcement and 1 vehicle fire. She then stated she was able to meet Tom Barrett last Thursday night, and he was late arriving, but Councilmember Kurtzweil made sure she was able to meet and speak with him for his support for the grant, and he said they already cut some out, so we are still being considered. Chief Conrad then reviewed her annual report. She then pointed out that there are new national reporting system and incidents are recategorized, so she will be moving some things around. She then stated page 5 shows the incidents by month and incidents by year going back to 2013. She further stated they had a 14% increase in calls from 2025. Partly due to the large number of smoke alarms they received from the grant. She stated lift assists had a 52% increase as well, and overall, the department has had 215% increase since 2013. She then pointed out the organizational chart and she said it reflects the largest number of staff our FD has ever had in at least 20 years. She stated she is proud to say that and she has been turning away applicants which is really never heard of. She wants to stay cautiously optimistic because it is a paid on and part time call department for firefighters, and that number could change at any time. She then pointed out pages 11-13 reflect honors and recognitions and she is very proud of the individuals because they have gone above and beyond for the community and the department. Chief Conrad then pointed out the different divisions that are part of the Fire Department and they all work together to make sure the department works together seamlessly. She then stated they have attended a lot of community events which are also reflected in the report. She further stated their first goal is to continue to provide the highest level of emergency response and customer service to the community with a focus on professionalism, efficiency and compassion. She further stated training and readiness will always play front and center in our operations and leadership and professional growth are her favorites and she will continue to support her staff to continue to learn and grow. Councilmember Kurtzweil stated she doesn't get to see a lot of department heads in action, but she had a great opportunity to see the professionalism when you were addressing a United States Congressman. She and City Manager Baaki were great and they were very impressed. She stated other people thought the same and she is very proud of both of them. Councilmember Squires stated he could see the training and readiness and he wants to give her kudos for always making sure our firemen are trained and he was very impressed.

##  $ \underline{\text{NEW BUSINESS}} $

1. Renew contract with Novi to provide dispatch services

Chief Unruh stated the current contract with the City of Novi to provide dispatch services to the City of South Lyon will expire on June 30, 2026. The City of Novi would like to enter a new five-year agreement, for the period of July 1, 2026-June 30, 2031 for the City of Novi to provide dispatch services to the City of South Lyon at the following rates which reflect a 4% increase each year. Councilmember Squires asked how long the city has been using Novi dispatch. City Manager Baaki stated probably since the mid 80's. At one time in the 70's we had our own dispatch, and found it to be very cost prohibitable. Novi does a great job for us, and we were expecting possibly a 5% increase, so we are very happy with the 4% increase each year. Location wise, it works great because we can lodge prisoners in their jail instead of driving them to Pontiac. They will also arraign them for us as well.

## The Cruise-In

As we embark on the 2026 edition of the South Lyon Lake Street Cruise-In, we thought it might be fun to take a look back at where we started and how we've grown.

It really started over a couple beers at the Lake Street Tavern on a snowy cold day in 2011. Mary Poole had just opened the Lake Street Tavern and she wondered what we could do to get people downtown to help the local businesses thrive.

Talk turned to the summer car shows that the Cowtown Cruisers, the Spark Plugs, and Brown's Root Beer would put on back in the day. We thought, could we revive that tradition and create our own version of a classic car cruise-in? Ideas were jotted down a cocktail napkin and the South Lyon Lake Street Cruise-In was born.

In 2012 we teamed up with the First Presbyterian Church on Lake Street for additional lawn parking at the corner of Reese and E Lake Street, which they owned at the time. They have been an integral part of our efforts.

In July 2018, responding to a request from the City, we created the first South Lyon Lake Street Cruise-In all day Motorfest which brought over 350 classic cars and a huge crowd to downtown South Lyon.

Each year we make a donation to a local South Lyon charity. Past recipients include: Operation Injured Soldiers, Families Building Faith, South Lyon Community Schools, The First Presbyterian Church, South Lyon First Responders Memorial Fund, Blessings in a Back Pack, South Lyon Kiwanis Club, and the Michigan War Dog Memorial.

We thank the South Lyon City Council, South Lyon Police and Fire Departments, Department of Public Works, and the Downtown Development Authority for their continued support.

### House Bill 5529 (Rep. Kristian Grant) — Land Division Minimums

Prohibits municipalities from establishing a minimum parcel size of more than 1,500 square feet for a parcel with a detached single-family residence that will be served by public water and sewer.

### House Bill 5530 (Rep. Jennifer Wortz) — Lot Size Minimums

Prohibits municipalities from establishing a minimum parcel size of more than 1,500 square feet on land zoned for a detached single-family residence that will be served by public water and sewer.

### House Bill 5531 (Rep. Cynthia Neeley) — Site Plan Reviews & Shot Clock

Requires local governments to develop and provide blank site plan application forms. Local governments could require any studies or other documents that are reasonably necessary to evaluate the proposal's nature, scope, and intensity as part of their review of the application.

After initial site plan approval, a local unit of government could not require the applicant to submit additional studies or other documents for the same application, or revise previously submitted studies or documents, unless necessary.

If the applicant seeks a change in the approved site plan, the local government cannot require additional studies or documents unless reasonably necessary for ensuring code compliance or addressing a public health or safety concern. The local government could not request additional studies or documents to impose new or additional requirements unrelated to the change or to reopen issues previously reviewed and approved.

Under the bill, local governments must decide on whether to approve, reject, or conditionally approve a site plan within 60 days of receiving the application. If rejected or conditionally approved, the decision must also include the specific reasons why and how it can be appealed.

### House Bill 5532 (Rep. Joseph Aragona) — Protest Petitions

Currently, an amendment to a zoning ordinance by a city or village is subject to a protest petition. If a protest petition is submitted, then approval of the amendment requires a 2/3 vote of the legislative body.

If a zoning ordinance amendment increases the authorized number of dwelling units, the bill would require a protest petition to be signed by either or both of the following:

The owners and any tenants of at least 60% of the area of land included in the proposed change

The owners and any tenants of at least 60% of the area of land included within an area extending outward 300 feet from any point on the boundary of the land included in the proposed change

This is increased from 20% of owners or tenants in both cases, and an increase from 300 feet to 100 feet for the boundary of land included in the proposed change.

The bill also would add a requirement that the city or village clerk verify the land ownership of petition signers and otherwise determine the adequacy of the petition.

### House Bill 5581 (Rep. Tom Kunse) — Dwelling Size Minimums

A zoning ordinance must not impose a minimum area requirement of greater than 500 square feet for a dwelling.

### House Bill 5582 (Rep. Kristian Grant) — Parking Minimums & Mobile Homes
