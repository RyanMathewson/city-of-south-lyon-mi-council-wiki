**Rules** **of Conduct for** **Public** **Comment** **at** **Council** **Meetings***


Members of the public may speak at a Council meeting upon recognition by the Mayor.


At the start of a speaker's comment, the speaker shall provide his/her name and address for the

record.


Public comment may only occur during periods designated on the agenda for public comment

or a public hearing. A person may speak for up to two (2) minutes during each of the two public

comment periods on agenda items or non-agenda items. Waivers of the time requirement may

only be granted in the discretion of the Mayor, and waivers to speak at a time other than a

designated comment period may only be granted by the Council. Any person wishing to make a

presentation of longer than two minutes or requiring audio-visual equipment is asked to contact

the City Clerk requesting to appear on a future agenda.


A person may only address Council from the podium. A person must state their name and

address. Only one person may occupy the podium at a time. All remarks are to be directed to

the Mayor and Council. Speakers are not to engage in direct dialog with other meeting

attendees.


Any person who violates the Rules of Conduct, disturbs the peace at a meeting, and/or

interferes with the meeting may be warned, ordered to be seated, removed, and/or ticketed.

**Introduction:**


The Owner contacted Preload via text 7/27/26, sending a video of a large leak (Figure 1 excerpt photo)
through the edge of one of the eight vertical pilasters on the exterior wall. The purpose of the Preload's
inspection was to determine the cause of the leak and identify any conditions on the tank exterior that
might require structural repair and maintenance to: a) get the tank safely back in service; and b) extend
the longevity of the tank's useful life.


From August 17th to 19th, 2026, Preload performed an interior and exterior inspection of the 1.0 MG
Prestressed Concrete Tank owned and operated by the City of South Lyon (Owner). Our main inspection
date was August 18th when Preload entered the tank in the afternoon following assessment of the dome
ring beam condition as well as gaining an overview of the tank wall circumferential wire corrosion at three

Executive Summary:


The above grade portions of the 46-year-old tank exhibit structural issues showing circumferential (hoop)
prestressing wire corrosion at the faces of three out of eight vertical pilasters. The problem is minor at
two of these pilasters (#'s 1 & 7), however there is significant loss of prestress at the left face of pilaster
#8. We believe the loss of hoop prestress at the left side of pilaster # 8 (from 6-feet above finish grade to
the top of wall) led to opening up of the shotcrete joint between two precast wall panels. The coating on
the inside face of wall at this location exhibits a vertical line that appeared to have been previously
injected. Contributing factors for the wire corrosion at this location are: a) the lineup of left edge of the
formed pilaster with a smooth steel cover plate bridging the ribbed steel diaphragm at the joint between
precast wall panels; b) original construction sequence of forming and placement of the 3-foot wide
pilasters close to the prestressing wires before 1" shotcrete cover was placed over the wires (an
unexpected construction sequence that is not standard practice today); c) incorporation of a formed dome
ring separated with a bearing pad on top of the wall (different than modern Preload dome rings which are
integral with the wall panel and bonded to the ribbed wall diaphragm.


The dome and remainder of the wall between pilasters # 1 to within a foot of # 7, are not exhibiting any
outward signs of structural distress or durability issues that would affect tank serviceability.


There are three items that Preload has identified as requiring maintenance/repair, before the tank can be

placed back into service, following pending structural analysis and design by Preload:


1. The exterior wall shotcrete delamination marked on attached sheet R-05, mainly between

pilasters # 7 to # 1 circumferentially, needs to be removed carefully down to sound shotcrete or
to the original face of the diaphragm. Caution is required because circumferential wire under
tension can whip outwardly if it is suddenly de-bonded from the corewall substrate. The
remainder of exterior above-grade wall should be sounded to confirm that no other extensive
delamination exists. This has already been checked from finish grade to 8-feet above finish grade.
2. The coating on the inside face of the wall from the right of pilaster # 7 to the left of pilaster _it_ 1
requires removal from the wall base curb to the top of the wall, 30-feet-8-inches above floor. The
exposed inside face of wall panel joints need to be closely examined. Epoxy injections will likely
be required at least one wall panel joint.
3. The wall will require removal of the 3" pilasters and above grade paint coating in order to bond

replacement prestressing wires with new shotcrete. Pending analysis will dictate whether the
original 5-1/2" core-wall can accept the new wires without first cutting the existing wires in the
75% of the wall not affected by the problems between pilasters # 7 to #1.

Tank Data and Background:


The tank is a Ground Storage Reservoir built by Preload, Inc. in 1980-81, with a precast core wall, cast-inplace membrane concrete floor, and a 3-1/2-inch-thick dome shell roof with a 1/10 rise with a pinned
(seated) wall connection. The floor slab is 6-inches thick. The inside diameter of the reservoir is 75-feet6-inches, and the design side water depth is 30-feet. There is a 9-foot-high soil backfill/berm above the
base of the wall all around the tank.


In September 2015 the Owner reached out to Preload for recommendations on stopping a small leak in
the tank wall. Preload responded that we have had success injecting DeNeef hydrophilic grout using small
ports drilled shallow depth through exterior shotcrete for random small leaks. It's not a common practice,
but a method we have occasionally used.


A small leak at the same location described above had been identified and included for information at a
pre-bid meeting in May 2020 prior to construction of the smaller 0.6 MG tank at the same site. Additional
information from this pre-bid meeting included photographs showing three small (< 1 square foot) spalls
in the exterior wall shotcrete at the edge of one or more of the pilasters, with some corrosion of the
circumferential wires. We are informed that after the smaller new tank was constructed following the
2020 bid, the subject tank was emptied, and a barrier liner coating was applied to the interior tank floor,
walls, and dome soffit.


In September 2022, the Owner's Engineer HRC reached out to Preload Sales Manager Steven Dudle to
inquire about wall leak injection and followed up with emails in February 2023 indicating that the injection
was successful.

ribbed diaphragm on the outside face of the wall panel transitions to smooth overlapping sheet metal
plates that span the 6-3/8" to 7-3/4" gap between the panels which becomes filled with shotcrete after
all the panels are tilted into place. The overlapping plates are screwed together with seams and screws
sealed with polysulfide.

Specific Findings to date:


A plan view of the tank with numbered pilasters, dome hatch, overflow and exterior ladder is presented
on sheet R-01. A developed wall elevation showing segments between the numbered pilasters is shown
on sheets R-02 to R-05. We used these sheets to record locations of delaminations and numbered probe
locations (small destructive holes chipped into shotcrete to expose underlying prestressing wires).


The above-grade portions exterior wall and pilasters were hammer-sounded from ground to about 6 to
8-feet above grade. Hammer sounding is a technique that identifies potential delaminations between
shotcrete layers by producing a hollow drummy sound when the wall surface is tapped with the hammer
at intervals of about 8" on center. The entire above-grade exterior wall area, and face of dome ring
clockwise between pilaster # 7 to Pilaster _H_ 3 were hammer-sounded from a manlift basket.
