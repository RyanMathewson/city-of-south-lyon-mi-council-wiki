## FIRE CHIEF REPORT

Chief Conrad stated year to date, we have had 1,160 calls. As of the last Council meeting we have had 59 calls, 47 EMS, 3 hazardous, 4 public service, 1 rescue, 1 fire, and 3 false alarms. She then stated Great Lakes Ace has graciously donated $1,000 to the city. Unfortunately, the State didn't get their grant funding and the State Fire Marshall is trying to get the funding. At the beginning of the year, we got 72 smoke alarms from the state and that is based on how many they think we'll be installing, and because we had the FEMA grant 3 years earlier, those numbers weren't reported to the State so we actually got less alarms than we should have gotten. They do have carbon monoxide detectors available this month, so we'll get as many as we can. And with the $1,000 donation, we should be able to get a good number at cost. Councilmember Bogert asked about why the carbon monoxide detectors are so important during the winter. Chief Conrad stated we turn our furnaces and gas fire places on in the winter and if either aren't working properly, they can give off odorless and colorless gas that can do harm. The CO detectors, we prefer to have one on every level, and if you can't afford that, the most important place to have one would be in the sleeping area. You want the alarm to wake you up, if you have one in the kitchen and you hear it, you are already alerted. Councilmember Bogert asked if there is a specific place they should be placed. Chief Conrad stated there isn't. Carbon monoxide weighs about the same as air, and if you have a leak, it will spread throughout your house. Councilmember Squires asked how much they cost. Chief Conrad stated they can cost anywhere between $20 and $80.00.

## WATER/WASTEWATER SUPERINTENDENT REPORT

Water and Wastewater Superintendent Beason stated discussed the inspection report of the one-million-gallon storage tank. He pointed out a few things in the report. He stated the contributing factors of the wire corrosion at the location of pilaster #8. The lineup of the left edge of the formed pilaster with a smooth steel cover plate bridging the ribbed steel diaphragm at the joint between precast wall panels. The original construction sequence of forming and placement of the 3-foot-wide pilasters close to the prestressing wires before 1" shotcrete cover was placed over the wires. He then stated that is not how it should be built. Those wires would be exposed, the only thing covering them would be the pilaster. He then explained the incorporation of the formed dome ring separated with a bearing pad on top of the wall. They no longer build the roof like that; it is now one piece. He then stated in September 2015 they reached out to Preload for recommendations on stopping a small leak. Preload responded that they had success injecting hydrophilic grout using small ports drilled shallow depth through the exterior, but you have to be careful not to pierce the diaphragm. He then stated prior to that, we had a dive team in the tank looking for any cracks and sealing cracks if we found any. Prior to 2022, each one of the fixes were done with the tank holding one million gallons of water. He further stated some cracks were found, and some of the wiring was rusted and separated. Then HRC contacted Preload and asked about a wall leak, and in 2023 they indicated the injection was successful. He further stated there were wires broke or rusted. It started at 12 feet and went up to the top of the dome. That was mainly on pilaster #8. He further explained they also found delamination and corrosion on other areas. He then stated the interior of the cracks appear to be on the panel on the inside and there is significant cracking. They believe that during the last fix in 2021 it was injected and it may have opened it up more and now four years later we have the catastrophic failure on pilaster 8. He stated that is the summary of what they found. The next discussion will be to replace or repair. Councilmember Bogurt asked if the wires are like rebar. Superintendent Beason stated it is actually stretched wire. It is holding everything together because the wall is only 4 inches. They are wound under pressure and the shotcrete holds the wires in place. Councilmember Popa asked about the reference about the ladder retro fit exterior ladder. It states it doesn't have adequate connection to the tank. Superintendent Beason stated it doesn't, that would have to be replaced. Councilmember Popa asked if this prevents someone from going up there and checking on the tank. Superintendent Beason stated no one will be going up there as of now. Councilmember Kurtzweil asked if they are replacing the hole dome. Superintendent Beason stated the pilasters would be removed to see what is behind them, such as exposed wires and anything else. Further discussion was held regarding the inspection report. Councilmember Hansen stated that to repair this, they would leave the corroded wire in there and then wrap around it. Superintendent Beason stated they would have to be very careful to not let the wires release. Councilmember Bogert asked what the lifespan is on the holding tank. Superintendent Beason stated it is 50-70 years. This one is at 46 years. They don't normally see this problem. It's usually corrosion, or problems with pipes protruding through the tanks. He further stated it is a one-year warranty on a new tank, and a one-year warranty on a rebuild. He then stated it is a tough decision. Councilmember Popa asked why the dates are different on the quote and the repair quote. Superintendent Beason stated they spent 4 weeks on the repair quote. It went to all of their departments numerous times. It is dangerous for them to do and it is concerning. Councilmember Popa asked if there is anyone that can counter bid this quote. Superintendent Beason stated there are only a couple other companies that we could look at. He further stated if we decide to replace it, that is a different story. Councilmember Dilg stated he doesn't see an insurance quote in the paperwork. Superintendent Beason stated they will have their own insurance. Councilmember Squires asked if Preload is the company that originally built the tank. Superintendent Beason stated they are. Councilmember Kurtzweil stated she wouldn't be in favor of going out for bid for this. She stated this is a highly specialized area of repair, and if she were Preload, she wouldn't want this information out there. Superintendent Beason stated it is our report. He further stated there is another company that is a close competitor with Preload. If we want

# PROCLAMATION HONORING

## Family Court Awareness Month

## WHEREAS

the mission of Family Court Awareness Month is to highlight the need to prioritize child safety in family court, as 1 in 3 families in Michigan are exposed to domestic abuse; and

## WHEREAS

EAS an estimated 58,000 children in the U.S. are ordered into unsupervised contact with abusive parents each year, resulting in almost 1,000 children in the United States murdered during visitation with an abusive parent since 2008. In Michigan, 39 children have been murdered by a parent since 2008 during divorce or custody disputes; and

## WHEREAS

EREAS the mission of Family Court Awareness Month is to promote judicial education that would include training on domestic violence, child abuse, childhood trauma, coercive control, and post-separation abuse for all professionals involved in family court cases; and

## WHEREAS

REAS Family Court Awareness Month recognizes the need to incorporate evidence-based research into the court's decisions. This includes the Adverse Childhood Experiences (ACEs) Study. This research highlights long-term effects of exposure to abuse in childhood and emphasizes the need for research-informed and impartial decision-making to protect children's well-being; and

HEREAS Family Court Awareness Month aims to encourage collaboration among legislators, advocates, professionals, and the community to improve family court practices, ensuring they are guided by evidence-based research and prioritize the safety and best interests of children;

NOW, THEREFORE, I, Stephen B. Kennedy, Mayor of the City of South Lyon, do hereby declare the month of November of 2026 to be Family Court Awareness Month in the City of South Lyon.

## Alzheimer's Disease Awareness Month Proclamation

WHEREAS, Alzheimer's disease and related dementias affect more than 6 million Americans age 65 and older, and an estimated 50 million people worldwide are living with dementia — a number projected to triple by 2050; and

WHEREAS, dementia is the seventh leading cause of death globally, and its impact extends far beyond those diagnosed, affecting families, caregivers, and communities who often provide care without adequate support or resources; and

WHEREAS, Alzheimer's Disease Awareness Month was first designated in November 1983 by President Ronald Reagan, and November remains the nationally recognized month to elevate awareness, reduce stigma, and honor those living with dementia and their caregivers; and

WHEREAS, Memory Matters, a youth-led nonprofit organization works to raise awareness about dementia and build a supportive community for those affected, reaching advocates, researchers, and families across more than 35 countries through its global newsletter and researcher network; and

WHEREAS, the City of South Lyon recognizes the importance of supporting residents and families navigating the challenges of dementia, and affirms its commitment to a compassionate, informed, and inclusive community;

NOW, THEREFORE, I, Stephen B. Kennedy, Mayor of the City of South Lyon, do hereby declare the month of November, 2026 to be Dementia Awareness Month in the City of South Lyon and call upon all residents, businesses, and community organizations to join in recognizing, supporting, and honoring the millions of individuals and families living with dementia.
