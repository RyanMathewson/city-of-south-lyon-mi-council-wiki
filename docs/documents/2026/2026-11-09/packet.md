# Rules of Conduct for Public Comment at Council Meetings $ ^{*} $

Members of the public may speak at a Council meeting upon recognition by the Mayor.

At the start of a speaker's comment, the speaker shall provide his/her name and address for the record.

Public comment may only occur during periods designated on the agenda for public comment or a public hearing. A person may speak for up to two (2) minutes during each of the two public comment periods on agenda items or non-agenda items. Waivers of the time requirement may only be granted in the discretion of the Mayor, and waivers to speak at a time other than a designated comment period may only be granted by the Council. Any person wishing to make a presentation of longer than two minutes or requiring audio-visual equipment is asked to contact the City Clerk requesting to appear on a future agenda.

A person may only address Council from the podium. A person must state their name and address. Only one person may occupy the podium at a time. All remarks are to be directed to the Mayor and Council. Speakers are not to engage in direct dialog with other meeting attendees.

Any person who violates the Rules of Conduct, disturbs the peace at a meeting, and/or interferes with the meeting may be warned, ordered to be seated, removed, and/or ticketed.

## WATER/WASTEWATER SUPERINTENDENT REPORT

Water and Wastewater Superintendent Beason stated discussed the inspection report of the one-million-gallon storage tank. He pointed out a few things in the report. He stated the contributing factors of the wire corrosion at the location of pilaster #8. The lineup of the left edge of the formed pilaster with a smooth steel cover plate bridging the ribbed steel diaphragm at the joint between precast wall panels. The original construction sequence of forming and placement of the 3-foot-wide pilasters close to the prestressing wires before 1" shotcrete cover was placed over the wires. He then stated that is not how it should be built. Those wires would be exposed, the only thing covering them would be the pilaster. He then explained the incorporation of the formed dome ring separated with a bearing pad on top of the wall. They no longer build the roof like that; it is now one piece. He then stated in September 2015 they reached out to Preload for recommendations on stopping a small leak. Preload responded that they had success injecting hydrophilic grout using small ports drilled shallow depth through the exterior, but you have to be careful not to pierce the diaphragm. He then stated prior to that, we had a dive team in the tank looking for any cracks and sealing cracks if we found any. Prior to 2022, each one of the fixes were done with the tank holding one million gallons of water. He further stated some cracks were found, and some of the wiring was rusted and separated. Then HRC contacted Preload and asked about a wall leak, and in 2023 they indicated the injection was successful. He further stated there were wires broke or rusted. It started at 12 feet and went up to the top of the dome. That was mainly on pilaster #8. He further explained they also found delamination and corrosion on other areas. He then stated the interior of the cracks appear to be on the panel on the inside and there is significant cracking. They believe that during the last fix in 2021 it was injected and it may have opened it up more and now four years later we have the catastrophic failure on pilaster 8. ## Alzheimer's Disease Awareness Month Proclamation

WHEREAS, Alzheimer's disease and related dementias affect more than 6 million Americans age 65 and older, and an estimated 50 million people worldwide are living with dementia — a number projected to triple by 2050; and

WHEREAS, dementia is the seventh leading cause of death globally, and its impact extends far beyond those diagnosed, affecting families, caregivers, and communities who often provide care without adequate support or resources; and

WHEREAS, Alzheimer's Disease Awareness Month was first designated in November 1983 by President Ronald Reagan, and November remains the nationally recognized month to elevate awareness, reduce stigma, and honor those living with dementia and their caregivers; and

WHEREAS, Memory Matters, a youth-led nonprofit organization works to raise awareness about dementia and build a supportive community for those affected, reaching advocates, researchers, and families across more than 35 countries through its global newsletter and researcher network; and

WHEREAS, the City of South Lyon recognizes the importance of supporting residents and families navigating the challenges of dementia, and affirms its commitment to a compassionate, informed, and inclusive community;

NOW, THEREFORE, I, Stephen B. Kennedy, Mayor of the City of South Lyon, do hereby declare the month of November, 2026 to be Dementia Awareness Month in the City of South Lyon and call upon all residents, businesses, and community organizations to join in recognizing, supporting, and honoring the millions of individuals and families living with dementia.

# CITY OF SOUTH LYON

# PROCLAMATION HONORING

## Family Court Awareness Month

## WHEREAS

the mission of Family Court Awareness Month is to highlight the need to prioritize child safety in family court, as 1 in 3 families in Michigan are exposed to domestic abuse; and

## WHEREAS

an estimated 58,000 children in the U.S. are ordered into unsupervised contact with abusive parents each year, resulting in almost 1,000 children in the United States murdered during visitation with an abusive parent since 2008. In Michigan, 39 children have been murdered by a parent since 2008 during divorce or custody disputes; and

## WHEREAS

the mission of Family Court Awareness Month is to promote judicial education that would include training on domestic violence, child abuse, childhood trauma, coercive control, and post-separation abuse for all professionals involved in family court cases; and

## WHEREAS

EAS Family Court Awareness Month recognizes the need to incorporate evidence-based research into the court's decisions. This includes the Adverse Childhood Experiences (ACEs) Study. This research highlights long-term effects of exposure to abuse in childhood and emphasizes the need for research-informed and impartial decision-making to protect children's well-being; and

## WHEREAS

REAS Family Court Awareness Month aims to encourage collaboration among legislators, advocates, professionals, and the community to improve family court practices, ensuring they are guided by evidence-based research and prioritize the safety and best interests of children;

NOW, THEREFORE, I, Stephen B. Kennedy, Mayor of the City of South Lyon, do hereby declare the month of November of 2026 to be Family Court Awareness Month in the City of South Lyon.

# CITY OF SOUTH LYON PROCLAMATION HONORING

## JEANETTE BENSON - VOLUNTEER OF THE YEAR AWARD 2026

WHEREAS, Jeanette Benson has served the Friends of the Salem-South Lyon District Library for nearly 30 years, serving as a member of the Friends of the Library Board, as Secretary, as Vice President and as President, and

WHEREAS, Jeanette played a crucial role in establishing the beautiful gardens at the library, developing a team of 30 volunteers from the community including herself, and making it a focal point and place of beauty and peace for the community to enjoy, and

WHEREAS, Jeanette also serves as the Friends of the Library Book Room Coordinator, leading a dedicated team of 17 volunteers as well as being responsible for the budgeting and financial reporting to the Friends Board, and

WHEREAS, Jeanette ensures the book room provides a place for community members to donate books for sale, with the funds providing significant financial support for library programming, resources, marketing, and communications, and

WHEREAS, Jeanette developed innovative ways to sell donated materials outside the library through online selling services such as Thrift-books, eBay and Amazon Seller, allowing the library to offer even more to users beyond the walls of the Salem-South Lyon District Library, and

WHEREAS, Jeanette has served our community in other capacities over the years as a volunteer at Active Faith, delivering food to those in need and at the St Joseph Catholic Parish bringing communion to the homebound, and

WHEREAS, Jeanette has proven to be a true Servant Leader whose traits include: strong communication, authenticity, integrity, compassion, empowerment, continuous improvement, and always putting others first, and

NOW, THEREFORE, I, Stephen B. Kennedy, Mayor of the City of South Lyon, do hereby declare that in recognition of her exemplary volunteer service to our community, Jeanette Benson shall be the recipient of this year's Dayna Johnston Volunteer of the Year Award.

## 2026 South Lyon Area Holiday Spectacular

## Rules and Regulations

1. All entries MUST have lights, including walkers, vehicles, animals, etc. Further, all entries must be appropriately identified with banners, signs, or other identifying materials.

2. All entries will only be accepted with Holiday Spectacular Committee approval. All applicants will be considered by November 30, 2026.

3. The Holiday Spectacular committee reserves the right to reject any entry that it considers inappropriate, not in good taste, or not in the best interest of the parade. Entries representing a political organization, candidate, or controversial issue will not be considered.

4. Motor vehicle drivers must be at least 18 years old with a valid driver's license and current proof of insurance.

5. The maximum height for floats and vehicles is 13 feet, and maximum width is 8 feet 6 inches.

6. No solicitation of any kind is allowed during the event. All literature, such as flyers, brochures, and pamphlets, must be approved by the Holiday Spectacular Committee before the event.

7. All participants must move forward continually. Maneuvers, drills, routines, etc., can slow down and interrupt the parade flow. We want to keep your creativity, but ask participants to remember that the parade must always be moving forward.

8. Please limit the use of sirens and horns, and respect other participants regarding music volume, if applicable.

9. Those responsible for animals must clean up after them. Pooper-scoopers may have lights, as well.

10. All parade participants must conduct themselves in a manner that does not infringe on the rights of those living in or along the parade route and staging area.

11.  $ \underline{\text{No Santa costumes or impersonators are permitted}} $—we have invited the real Santa, and he will be attending for all to see!

## Safety Requirements

1. All parade units must maintain a distance of at least 25 feet between the units ahead of and behind them.

2. Throwing candy along the parade route is prohibited (See City Code 1988, § 9.165 and 9.196). Candy can be dropped in front of the child or handed directly to them.

3. Open flame and live fire is prohibited on all floats.

4. Straw and hay are prohibited on all floats.

5. Refueling of generators during the parade is prohibited. Fuel cans are not allowed on the entry but must be stored in the pulling vehicle. Fuel containers shall be "safety type cans" with self-closing lids.

6. Alcoholic beverages are prohibited in the staging area, on the float, and during the parade.

7. SMOKING IS PROHIBITED on or near parade entries.

8. Generators must be placed in a well-ventilated area on each parade entry. Maintain a minimum clearance of 18 inches between generators and combustible materials.

## Project Description

### Describe the physical location of the proposed project and ownership of the property. $ ^{*} $

The physical location of the proposed project is 390 S. Lafayette St. in downtown South Lyon. The proposed site is a 100-foot by 60-foot parcel located at the northwest corner of S. Lafayette St. and W. McHattie St. The City of South Lyon owns the property where the proposed project would take place. This parcel is a prominent corner in the downtown and serves as an entrance to Downtown South Lyon.

## Describe the project design and why it was chosen

This project design for a park in downtown South Lyon was chosen after considerable input from various community groups. Previously, this parcel was identified in the City of South Lyon Master Plan as a site for mixed-use, infill development. However, once the City of South Lyon conducted soil borings at the site, a presence of peat and ground water was found, resulting in a location that would be extremely costly to develop. In light of this information, the City of South Lyon, South Lyon Parks and Recreation Commission, Cultural Arts Commission, and the Downtown Development Authority began to offer input regarding the establishment of a park on the site. Feedback was sought from each of these groups to obtain an overall vision for the development of the space. Once this was completed, it was included in the City of South Lyon's updated Parks and Recreation Master Plan, which was formally adopted by City Council on December 13, 2021.

## Provide a relevant history/background information (including any environmental concerns)

The City of South Lyon purchased the property in approximately 2014. The parcel contained a building that was in disrepair and was demolished shortly after the city acquired it. Since that time, it has sat vacant with no development. Given the poor soil conditions present at this location, a downtown park is the most feasible and productive option for this site.

## Describe the community need for the project

Currently, there is no park in the DDA district. This project has been identified in the City of South Lyon's Parks and Recreation Master Plan as well as by the Downtown Development Authority. Studies have shown that parks in an urban setting provide essential environmental, aesthetic, economic, and recreation benefits. They enhance property values, increase municipal revenue, and attract homebuyers and workers to the area. This section of the downtown does not have the concentration of retail and restaurants present in the core downtown area at the intersection of Lake and Lafayette Streets. However, this park would provide an important development to the downtown. Additionally, this area will become an important area for the promotion of arts and cultural programs.

As demographics continue to shift throughout Oakland County, Michigan, and the United States, planning for an inclusionary and diverse community must be a priority so that all people may access parks that will enhance their overall quality of life. With this in mind, parks provide essential places for play cities across the country are striving to provide.
