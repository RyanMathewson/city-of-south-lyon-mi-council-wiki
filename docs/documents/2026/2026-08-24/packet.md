### 4. FIRE STATION CEMENT REPLACEMENT

Fire Chief Conrad states that they are looking to replace the cement approach, the ambulance parking area, curbing and sidewalk. This area is severely deteriorated. Funding was approved in the FY 2026-2027 budget in the amount of $100,000.00. MAC Concrete Demolition was chosen to provide this service because the contractor submitted a comprehensive written contract that clearly defines the scope of work, including the exact dimensions of the concrete to be removed and replaced, the construction methods, project phasing, and warranty of work. This level of detail provides a clear understanding of the contractor's responsibilities and minimizes the potential for misunderstandings, change orders, or disputes regarding the work to be completed. The recommended contractor also processes completing the project in two phases with a five-day concrete cure period. The longer cure time is expected to produce a more durable finished product. Chief Conrad attached 3 quotes for Council to review. MAC Concrete Demolition, $46,377.00, Copeland Paving, Inc., $46,884.00, and Boley Construction, $44,113.00. Councilmember Popa thanked all of the information Chief Conrad turned in. Councilmember Hansen stated the information was very detailed. # AGENDA NOTE New Business: Item #4

MEETING DATE: August 24, 2026

PERSON PLACING ITEM ON AGENDA: Jeff Archey, DPW Superintendent

AGENDA TOPIC: Award Cemetery Dirt Pile Removal to Nicola's Contracting.

EXPLANATION OF TOPIC: Over the years, excavation of graves has resulted in a significant accumulation of excess soil, as only approximately half of the excavated material is placed back into the graves. The remaining spoils need to be removed from the cemetery.

The timing of this work is important due to an upcoming paving project within the cemetery. While the existing soil piles are not in the way of the paving project, it is more practical to remove the material before the new roads are paved. Completing the hauling beforehand will prevent heavy trucks and hauling equipment from damaging the newly paved roads and will help protect the investment in the upcoming paving project.

Two companies submitted bids to load and haul the excess soil. The cost was also evaluated using DPW personnel to load the material, with a contractor providing only the trucking. Based on the pricing and resources required, it is more efficient and cost-effective to have one contractor handle both the loading and hauling of the material. This approach will reduce the demand on DPW staff and equipment and allow the material to be removed before the paving project is completed.
