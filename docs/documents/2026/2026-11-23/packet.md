# Rules of Conduct for Public Comment at Council Meetings $ ^{*} $

Members of the public may speak at a Council meeting upon recognition by the Mayor.

At the start of a speaker's comment, the speaker shall provide his/her name and address for the record.

Public comment may only occur during periods designated on the agenda for public comment or a public hearing. A person may speak for up to two (2) minutes during each of the two public comment periods on agenda items or non-agenda items. Waivers of the time requirement may only be granted in the discretion of the Mayor, and waivers to speak at a time other than a designated comment period may only be granted by the Council. Any person wishing to make a presentation of longer than two minutes or requiring audio-visual equipment is asked to contact the City Clerk requesting to appear on a future agenda.

A person may only address Council from the podium. A person must state their name and address. Only one person may occupy the podium at a time. All remarks are to be directed to the Mayor and Council. Speakers are not to engage in direct dialog with other meeting attendees.

Any person who violates the Rules of Conduct, disturbs the peace at a meeting, and/or interferes with the meeting may be warned, ordered to be seated, removed, and/or ticketed.

# CITY OF SOUTH LYON – BOARDS AND COMMISSIONS

City Council: Elected Office (7 Members) – Meets second and fourth Monday of the month at 7:30 pm in the Auditorium. Nominating petitions required. Petitions are available at the City Clerk's office.

Planning Commission: Appointed Office (7 Members) – Meets the second and fourth Thursdays of the month at 7:00 pm in the Auditorium. The Planning Commission prepares a Master Plan for existing and future land uses within the City; develops a Zoning Ordinance to implement the Master Plan, reviews change of land use requests, site plans and related approvals for development, and/or change of occupancy; and assists with the development of a Capital Improvement Plan.

Zoning Board of Appeals: Appointed Office (5 Members) – Meets the third Thursday of the month at 7:00 pm in the Auditorium. The ZBA is a quasi-judicial board that considers appeals, variance requests, and ordinance interpretation. Plus, two (2) alternate positions, as needed.

Cable Commission: Appointed Office (3 Members) – Meets the second Wednesday of the month at 7:00 pm in the North Conference Room. The Cable Commission is responsible for the content on the City's local government Channel 19. They record football games, interviews with local business owners, and informational content for the City.

Cultural Arts Commission: Appointed Office (6 Members) – Meets the first Thursday and third Wednesday of the month at 6:00 in the South Conference Room. The Cultural Arts Commission encourages, facilitates and promotes Culture and Art in the City, such as promotional and educational activities to increase public awareness of the necessity and desirability of visual arts, music and culture in the community.

Parks and Recreation Commission: Appointed Office (7 Members) – Meets the second Wednesday of the month at 7:00 pm in the South Conference Room. This Commission works with the City to develop a 5-year Parks and Recreation Master Plan. The Commission ensures that the Parks and Recreation Master Plan is being followed and will make recommendations for other improvements to recreation facilities.

Downtown Development Authority: Appointed Office (9 Members) – Meets the second Thursday of the month at 8:00 am in the South Conference Room. This Board develops a Downtown Development Plan and oversees the budget for tax increment financing with the DDA District.

Ethics Commission: Appointed Office (5 Members) – Will meet on an as needed basis to ensure that the City's Ethic Ordinance is being followed.

Housing Commission: Appointed Office (5 Members) – Meets the third Thursday of the month at 4:00 pm in the South Conference Room. The Housing Commission shall determine in what areas of the City it is necessary to provide proper sanitary housing facilities for families of low income and for the elimination of housing conditions which are detrimental to public peace, health, safety and/or welfare.

Historical Commission: Appointed Office (5 Members) – Meets the first Wednesday of the month at 7:30 pm at the Witch's Hat Museum. The Commission shall, in concert with the City of South Lyon and the City Manager, oversee the operation and maintenance of the Historical Village and its buildings and grounds. The Commission works to preserve the history of South Lyon by collecting materials and other objects and materials illustrative of and relating to the history of the South Lyon area, to procure and preserve narratives of the early pioneers, their exploits, perils, privations and achievements.

Road Improvement Committee: Appointed Office -- The Road Improvement Committee is established to bridge the divide between the City and its residents by communicating the Road Improvement Program and the upcoming 2022 Bond Proposal. The meetings will be held on an as needed basis to suggest information that will be conveyed to the public including information on available road funding, infrastructure improvements, etc.

Local Officers' Compensation Commission: -- The local Officers Compensation Commission consists of five members that must be registered voters in the City of South Lyon and appointed by the Mayor and Council. The commissioners are responsible for recommending the salaries of elected officials unless a resolution adopted by two thirds of City Council reject the recommendation. The commission will meet in odd years and be appointed in January. Family members and employees of the city are not eligible to be on the commission.

<table><tr><td>To:</td><td colspan="2">Ron Beason, Water Treatment Plant Superintendent</td></tr><tr><td>From:</td><td colspan="2">HRC Process and Structural Department</td></tr><tr><td>Date:</td><td colspan="2">November 17, 2026</td></tr><tr><td>Subject:</td><td>Water Ground Storage Tank Replacement Study</td><td>HRC Job No. 20260562</td></tr></table>

The following provides a summary of our findings from the Water Ground Storage Tank 1MG study that Hubbell, Roth & Clark, Inc. (HRC) performed for the City of South Lyon. The purpose of the study was to provide ground water storage tank alternatives to replace the existing damaged 1-million-gallon (MG) prestressed concrete tank. Each alternative was reviewed for benefits and disadvantages, constructability, estimated construction cost, estimated operational and maintenance costs, and Net Present Worth Analysis.

## Background and Introduction

The existing South Lyon Water Treatment Plant provides water for the City of South Lyon. Groundwater is treated and stored in either the 1 MG ground storage tank or 600,000-gallon ground storage tank, prior to high service pumps conveying potable water to the City's 500,000-gallon elevated storage tank for distribution.

The existing 1 MG ground storage tank was installed approximately in 1980 and is a prestressed concrete tank. In July, 2026, City personnel identified an exterior crack on the 1 MG storage tank. The storage tank was taken out of service for structural inspections by the existing tank manufacturer, Preload LLC. Preload concluded that prestressed wire corrosion was found on multiple tank wall pilasters and premature failure was due to outdated design and construction methodology.

It is HRC's understanding that the City wants to replace this 1 MG storage tank with a similar size storage tank and within the footprint of the existing tank. HRC did not consider repairing the existing tank or replacement in a separate location in this memorandum.

## Proposed Tank Options

HRC identified two alternative types of ground storage tanks to replace the existing damaged 1 MG tank.

1. Replace in-kind with prestressed concrete tank.

2. Replace with bolted or welded steel tank.

Both options assumed complete removal and replacement of the existing tank foundation floor and the proposed storage tanks would mimic in similar size and function as the existing 1 MG storage tank. The benefits and disadvantages, and most probable design of each option, are presented in the following section.

## Option #1: Prestressed Concrete Tank

This option involves the removal of the existing 1 MG prestressed concrete tank and foundation, and replacement in-kind with a new prestressed concrete tank of equal size. Construction would involve a temporary earth retaining system to prevent damage to nearby structures. Nearby yard piping and utilities would be removed and replaced, or protected during construction excavation. The two most recognized prestressed concrete tank manufacturers include Preload LLC, and DN Tank LLC.
