### 1. Moratorium on data centers

Interim City Manager, Doug Baaki states that council and residents have expressed concerns regarding data processing centers and their environmental impacts, including potential increases in electricity costs, strain on local water supplies, and noise disruption. A temporary moratorium will halt new applications and permits for data processing centers for a period of 180 days. This will allow the city and its consultants to review and update its City Code of Ordinances, including the Zoning Ordinance. Baaki adds that a copy of the resolution is attached to the agenda note. He states that the possible courses of action is to approve or not approve the resolution pertaining to Data Processing Centers. We have Attorney Kristen Colb and also Planner Hannah Smith if council has any questions.

Councilmember Squires questions if we can legally extend the 180 days to 365 days. Attorney Colb confirms that moratoriums can be extended. If the city is actively taking a look at the matter and needs more time to conclude its study, council may come back and extend the moratorium. Councilmember Dilg questions if it is possible to put that in now. Colb states that they can change the time period before you adopt the resolution as well. Dilg stated that the ability to petition to have a hearing in front of city council, is this standard and do we have to have it in here? Colb states that they would definitely recommend to leave this language in because it protects the city from a due process violation claim and potentially a 5th amendment takings of property claim. Another words, if a moratorium in in place, if you have a property owner who has purchased property and is in the planning and development process for a data center and let's say they were planning on submitting their application tomorrow or next week, or in March, they would have a claim because they have spent money, expended time and have certain expectations that they can proceed assuming it's a permitted use and or to apply for a special land use or whatever the city zoning ordinance requires. If they can successfully demonstrate to a court that they have been harmed because of the moratorium then they can get damages against the city. Colb states that contrary to having the language in there is a problem, a legal issue for the city, it's their opinion that the opposite is true. Without that language, you are potentially setting the city up for a nasty lawsuit. Dilg questions if this would only be in effect if somebody currently has land that they want to use for that. Colb states that this provision gives them the opportunity to come to council and demonstrate that they have an issue and therefore the moratorium should not apply. It will depend on how much time they have spent and how much money they have spent and how much work they have done to advance a data center in the city. If they wake up tomorrow and decide they want to start looking into it, that's probably not going to qualify them as aggrieved under Michigan law. Again, if you've got someone out there that you are not aware of, who has purchased property and doing planning and hired architects and designers, they could come and say that they have spent X amount of dollars and if they can't move ahead, they will lose money and their business. Dilg confirms with Colb that if they came to a meeting and we denied it anyway, we are still going to get sued. Colb said potentially and it will very much depend on what the circumstances are. Dilg adds that she understands the legal standpoint, and I don't think that there is anybody right now that is doing this in South Lyon, but there could be and this has become a huge community issue. And she would really hate to be on the side of now we have to let this data center in because they are aggrieved and we left this loophole in there. Colb states that this doesn't allow them to have a data center. It allows them to come to council and attempt to demonstrate that they've been harmed, that their due process rights have been violated, and they potentially have a takings claim. They still have to come in and submit an application and go through the process. This is not a jump to the end of the process. They would still have to submit all the applications, get all the fees, and go through all the public hearings before and if they are able to get approved.
