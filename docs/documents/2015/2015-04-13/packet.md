## OLD BUSINESS

1. Second Reading: Amendment to the City of South Lyon Ordinance adding regulations concerning the Medical Use of Marihuana and related land uses and activities

Attorney Wilhelm stated at the last meeting there was a comment made regarding a section in the ordinance regarding the inspection of the facilities by City officials. He has done additional research and the Medical Marihuana Act states having a registered care giver card does not give probable cause for inspection and that section in the ordinance should be removed. He stated there may be another way for the City to do inspections to ensure the business is staying in compliance, possibly by some way of the special land use issue to ensure the location is in compliance. Councilman Kramer stated he is concerned regarding how we can ensure they are in compliance with the act, more than the law enforcement part of it. He stated he would like some way for the City to ensure compliance, and right now our Ordinance is silent regarding that. There should be some avenue for us to ensure they are in compliance. Councilman Wedell stated he is concerned with this as well. Some communities are dealing with issues of non-compliance regarding the number of plants allowed, and he would like to see something that will ensure they are in compliance. Councilman Kivell stated the fact they put these metrics in the act, why would the numbers be there if we do not have the ability to ensure they do not have more plants than allowed? We should be able to validate that they are not exceeding the law. Councilman Kivell stated it would be helpful to know what the repercussions are if they are not in compliance, and if they could lose their license. Attorney Wilhelm stated if they violate, we could revoke the special land use. He further stated depending on the violation, it could also be a law enforcement issue. Councilman Kramer stated he would like to see language in the ordinance regulating the zoning use, or land use, in some way to ensure they are in compliance.

## Arbor Day Foundation Names South Lyon Tree City USA

South Lyon, MI was named a 2014 Tree City USA by the Arbor Day Foundation in honor of its commitment to effective urban forest management.

South Lyon achieved Tree City USA recognition by meeting the program's four requirements: a tree board or department, a tree-care ordinance, an annual community forestry budget of at least $2 per capita and an Arbor Day observance and proclamation.

The Tree City USA program is sponsored by the Arbor Day Foundation, in partnership with the U.S. Forest Service and the National Association of State Foresters.

"Everyone benefits when elected officials, volunteers and committed citizens in communities like South Lyon make smart investments in urban forests," said Matt Harris, chief executive of the Arbor Day Foundation. "Trees bring shade to our homes and beauty to our neighborhoods, along with numerous economic, social and environmental benefits."

Cleaner air, improved storm water management, energy savings and increased property values and commercial activity are among the benefits enjoyed by Tree City USA communities.

More information on the program is available at  $ \underline{\text{arborday.org/TreeCityUSA}} $.

About the Arbor Day Foundation: The Arbor Day Foundation is a million member nonprofit conservation and education organization with the mission to inspire people to plant, nurture, and celebrate trees. More information is available at arborday.org.

The City of South Lyon is located near the crossroads of Interstate 96 and US-23 in the southwest corner of Oakland County. Lansing, the capital of Michigan, is located less than an hour drive west, and Detroit is just 45 miles to the east.

The center of the business district is at Ten Mile Road and Pontiac Trail, known as Lake Street and Lafayette Street within the city. At just 3.5 square miles in size, the city offers small town living with the convenience of nearby interstate highways, major metropolitan areas and international airports.

Originally established as an agricultural center built around two railroads and incorporated as a city in 1930, South Lyon is becoming a center for manufacturing with 70 acres of light industrial properties located within the city limits. South Lyon's utility systems service residents and businesses, an important feature unavailable in surrounding areas. Known for its historic and charming downtown, annual festivals, top-notch parks and excellent schools, the City of South Lyon offers an unparalleled quality of life.

## CALLS FOR SERVICE

A Call for Service is an event occurring in the City of South Lyon to which a South Lyon Police Officer must respond to evaluate or take action, or an event that comes to the attention of police or is initiated by police that requires formal documentation (e.g., case report, supplemental report or accident report).

Examples: Citizens reporting a theft, citizens reporting a traffic accident with injuries, citizens reporting a burglary, citizens reporting a ringing alarm bell, officers finding a stolen vehicle, detectives making an arrest, officers searching out and arresting a wanted subject, officers stopping citizens for traffic violations, officer field interviews of persons observed in suspicious circumstances, citizens reporting a neighbor problem or suspicious activity.

South Lyon Police Officers documented 10,263 calls for service during the year, which is an increase of 4,745 calls from 2013.

# LIABILITY TRAINING

*[Image or figure on this page; see the original.]*

The one day course presented by Keith Wuotinen on May 1st provided police supervisors at all levels and ranks with an overview of the legal liability we face as supervisors and leaders. The course covered Michigan and Federal Laws, including legal theories used against police supervisors and their agencies. Concepts such as gross negligence, intentional torts, deliberate indifference, and 42 USC Sec 1983 were discussed, among others. In addition to providing a legal framework that is essential to understanding our liability as police supervisors, the course offered practical techniques and tips that supervisors can utilize to help minimize risk. Sixty command officers/supervisors from all over the state were represented. As a result of hosting the class at the City Hall Auditorium, six command officers received free training for the one-day course, a savings of $900.

# CADET PROGRAM

In order to provide an enhanced level of customer service to community members, the South Lyon Police department maintains a Police Cadet Program. The position of police cadet is a part-time, non-sworn, uniformed function which increases efficiencies by:

1) Providing a police department employee at the front desk as needed.

2) Performing functions that allow for “re-deployment” of Uniformed Division Officers and Supervisors to patrol duties throughout the city.

3) Providing an avenue for Police cadets to gain valuable experience and training in preparation for a career in law enforcement.

# SCHOOL SAFETY

SLPD

2014

As many kids walk into school each morning and head home in the afternoon, there's an extra uniform or two in eyesight. For students at the public schools within the South Lyon city limits, a police officer is on hand to monitor the situation. Not that there's any problems, but Police Chief Lloyd Collins wants to assure students, parents and staff that protecting the schools is a big part of the police department's job. "We take our responsibility to the schools seriously," he said. That includes South Lyon High School, both middle schools, the early childhood center and Bartlett and Sayre elementaries. Officers are on hand during the start and dismissal times at each building. "The various buildings have staggered start times," Collins explained. "That allows us to do what we do." Whether it's Columbine or Sandy Hook, school shootings nationwide in recent years have placed more focus on school security for schools both near and far. South Lyon Community Schools is in its second year of increased entrance door security at all of its schools – other schools are located in Lyon, Green Oak and Salem townships. During school hours, all exterior school doors are locked. Visitors must buzz in to the main office and show a picture ID before entry is granted. The schools are most vulnerable when those doors are unlocked before and after school, which is why the officers are there. "It's a target-rich environment," Collins said. "We're very happy the schools went to that (buzzer) system." And since Collins directed his staff to stop by the schools every day, the response from school officials and the community has been positive, the police chief added. "People would just roll by and say they appreciate us being there, making it a priority," Collins said.

## ~Multiple visits~

Officers added the daily stops last school year. In 2014, Lt. Chris Sovik said there have been more than 1,400 school checks. It's not just before and after school that officers make their presence seen. They stop by while students are in class or at lunch, as well. "Every school, every day, gets at least one walk-through," Sovik said. "It's unpredictable. It's not the same time every day." Depending on the location and time of day, those walks could last 5-20 minutes. The visits also give officers a better understanding of the school layout. Often times it's through empty halls but other times it's difficult for officers to get from one end to the other. "Most of the time, students will approach the officer," said Sovik. "They may say something silly or want to get a picture. Often they're just saying hi." Superintendent Bill Pearson said the extra attention from authorities is welcome. "We are very grateful to the South Lyon Police Department to be around the entrance and exiting times of the school day," he said. "Everybody appreciates it: the principals, the staff, the parents, the kids. It's good for everyone to know the police are always nearby. I have never heard any negative comments or suggestions in that regard." And police officials said they appreciate the support from the schools. Police officers have their own entry cards to the buildings, so even during a lockdown situation police can get into the school building, said Collins. Police are also active during school drills like tornadoes and lockdowns. It benefits the police to see how the school population will react during a crisis situation, as well as give school staff an extra set of eyes on its procedures. "It's like an inspection process, as well," said Sovik. "They want feedback on what we see. If there are any issues, that's the time we want it to happen." South Lyon Police took part in this past summer in school shooting training with real-life scenarios at Centennial Middle Schools. That gave emergency personnel a chance to work together on new procedures when handling crises, said Collins.

## ~Visual deterrent~

Having police at the schools multiple times a day is one of several preventative measures Collins said his department uses to prevent crime in the city. Marked cars at the schools during various times of the day, as well as throughout the city, reminds would-be wrongdoers that there is a strong police presence within the city, Collins reasoned. “There’s a good possibility they will go somewhere else to (commit a crime) because of visual deterrent,” said Collins. There’s no data that can quantify uncommitted crimes, he added. There have been occasions where the officer’s presence was needed. An officer used an automatic external defibrillator to save a teen girl’s life. An officer sitting outside the middle schools was alerted to a pair of students spraying graffiti on the back of a strip mall. And they’ve been on hand to help restrain students, drug possession charges and altercations at the football games. And both Collins and Sovik said they are happy to keep their involvement limited to those types of relatively-minor issues. But it’s the “what if” moments they continue to prepare and train for. “If we didn’t think those things (like school shootings) could happen here, we wouldn’t be doing those things we’re doing,” said Sovik.

2014

## FREEDOM OF INFORMATION REQUESTS

SLPD

2014

The Michigan Freedom of Information Act (FOIA) provides all persons, except those incarcerated in correctional facilities, with access to public records. Requests to inspect or receive records must be submitted in writing, and must describe the requested records with enough detail that we can identify them. You will receive a prompt written response to your request, either granting your request or explaining why all or part of your request cannot be granted. A fee will be charged for processing your request, which must be paid in advance. The South Lyon Police Department processed 162 Freedom of Information requests during 2014, an increase of 35 from 2013.

# CITIZEN'S POLICE ACADEMY SLPD 2014

*[Image or figure on this page; see the original.]*

Use of Force Instructor Sgt. Doug Baaki with Department AR-15 Rifle

The South Lyon, Novi, Walled Lake, Wixom and Wolverine Lake Police Departments created the Lakes Area Citizens Police Academy, a 30-hour instructional course designed to give the public a working knowledge of police department personnel and policies. It was created to provide citizens with information to enable informed judgments about the police department and police activity and hoped to increase rapport between the police and the community through this educational process. Through interaction with the students, police departments become more aware of the concerns of the communities they serve. There is no cost to the students who attend the Academy. The 2014 class was the 36th Session of the Academy since its inception. Of the thirty-six citizens that signed up and attended in 2014, six of them either lived or worked in the City of South Lyon. As a component of the Community Oriented Policing Program, participation in the Lakes Area Citizens Police Academy is a highly regarded public relations activity. The Police Department will continue participation with partner agencies.

# 2015 GOALS

SLPD

2014

• Due to the continuing threat of active shooter situations and incidents of terrorism, the Police Department will persist in collaborating with local, state, and federal enforcement and intelligence agencies and fusion centers. Special emphasis and asset allocation will be placed upon surveillance and protection of vulnerable targets including schools and critical infrastructure such as water, sewer, and energy systems. As part of our association with OakTac consortium, officers will continue to train on large and small scale exercises with outside agencies in response to major incidents requiring mutual aid. The Police Department will continue coordinated active shooter training exercises with the South Lyon Fire Department.

• Police Department personnel will continue school safety checks and surveillance. Checks include all public schools within the city. Uniform officers are present on school property during student arrival and dismissal times, and also perform walk-through checks on a daily basis. Detectives also conduct random surveillance checks of schools during school hours.

• The Police Department Policy and Procedure manual is continually reviewed and evaluated with respect to current court decisions and liability exposure. With the assistance of the City Attorney's Office, the manual will be revised as appropriate.

• The Police Department's performance evaluations have been revised and implemented. Command Officers will continue to monitor the progress and performance of personnel at all levels to ensure compliance with performance standards and expectations.

• The Police department will continue to utilize social media outlets (Facebook, Twitter & Nixle) in addition to the department website and the local news media in order to facilitate information sharing. The information will include crime prevention materials, safety tips, weather advisories, and general information.

• The Police Department will continue the development of department members through training, and by encouraging and supporting furthered college education. We will continue to strengthen staff skill levels and morale, and continue with staff meetings to ensure department expectations are defined and met.
