MEETING DATE: September 9, 2015

PERSON PLACING ITEM ON AGENDA: City Manager

AGENDA TOPIC: Work Session – Streets and Roads

EXPLANATION OF TOPIC: The City of South Lyon's street and road infrastructure has been formally assessed using the RoadSoft software and rating each street section with the PASER rating to provide the Council with an understanding of the current condition of our infrastructure and the need to make improvements. The Roadway Asset Management Assessment also develops a unique understanding of the condition of the roads, the average rating of the City's streets, and develops several potential strategies for the Council to consider when adopting the final report including identifying the long term plan the council has for maintaining and improving the streets and roads long term within the City Limits.

In addition to the RAM Assessment I have included a couple of additional documents for your review. The first is a breakdown of the Taxable values for houses in the City of South Lyon as of the 2015 Assessment year. This breaks the housing values down into four quartiles again it is the taxable not the assessable valuation of the houses. This first sheet also shows what the approximate annual impact of several different potential dedicated street mill levy amounts would have on property owners in each quartile. This is the first step in educating people about the cost to improve and maintain our roads to certain levels. The next sheet is a spreadsheet that shows based upon the current overall valuation for the City of South Lyon not accounting for the build out of the current developments that are either under construction: Charleston Park and Knolls or the potential developments of Jamestown or Jamie Vista with a modest 3% valuation increase instead what the City can anticipate their SEV/Taxable valuation will be over the next 10 years. It also shows what that will mean in terms of the value of a single mill in property tax. The last spreadsheet shows what the City current receives from the State in terms of revenue sharing for Major and Local Streets and our approximate annual Tri-party allocation. For this spreadsheet I used a 2% increase for inflationary measures to indicate what we under the current state programs can expect to see from the State to help pay for local and major streets which does not include the streets shared with RCOC, nor does it cover streets designated private in private subdivisions.

MATERIALS ATTACHED AS SUPPORTING DOCUMENTS: HRC RAM Assessment, three spreadsheets, HRC powerpoint

## Section 2 - Introduction

Act 199 of the State of Michigan Public Acts of 2007 encourages all departments, Counties and municipalities to develop an Asset Management Plan for their roadways. According to Act 199, asset management is defined as "an ongoing process of maintaining, upgrading, and operating physical assets cost-effectively, based on a continuous physical inventory and condition assessment." The use of asset management can improve an agency's performance, cost-effectiveness, communication, accountability and credibility. Each County Road Commission, City and Village in the State of Michigan is required to submit an Asset Management Plan to the Michigan Transportation Asset Management Council (TAMC) annually which includes a multi-year program. The process for developing an asset management plan is outlined in the TAMC's Asset Management Guide for Local Agencies in Michigan and includes the following tasks:

1. Assess current conditions

2. Create a mix of fixes, estimate costs and funding levels

3. Predict future condition and develop performance measures and targets

4. Conduct tradeoff analysis and identify candidate projects

5. Set priorities and develop a multi-year program

6. Report results

Hubbell, Roth & Clark, Inc. (HRC) developed the City of South Lyon's Asset Management Plan consistent with the tasks provided by TAMC as described in the following sections.

## Section 3 - Task 1 - Condition Assessment

The first step is to assess the current condition of the 27 miles of roadway under the City's jurisdiction. The City of South Lyon has adopted the Pavement Surface Evaluation and Rating (PASER) system for measuring its pavement condition. PASER uses visual inspection to evaluate pavement surface conditions and assigns a rating number based on the pavement material and types of deterioration present. The PASER ratings range from 1, which means the road is failing, to 10, which means the road is in excellent condition.

##  $ \underline{\text{Data Collection}} $

After coordinating the evaluation process to the City's DPW Superintendent, HRC staff spent an entire day in August assessing the current conditions of all the City's roads. The HRC team of 2 drove each road maintained by the City of South Lyon and recorded its PASER rating. The roads which are maintained by the Road Commission for Oakland County (RCOC) are federal aid roads, and rated by the Southeast Michigan Council of Governments (SEMCOG). Any off-street parking is provided behind the building, as opposed to lots fronting the street. This form of development also relies on both public and on-street parking within 500 feet of the subject site.

By holding on to the property at 390 S. Lafayette Street, the City will have a piece of the development puzzle for that block and can use it as an incentive to entice developers. The sale of the property now would limit the ability of the City to encourage new development there in the future.

The current zoning requirements, however, are not in-line with the proposed City of South Lyon Master Plan and Zoning Ordinance.

These zoning regulations will allow for a range of uses typical for a traditional downtown and also permit the use of both public lot and on-street parking within 500 feet of the site.

## ARTICLE 8 CENTRAL BUSINESS DISTRICT (B-1)

### Sec. 102-110 Intent

The Central Business District (B-1) is intended to provide for a traditional mixture of small office buildings, specialty retail stores, entertainment, public spaces, and related activities that are mutually supporting and serve the needs of both the City and surrounding communities. The intent of these district regulations is to encourage a lively social environment and economically viable downtown with a wide variety of uses in a pedestrian oriented unified setting, with shared parking. The district makes special provisions for vertical zoning, allowing the upper floors to be used as residential dwellings. The B-1 District is also designated as a Planned Unit Development (PUD) which covers the entire Central Business District to help ensure that the development/redevelopment of sites matches the Preliminary PUD Plan for Downtown South Lyon, is cohesive with surrounding uses, and meets higher site design standards for this district.
