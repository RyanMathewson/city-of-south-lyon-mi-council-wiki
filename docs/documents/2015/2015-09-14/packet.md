### 2. Consider adopting renewal of Huron Valley Ambulance contract

Chief Kennedy stated the HVA tri party contract between the City, Lyon Township and Huron Valley Ambulance came up for renewal earlier this year. Lyon Township desired to increase their transport frequency and ability. Later this year, Lyon Townships Fire Department will receive a new ambulance the Township has purchased. Lyon Township sent notice they wanted to terminate the contract, which opened the contract for negotiations, and through several meetings, we were able to come up with an agreement for all three parties which allows Lyon Township to do more of their transporting than in previous contracts. He stated his prospective is the City is very happy with HVA; they have been an outstanding community partner. He further stated if Lyon Township decided to terminate the tri party contract, we may have had to look at other options; there is a mutual benefit of the City and the Township using the same ambulance service provider. Councilman Wedell asked if there is a change in cost to the City. Chief Kennedy stated there is not. Councilman Kramer asked what if Lyon Township decides to provide their own ambulance services to their residents in the future, where would that leave us. Chief Kennedy stated he doesn't foresee that in the near future, but there are a lot of issues that would need to be worked out. If that did happen we could still stay with HVA, they cover Green Oak Township and Washtenaw County as well. He had confirmation early on from Dale Berry if Lyon Township did cancel the contract, HVA would remain with the City. Chief Kennedy stated he is happy with this contract. Councilman Kivell asked why Lyon Township doesn't do the major emergency events, is it a training issue, or time intensity issue. Chief Kennedy stated it is a training level issue. Right now the City and Lyon Township both operate on basic life support, but we cannot do cardiac monitoring, or advanced airways, so for someone that is acutely ill they need the services HVA can supply. Dale Berry of HVA stated they are a non-profit nationally accredited agency. He stated they are based in Ann Arbor and serve 144 municipalities. He stated he was a City Police Officer for South Lyon in 1976-1981. He further stated he was the ambulance Chief when the City owned their own ambulance. Mr. Berry gave a brief history of the ambulance services in the City of South Lyon. Mr. Berry stated they have served South Lyon since 1990. He spoke with Lyon Township and two changes were made to the contract because Lyon Township wanted some flexibility if the HVA ambulance was too far away, they wanted to use their own ambulance. Another change is that we have agreed to improve ambulance to fire truck communication and they are working on that currently. He further stated he has spoken with Lannie Young from Lyon Township and they do not plan on getting into the ambulance business, it is very expensive. He further stated the HVA gets revenue from the patients' health insurance. He further stated the current agreement is very solid. He further stated HVA crosses municipal boundaries every day. Councilman Ryzyi stated he is impressed with the collaboration with our Fire Truck and the HVA.

### 3. Consider adopting use of Unsafe structure placards

Chief Kennedy stated earlier this summer the Fire Department responded to a call and from the outside of the house, everything looked fine, but once inside they found a very hazardous hoarding situation. He stated they were able to get the homeowner some much needed help. Had our crew entered this house to respond to a fire in the middle of the night, they would have had no way of knowing the dangerous situation inside. He has spoken with other Fire Chiefs across the State regarding this issue and he is asking Council to allow the use of unsafe structure placards to be placed on buildings that have a hazardous situation. This is a literal red flag to our staff that there is an unsafe issue inside. Chief Kennedy stated this is permitted with the current code. He further stated this is a good idea, but if Council is not supportive, he will not move forward. Chief Kennedy stated in speaking with other Fire Chiefs that currently use these placards, it has caused some of the local businesses to clean up the buildings; therefore it has helped in that way as well. Councilman Ryzyi stated he thinks the City has been lacking with not insisting businesses keep their buildings up. He further stated we need to protect our Firefighters we need to do this, and if it prompts the business owners to clean up their building, that is good too. Councilman Kramer asked why we need to add a scarlet letter by the Fire Department, why can't we force the business owners to tear the building down. He further stated he believes we should do as Brighton has done in the past and take the building owners to court and force them to clean it up or tear it down. He believes this is a good first step, but we should be able to do more. City Manager Ladner stated we are currently looking at Brighton's Ordinance and we are looking at adopting the International Property Maintenance Code. Councilman Wedell stated he is supportive of this, but will we go back again to check the property to see if the building has been improved. Chief Kennedy stated they would check the building annually, but he is also hoping to be in contact with the business/property owners more than annually. City Attorney Wilhelm stated he believes the placards will go hand in hand with Building Code and property maintenance code enforcement, which will offer the potential for additional legal action for the City to force a property owner to make their buildings safe. Council agreed to allow Chief Kennedy to use hazardous placards when necessary.

### 9. Consider demolition bids for 390 S Lafayette

City Manager Ladner stated we received 2 bids for demolition; we were not able to get a bid package out for rehabilitation. She further stated she believes we need more time to get additional complete bids. She feels Council should reject the bids and we will advertise once again with additional time to get more bids. City Manager Ladner stated she received a letter from the code enforcement officer stating the building is rotting out with a horrible stench. Councilman Kivell stated just because it is dirty doesn't mean it cannot be refurbished. He further stated it is too short of a timeline to gather good information. He stated good decisions are made by actually thinking things through and seeing the possibilities rather than rushing and acting radically.

Councilman Wedell stated he wants to disclose he knows Mr. Hoffman who is one of the bidders. Mr. Hoffman worked as a County Commissioner when he worked for the Board of Commissioners, but that will not forfeit him from the discussion or vote. He further stated the two bids appear to be based on two entirely different bases. One was based on a tour and one was with remediation as bests and lead paint, the other one just stated there would be additional fees. He stated he agrees with the City Manager that we reject the two bids that were received and re-bid the demolition project and require they either tour the building or include any remediation with the bid.

Councilmember Kopkowski stated she is all for getting rid of blight, but she would like to see all aspects of all information. She stated she is not against tearing it down, but we need to look at what will happen after it is torn down. There is a change in zoning ordinance that needs to be taken into consideration. We need more information then we should make a decision. Councilman Ryzyi stated he agrees that we need to give more time for bids to come in, but he does want this item on the next Council Agenda. Councilman Kramer stated he is worried about the liability, so he wants something done sooner than later, the overhang is falling down.

# AGENDA NOTE New Business: Item #

MEETING DATE: September 14, 2015

PERSON PLACING ITEM ON AGENDA: Robert J. Martin, Water/Sewer Department Superintendent

AGENDA TOPIC: Purchase of three (3) Kaeser Bare Blowers

EXPLANATION OF TOPIC: The way we treat our wastewater is through biological reduction with the addition of oxygen. The oxygen is supplied by four large 125 HP mechanical Kaeser blowers. These large pieces of equipment alternate run time of 24 hours per day, 7 days per week. Although we perform daily maintenance breakdowns still occur. When this occurs the manufacturer representative helps services the failed equipment. Most times blowers can be repaired, however other times repairs would exceed the cost of a new blower. Currently Kaeser blower #4 needs to be replaced immediately.

As you may remember the Clean Water Plant was renovated in 2005 with the new biological method of treatment. At that time Kaeser was chosen as the brand of pump which would be used to service the Plant. Not long after operations began, service and maintenance issues occurred with the blower system. We have continuously serviced, maintained and communicated with Kaeser and the local rep from Professional Pump, Inc.. With this said much due-diligence and research has taken place at the plant between the workers and numerous vendors. Quotes have been received and blueprints have been created only to finally realize that in order to change product manufacturer footprints would need to be changed and very large dollar amounts be spent. (See attached quote from Kerr Pump & Supply)

We feel that after much investigating the Kaeser product has been improved with better bearings and mechanical seals which we feel will meet the requirements of our needed service. Upon meeting with the Kaeser rep he then quoted us with a one-time purchase discount on either complete units or just bare blowers. Since we have all the existing units in place the purchase of bare blowers at this time will service the Plant going forward for years to come.

As stated earlier, four blowers service the plant. With the purchase of three bare blowers one will be put into service immediately and two others will be in inventory until needed. We can purchase these units for a special pricing of $11,020.75 each, total price of three bare blowers is $33,062.25. I have budgeted for this purchase in the 2015/2016 fiscal budget.

Model7000PD PLUS heavy duty industrial blowers are designed for high performance applications, up to 18 PSIG pressure boost or 17" Hg dry vacuum (24" Hg water injected).

## 17 /46 Series

This series has wide application in pneumatic conveying, wastewater treatment, and the general process industry where high pressure, high volume air is required. Seal areas are vented to atmosphere to relieve process pressure against the internal lip seals, and to provide oil-free air.

## 57 /81 Series (Single Envelope Gastight)

This series is utilized in such applications as closed loop pneumatic conveying, fuel or process gas handling, or elevated pressure applications up to 100 PSIG discharge. Vent openings are tapped and plugged to prevent gas leakage, or may be plumbed to the inlet port. These fittings can also accept an inert gas purge for positive containment of the process gas.

## 64 /67 Series (Double Envelope Gastight)

This series is built to laboratory standards where virtually complete sealing is required. In addition to the features shown on the 57/81 series, the drive shaft is mechanically sealed and the oil sumps are plugged to provide an even higher degree of leakage protection.

## Flexibility by Design

Detachable mounting feet allow field conversion from horizontal to vertical flow, or vice versa without additional components.

### 7 :81. - Permit required

No person shall go from place to place or remain in a public place or place open to the public soliciting contributions or selling goods for the benefit or purported benefit of a charitable, religious or educational organization or cause without first obtaining a permit.

### 7 :82. - Application for permit

Application for a charitable solicitation permit shall be made on forms supplied by the Mayor. The forms shall require the applicant to indicate the nature of the organization and the date, location and means of solicitation proposed. Applicants may be required to supply such additional information as the Mayor determines is necessary to assure that applicants comply with this chapter.

### 7 :83. - Issuance of permit

The Mayor shall issue a solicitation permit if he can make the following determinations:

(1) The solicitors will fairly represent to the public the purpose of their solicitations.

(2) The solicitors or the organizations for which they are soliciting have not, within the previous 12 months, misrepresented the purpose of solicitations.

(3) The solicitation will be in compliance with all state, federal and local laws and ordinances, including those specified in this chapter.

(4) The time and location of the solicitation will not coincide with special events or other solicitations in a manner that will cause traffic or vehicle congestion.

### 7 :84. - Revocation of permit

The Mayor may revoke a solicitation permit upon a determination that it is being used in violation of this chapter.

### 7 :85. - Appeals

The decision of the Mayor to deny or to revoke a solicitation permit may be appealed to the City Council.

### 7 :86. - Regulations

The Mayor may adopt regulations to implement this chapter.

### 7 :87. - Duration of permits

Permits shall be valid for the date indicated on the permit. Permits for door-to-door solicitation in residential areas may be issued for up to 1 year. Permits for other forms of solicitation may be valid for up to 60 days.

### 7 :88. - Prohibited solicitation practices

No person engaged in soliciting that is regulated by this chapter shall do any of the following:

(1) Solicit while not carrying a copy of the solicitation permit.

## THE CITY OF NOVI ORDAINS:

PART I. That Chapter 22, "Offenses," Article I, "In General," Section 22-7, "Begging in public places," of the City of Novi Code of Ordinances is hereby amended to read as follows in its entirety:

### 22 -7. Begging in public places

(1) Except as otherwise provided in this code of ordinances, or as otherwise authorized by law, no person shall beg or solicit for the immediate payment of money or goods from another person under any of the following circumstances:

a. Within 20 feet of a bank or automated teller (ATM) machine.

b. By soliciting a patron at a sidewalk café without first having obtained the permission of the operator of the café.

c. By knowingly making a false and misleading representation in the course of a solicitation.

d. In a manner that constitutes aggressive begging, which shall mean the following:

(1) Touching the solicited person without that person's consent.

(2) Blocking the path of the person being solicited, or the entrance to any building or vehicle.

(3) Engaging in repeated requests after a refusal by the person being solicited.

Sec. 46-12. - Aggressive begging, soliciting and panhandling.

(a) Definitions. The following words, terms and phrases, when used in this section, shall have the meanings ascribed to them in this section:

Aggressive manner shall mean:

(1) Touching or causing physical contact with another person or their property without that person's consent;

(2) Blocking the path of a person or vehicle being solicited, or the entrance to a building or vehicle;

(3) Following behind, alongside or ahead of a person who walks away from the solicitor after being solicited:

(4) Using abusive language, either during the solicitation or following a refusal to donate, or making any statement, gesture, or other communication which would cause a reasonable person to be fearful or feel compelled to make a donation;

(5) Soliciting any person within 20 feet of the entrance, or in the parking area of, any bank, automated teller machine, automated teller machine facility, check cashing business, gas station, fast food drive-thru, or outdoor seating areas of any café, restaurant or other business, without the permission of the owner.

Beg, begging or panhandling shall mean asking for money or objects of value, with the intention that the money or object be transferred at that time and at that place.

Solicit or soliciting shall include using the spoken, written or printed word, bodily gestures, signs, or other means of communication with the purpose of obtaining an immediate donation of money or other thing of value the same as begging or panhandling and also include the offer to immediately exchange and/or sell any goods or services.

(b) Prohibited activity. It shall be unlawful for any person to beg, panhandle or solicit any other person in an aggressive manner.

(Ord. No. 394, 1-6-2014)

In response to your constituent request regarding a SAFETEA-LU earmark for the City of South Lyon, and pursuant to our phone conversation yesterday afternoon, we are providing the following information. Under federal budget law, funds for the earmark in question can only be used for the purpose for which they were made available. The language stating the purpose of the South Lyon earmark is in law, therefore the funds can only be redirected if Congress amends the language. Such changes were relatively common. In recent years, however, Congress has not made such changes because it has banned earmarks, and redirecting this funding could be deemed as an earmark. As discussed, when a community is unable to use earmark funding, it remains unspent.
